TL;DR

The headline finding. The engine's validated skill — the 1.5–2.1 cm flat-top LCFS on the 112-shot MSE-gate held-out cohort — is a curated-cohort number. On the uncurated stratified census draw the same four-pass chain degrades 3–10×. This corrects any reading of "the validated engine works corpus-wide": it works where it was validated and degrades elsewhere. §6b measures how and where.

G-E2 — per-class quality: helps the routine classes, inverts the single-nulls

The pattern is physical, not noise:

  • limited & connected-dn — the coupled solve pulls the boundary in correctly (limited 33.4 → 7.0 cm; connected-dn 8.9 → 4.6 cm). connected-dn is the best class and the closest to the bar.
  • single-null (sn-lower, sn-upper) — the solve inverts: sn-lower goes 7.9 → 18.5 cm, worse than the bare seed, with the axis drifting outboard to 26 cm. The symmetric n_p=n_f=3 profile ladder plus a centroid pin cannot carry the up/down asymmetry of a dominant single X-point, so the force-balanced solution the optimiser finds sits outboard, toward the null side — the outboard attractor winning exactly on the topologies with the strongest single-sided pull.

G-E3 — the vessel-eddy known-drive regresses (negative result)

The ramp arm injects the per-shot vessel-eddy trajectory — precomputed from the measured coil + plasma drives on the section-averaged passive eigenbasis — as a known additive drive through the frozen passive Green's columns (amplitudes pinned at high weight). The pre-declared expectation: it must not regress any class and should improve the t < 0.2 s bins.

Interpretation. Pinning a feedforward trajectory at drive strength injects the trajectory's error as real flux. The section-averaged eigenbasis trajectory is not accurate enough to pin; a strong known-drive therefore corrupts the field rather than stabilising the ramp. The ramp deficit is not closed by a feedforward eddy term — it needs the coupled (state-estimated / learned) passive treatment, which is exactly the parallel-in-time dynamics coupling owned downstream. G-E3 is a clean negative that redirects the ramp fix away from feedforward injection.

Basin-pass ablation — the basin solve is not load-bearing on the census

The two-pass chain spends ~44% of solve wall on the basin solve (pass 1) before the coupled profile solve. The ablation drops pass 1 entirely: the profile solve cold-starts from the disc seed under the same centroid pin and diffusion prior. Pre-declared decision rule: no-basin matching the full chain within tolerance on every class ⇒ queue a retire-pass-1 followup; any class-conditional regression ⇒ basin proven load-bearing.

Finding. Under the full pin+prior stack the basin solve is not load-bearing on the census — dropping it is neutral-to-beneficial and ~1.8× cheaper. This does not by itself justify retiring pass 1: the basin's necessity was claimed on the curated MSE-gate cohort, where both arms would clear tolerance and a small regression would be visible. The honest next step is a targeted basin-necessity re-test on that curated held-out set before retiring; if it holds there too, pass 1 retires for a free ~1.8× on the whole label factory.

Mechanism summary

  • Curated ≠ corpus. The 1.5–2.1 cm skill is real on the MSE-gate cohort (beam-on, good-coverage, flat-top-weighted shots). The census stratified draw spans early campaigns, ramp-heavy and low-Ip slices; there the same chain posts 4.6–18.5 cm.
  • The single-null deficit is representational. The n_p=n_f=3 monomial-nonneg profile ladder + a 2-DOF centroid pin has no term for the up/down asymmetry a dominant single X-point imposes; the force-balanced optimum drifts the axis outboard (sn-lower/sn-upper axis 23–26 cm). This is the same interior-null degeneracy the spine notes flag ("the axis is an interior null … needs dynamics or kinetic data"), sharpened by topology class.
  • The ramp needs coupling, not feedforward. A pinned feedforward eddy drive regresses; the eddy state has to be co-estimated.
