Equilibrium Topology Fidelity

Resolving the X-point, the plasma boundary, and the limited↔diverted class beyond EFIT. This plan owns the open frontier of the equilibrium layer: the magnetic axis is solved to referee grade (patch-equilibrium-wm-integration), but the boundary and its topology class are honest negatives. The organizing insight — and the change since this plan was first drafted — is that those negatives are two physically different problems: the boundary is externally well-posed (a representation fix, fast, no new data), while the interior profile is genuinely ill-posed (needs more/higher-fidelity internal data, a prior, and dynamics). It is the concrete home of the stage-2 done-when the driving plan machine-agnostic-latent-world-model (f-malwm-02) records as still open on the X-point/boundary, and it stays inside the North Star (physics-world-model-strategy) by construction: physics as coordinate system + soft priors, EFIT firewalled to referee-only.

§1 — The problem: a discrete decision on a continuous field, very finely judged

Deterministic equilibrium codes (EFIT and kin) spend their hardest effort not on the magnetic axis but on one decision: is the plasma limited or diverted? This is a discrete classification riding on a continuous field. The plasma boundary is the outermost closed flux surface that does not intersect material. There are two ways it can be set:

  • Limited — the boundary is fixed by physical limiter contact: the last closed surface just touches a material limiter or the wall, and the bounding flux is ψ at that contact point.
  • Diverted — the boundary is the separatrix, the flux surface passing through an off-axis ψ-saddle — the X-point, the field null that a divertor coil pulls in from the wall. The bounding flux is ψ at that saddle.

The boundary is whichever of these is the outermost closed surface — essentially max(ψlimiter, ψX). As the divertor coil sweeps the X-point inward, ψX rises and crosses ψlimiter. Near that crossing the two candidate boundary fluxes are nearly degenerate: a tiny error in the reconstructed ψ flips the classification, the plasma-facing footprint, and the whole downstream heat-load picture. This is what "very finely judged" means — the code is resolving a topology decision whose margin is a small fraction of the flux resolution. Getting the axis right (which we do) says nothing about getting this right. What makes it tractable is the physics of §2: this decision is a boundary-shape question, and boundary shape is exactly what external magnetics constrain well.

§2 — The split: the boundary is externally well-posed, the interior profile is not

The load-bearing physics of this plan is a decomposition of the "beyond-axis" problem into two pieces that behave oppositely under external magnetics. Getting this split right is what re-orders the whole plan.

Outside the plasma, ψ is a source-free solution of the homogeneous Grad–Shafranov operator. The GS operator is Δ*ψ = R ∂/∂R(1/R · ∂ψ/∂R) + ∂²ψ/∂Z² = −μ₀ R Jφ. In the current-free vacuum region between the plasma and the sensors, Jφ = 0, so Δ*ψ = 0 — the homogeneous GS equation. This is the toroidal analog of Laplace's equation (it is not literally ∇²ψ = 0; the correct operator is Δ*, but the content is the same "source-free field" condition), and div B = 0 is automatic. A source-free field is a highly constrained object: like a harmonic function, it is pinned throughout the vacuum region by its behaviour on the surfaces bounding that region.

External magnetics therefore fix a finite set of low-order current moments — and those moments set the boundary shape. Flux loops and B-probes sample this source-free field on a surface surrounding the plasma. A source-free field around a current distribution has a convergent multipole/moment expansion; the external sensors determine the lowest moments well — total current Ip, the current-centroid position (R,Z), and the low-order shape moments (elongation, triangularity, and a few higher) — with sensitivity that degrades rapidly for higher moments. Those low-order moments are precisely what set the shape of the outer flux surfaces: whether the outermost closed surface is limiter-set or X-point-set, and where the X-point sits, is a low-order-moment / boundary-shape question. So limited↔diverted is externally well-posed — a well-conditioned readout of a handful of moments the external magnetics measure well (Braams 1991; Alladio & Crisanti 1986 — §research).

What external magnetics cannot resolve is the interior profile — the higher moments. The detailed radial distribution of Jφ (equivalently the p′, FF′, q profiles) inside the plasma is the genuine null-space: many interior profiles produce the same external field. This is the classic external-magnetics ill-posedness, and it is why the interior/axis path saturates and why interior diagnostics are needed for the profile.

This splits our two measured negatives — they are not the same wall:

  • The A4 boundary negative is a representation artifact, not fundamental under-determination. We read the boundary off a ψ generated by ~5,000 free interior patch currents. The null-space slack in that interior current — arrangements that fit the magnetics equally — injects small-scale lumpiness into ψ that survives into the vacuum region as spurious saddles and an under-sized last-closed surface. A4's own verdict is consistent with this: no readout-level lever on the same lumpy ψ helped (they were bit-identical or negligible). The fix is not another readout tweak on that ψ — it is a different, source-free / low-moment-constrained external representation for the boundary, which does not carry the interior slack near the separatrix. Fix the representation and the boundary comes back with the data we already have (thread 1, §5).
  • The balanced-sampling negative is the fundamental null-space — but it lives in the interior profile. The axis median went 4.3 → 15.9 → 17.5 cm as the magnetics fit tightened monotonically (grounding 8.9 → 20.6 → 28.3×): the magnetics-only encoder saturates because the interior current is under-determined, exactly as above. That is the interior-profile problem, and its cure is genuinely more information — interior diagnostics + prior + closure smoothness + dynamics (threads 2–6, §6–§10).

§3 — Where we are (all measured, patch-equilibrium-wm-integration)

The equilibrium layer that this plan extends resolves the axis to referee grade and the boundary/topology not at all — and that split is measured, not asserted.

  • Axis is solved. The training-free per-slice inverse scores axis skill +0.019 > 0 on 160/160 held-out slices at a 2.8 cm median (flat-top skill +0.286 @ 2.09 cm, n=101); the corrected amortised encoder reaches a 4.3 cm axis median on the 5k-shot corpus. The flux-map comparison against firewalled EFIT maps calls the axis referee-grade across regimes (flat-top 2.4 cm median).
  • Boundary/X-point is an honest negative — and §2 says it is a representation artifact. LCFS/boundary reads on the free-current ψ hit spurious saddles (skills ≈ −3 to −11 everywhere); the post-fix A4 baseline records LCFS skill −6.26 with the last-closed surface under-sized by 34.1 cm (all) / 37.2 cm (flat-top), and the flux-map report measures the flat-top LCFS under-sized 31.3 cm median. The X-point set is the first amortised quantity to go marginally positive (+0.058), but nowhere near the bar. Per §2 this is the free ~5k-DOF patch-current representation leaking interior null-space slack into the near-boundary ψ, not fundamental under-determination — thread 1 tests that directly.
  • A4 proved no readout-level lever on that ψ helps. All three candidate boundary-read arms were measured leakage-free (tuned on train, frozen, 160/160 held-out, invert-once grid isolating the readout as the only varying factor): the saddle-distance guard and ψ-smoothing are bit-identical to baseline across the full swept range, and current-smoothness moves every metric < 0.01 in the wrong/negligible direction. This is exactly what §2 predicts: a readout tweak on the same lumpy free-current ψ cannot remove the interior slack — the lever is the representation (a source-free / moment-constrained boundary read, thread 1), or training-time interior information (threads 2–6).
  • The interior null-space is now measured directly (balanced-sampling negative). The magnetics-only encoder axis went 4.3 → 15.9 → 17.5 cm across the 5k → full → full-balanced runs while grounding rose 8.9 → 20.6 → 28.3× — the magnetics fit tightening monotonically while the axis does not follow. Signature+regime-balanced resampling did not recover it, so this is a null-space signature, not a data-composition artifact: the magnetics-only encoder saturates at ~4.3 cm and more magnetics data cannot cross the interior-current-underdetermination wall. This is the empirical mandate for threads 2–6.
  • Standing caveat (programme-wide). The per-slice variational inverse is chaotically sensitive; its absolute axis/LCFS cm figures carry device+run variance of order several cm (identical-config GPU 2.58 cm vs CPU re-run 6.4 cm). Comparative verdicts under one device/run are safe; the PASS/FAIL topology verdicts here stand, but the exact cm are not precise beyond a few cm and must cite their run.

The read is clean: the boundary is a representation problem we can fix with today's magnetics, and the interior profile is where the information is genuinely missing. Thread 1 attacks the former; threads 2–6 add the information for the latter.

§4 — The ambition: beat EFIT the way GenCast beat ECMWF

The north star for this plan is stated plainly: beat EFIT on the X-point, the boundary, and the topology class — first match, then exceed. Matching is nearly done on the axis; exceeding comes from two moves EFIT does not make. The template is GenCast (Nature, 2025): it beat the ECMWF ensemble on ~97% of forecast targets not by being a better deterministic solver but by being a better probabilistic model of the state — generative, multimodal, dynamics-aware, carrying calibrated uncertainty.

What EFIT actually does (verified against ~/Code/imas-efit, §research). It is important to be precise, because the earlier draft over-stated EFIT's blind spot. EFIT is a per-slice deterministic point solve built on grid Green's functions plus a Buneman free-boundary Grad–Shafranov solve, with the LCFS chosen by an explicit limiter-vs-X-point topology classifier (adjudicating ψlimiter vs ψX with hysteresis). Crucially, EFIT already fits interior data when it is available: MSE pitch angle, a kinetic pressure profile (and p′/FF′ constraints), interferometer line-integrated and local density, Faraday-rotation polarimetry, and internal q₀/β are all first-class fitting rows in its response matrix. So our edge is not "using interior data at all." Our edge is the GenCast move EFIT structurally cannot make on top of that:

  • A constrained external boundary representation — read the separatrix/X-point off a source-free field the free-current ψ never enforces, closing the boundary negative with data EFIT and we both already have (thread 1). This is a representation EFIT does not use — it has no toroidal-harmonic or current-moment boundary model — so even the "match" here is a genuine addition.
  • More and higher-fidelity interior data + a generative prior over states — fuse the interior diagnostics more richly than a per-slice least-squares does, and regularise toward a corpus-trained manifold of plausible equilibria instead of a fixed current-profile parametrisation (threads 2 & 4).
  • Dynamics-aware — the transition is a smooth time process; use temporal coherence to disambiguate the instantaneous saddle (thread 5), which a per-slice code cannot.
  • Uncertainty-carrying — return a calibrated limited/diverted probability, not a point estimate, exactly where the decision is most brittle (thread 6).

EFIT stays firewalled. Per the driving plan's locked firewall (code-outputs-only), EFIT's reconstructed boundary/X-point/ψ is a referee that only judges our readouts — never an input, never a training label. Sensor geometry (where diagnostics sit and point) is freely-used apparatus metadata and remains the token positional encoding. Beating the referee means beating it on raw held-out observables and calibrated topology reliability, not on agreement with its own reconstruction.

§6 — Thread 2: interior-diagnostic fusion (the missing information for the profile)

This thread addresses the interior profile — the genuine null-space of §2, the higher moments the balanced-sampling negative measured. The correction to the earlier framing matters: EFIT already ingests MSE, kinetic pressure, interferometer density, and polarimetry as fitting constraints (§4, §research). Our edge is more and higher-fidelity interior data, fused into a learned latent rather than a per-slice least-squares, and carried with geometry-tagged tokens. Carry the interior diagnostics into the encoder as geometry-tagged token streams alongside magnetics, each with its integration geometry (line-of-sight chord endpoints for the integrated diagnostics, beam geometry for MSE), exactly as the driving plan's §5 representation prescribes:

  • MSE (motional Stark effect) — measures the magnetic pitch angle Bpol/Btor along the neutral beam, which directly constrains the internal field and current distribution — the higher moments that magnetics cannot see. MSE is a raw diagnostic (never a solver output), so it is firewall-clean; it is also the same signal Stage-3 uses as its held-out validation key (beam-on slices).
  • Interferometer (line-integrated density) and SXR (line-integrated core emission) — add internal structure constraints along their chords, complementary to MSE and densely available when the beam is off. (Note SXR is a diagnostic EFIT does not ingest — a genuine addition, not just a fusion upgrade.)

Gate 2: with interior diagnostics fused into the encoder, held-out X-point and interior profile skill (and the axis, the measured null-space quantity) rise measurably versus the magnetics-only baseline of §3 (axis saturated ~4.3 cm; X-point set +0.058), per quantity, on the firewalled-EFIT referee. The honest success criterion is direction and magnitude of the rise attributable to the interior channels (ablate them out to confirm), not a fixed absolute cm target — the standing caveat forbids over-reading the absolute number.

§7 — Thread 3: a smoothness prior on the recovered closures (tame the interior null-space)

The interior null-space of §2 has a well-motivated soft regulariser that costs no new data: the recovered affine-GS closures are physically smooth functions of ψ. The per-bin coefficients ak = p′ and bk = FF′/μ₀ trace out p′(ψ) and FF′(ψ), which are smooth in every physical equilibrium, so a second-difference penalty on the {ak}, {bk} sequences is a principled prior that suppresses the high-frequency interior-current excursions the balanced-sampling negative exposed (axis 4.3 → 15.9 → 17.5 cm as grounding rose). This is a direct restriction of the null-space toward physical profiles — the soft-prior analog of the fixed current-profile parametrisation EFIT uses to tame the same ill-posedness, but learned/ablatable rather than imposed.

The mechanism already exists as an ablatable soft rung in patch-current-force-balance P2 (the optional {ak},{bk} ψ-smoothness penalty alongside the F²≥0 integrability rung), so this thread is a measurement, not a build: turn the rung on, sweep its weight leakage-free, and keep it only if load-bearing (the P5 discipline that dropped the passive block on evidence).

Gate 3: with the closure second-difference penalty active, held-out axis and X-point skill improve (or the metre-scale outlier tail shrinks) attributably to the penalty (ablate to confirm), without degrading the magnetics grounding or the F²≥0 rate. If it does not help, record the honest negative — the penalty is a candidate, not an assumption.

§8 — Thread 4: a learned equilibrium prior (pick the physical saddle)

A per-slice inverse with no prior lands on any ψ that fits the magnetics, including one with a spurious off-axis saddle — the mechanism behind the ≈ −3 to −11 boundary skills. The GenCast insight is to replace the bare point solve with a generative prior over the state: the corpus-trained latent learns the manifold of plausible equilibria, so near the ambiguous transition it prefers the physical X-point over a numerical artifact. This is the concrete realisation of the driving plan's hybrid-latent design — the boundary/X-point emerge as readouts of a ψ regularised toward the learned manifold, never supervised. It complements thread 1 (a constrained external representation is a hand-built prior on the vacuum field; the learned prior is a data-built prior on the whole state) and thread 3 (the smoothness penalty is a fixed-form special case of what the learned prior discovers).

Gate 4: on thin-flux-margin slices (the finely-judged band, selected by |ψX − ψlimiter| small on the referee), the prior-regularised topology read picks the correct class (limited vs diverted, and the correct X-point when diverted) more often than the raw ψ-saddle read of the un-regularised inverse. Report the fraction correct vs flux-margin, held-out, referee as ground truth.

§9 — Thread 5: temporal coherence (the transition is smooth in time)

The limited→diverted transition is not an instantaneous event: the X-point sweeps in from the wall as the divertor coil ramps, a smooth trajectory in time. A per-slice reader throws that away and re-decides the topology from scratch every frame, so it is maximally exposed to the instantaneous degeneracy. A dynamics-aware read — the X-point as a coherent track through the stage-3 closed-loop filter, with the flux-diffusion transport prior enforcing smooth ψ evolution — disambiguates the instantaneous saddle by borrowing strength from neighbouring slices. This is the natural bridge from this plan into the driving plan's stage-3 filter (f-malwm-03): the equilibrium topology becomes a filtered quantity, not a per-slice guess.

Gate 5: on transition shots (shots that cross limited→diverted in the held-out set), a temporally-smoothed X-point track beats the per-slice read — lower track error and fewer topology flip-flops through the transition — with the improvement attributable to temporal coherence (ablate the smoothing to confirm).

§10 — Thread 6: calibrated limited/diverted classification (honest at the knife-edge)

At the finely-judged crossing the physically honest output is not a decision but a probability: "60% diverted." EFIT returns a point estimate and hides the knife-edge; a probabilistic model carries the uncertainty forward to whoever consumes the topology (heat-load prediction, control, commissioning). Because the belief is probabilistic (North Star §3), the model can say how sure it is, and that certainty should collapse to a coin-flip exactly in the degenerate band and sharpen away from it — the signature of honest calibration. This dovetails with patch-equilibrium-wm-integration's open topology-calibration followup (current-space heteroscedastic σ), which is the mechanism this thread's probability draws from.

Gate 6: the diverted-probability is reliable against the firewalled EFIT referee — a reliability diagram with flux-margin (|ψX − ψlimiter|) as the difficulty axis shows predicted probability tracking observed frequency, and the model's confidence rising with the flux margin. A calibrated 55% at the knife-edge is a pass; a confident-and-wrong point estimate is the failure this thread exists to replace.

§11 — Research: methods and the EFIT characterization

External-field / boundary reconstruction methods (thread 1). The claim that external magnetics fix the boundary shape via a finite set of low-order moments — while leaving the interior profile under-determined — is standard:

  • Braams, Plasma Phys. Control. Fusion 33 (1991) 715, "The interpretation of tokamak magnetic diagnostics" — the reference statement of what external magnetics can and cannot determine: a finite number of current moments are well-constrained, higher moments (interior profile) are not. This is the §2 well-posed/ill-posed split.
  • Alladio & Crisanti, Nucl. Fusion 26 (1986) 1143, "Analysis of MHD equilibria by toroidal multipolar moments" — the direct precedent for reconstructing the boundary from a toroidal-harmonic / multipole moment expansion of the external field. The toroidal-harmonic option of thread 1.
  • Zakharov & Shafranov, moment method (L. E. Zakharov & V. D. Shafranov, in Reviews of Plasma Physics Vol. 11; and Sov. Phys. Tech. Phys. 18 (1973)) — equilibrium described by low-order current moments; the current-moment option of thread 1.
  • Toroidal (ring) functions — the separable eigenfunctions of Δ* in toroidal coordinates are the half-integer-degree Legendre functions Pn−½, Qn−½ (see e.g. Morse & Feshbach; Abramowitz & Stegun §8 conical/toroidal functions). These are the concrete basis for the toroidal-harmonic expansion.
  • Lackner, Comput. Phys. Commun. 12 (1976) 33 (von Hagenow–Lackner method) — the classical free-boundary GS approach that splits the vacuum (homogeneous) and plasma (source) parts of ψ; background for the source-free vacuum-region argument.
  • Lao et al., Nucl. Fusion 25 (1985) 1611 — the EFIT reference (current-profile parametrisation + magnetic fitting), the referee this plan is firewalled against.

What EFIT actually constrains with (verified in ~/Code/imas-efit). The fitting response matrix is assembled in src/EFIT/matrix.f90 (forward χ² in cnstrn.f90; Green's functions in green.f90 + src/EFUND/). Findings:

  • EFIT does fit interior data — all first-class fitting rows: MSE pitch angle (constraint group 'mse', tangam / a1..a6gam; a full beam kernel in msekern.f90), a kinetic pressure profile (group 'pressure', pressr) plus separate p′ and FF′ constraints, interferometer line-integrated and local density ('integrated_ne' / 'discrete_ne'), Faraday-rotation polarimetry ('faraday'), and internal q₀/β. So "EFIT ignores interior data" is false; the corrected framing (§4, §6) is that our edge is more + higher-fidelity interior data, a learned prior, dynamics, and calibration.
  • EFIT's boundary/external-field method is grid Green's functions + a Buneman free-boundary GS solve, with the LCFS chosen by an explicit limiter-vs-X-point topology classifier (bound.f90, adjudicating ψlimiter vs ψX with hysteresis). It does not use a toroidal-harmonic expansion or a current-moment method (moments appear only as post-processing diagnostics in postprocess.f90), and there is no von Hagenow virtual-casing step. So thread 1's constrained external representation is genuinely a move EFIT does not make — even the "match" is an addition, and it is what lets us close the boundary with today's magnetics.
  • Not present in EFIT: SXR/soft-x-ray (a genuine new channel for thread 2) and any dedicated measured edge-current-density constraint (edge current is shaped indirectly via the flux-function basis + p′∝FF′ proportionality + Tikhonov damping).

§ Decisions

Thread 1 — which constrained external-field representation reads the boundary: a toroidal-harmonic expansion, or an external current-moment solve?

Kickoff lock per the plan's own recommendation: prototype the current-moment solve first for a fast Gate-1 saddle-free/classification/LCFS number. Implemented as a low-order current-moment basis (K~6-10) on the plasma cells, fit to the plasma sensor signature (measured - vacuum) by whitened least squares with a Rogowski-Ip anchor, reusing the existing patch Green's operator (m_sens patch->sensor, g_pg patch->grid flux). This is psi-producing (unlike a pure shape parametrisation) so the vacuum-region consistency check (patch-psi vs constrained read) in the plan holds, and the same topology read + firewalled-EFIT scoring apply unchanged. The heavier toroidal-harmonic (half-integer Legendre) expansion is retained as a documented follow-up if the full-field consistency check or a marginal LCFS recovery warrants it.

Which interior diagnostic does thread 2 fuse first?

MSE carries the most X-point-relevant information but is confined to beam-on slices (the same availability the Stage-3 held-out key exploits). Interferometer+SXR are always-on and de-risk the fusion plumbing on the full corpus before MSE's sparse, high-value slices are added. Recommend deciding at kickoff on the measured beam-on slice count vs the plumbing risk.

How is the interior-prior topology read produced — a soft differentiable field-null term in the training loss, or an eval-time prior-regularised read?

The eval-time read reuses the existing trained latent and is the fast path to a first thread-4 number; the in-loss field-null term couples the topology objective into training and is the stronger long-run mechanism but costs a retrain. Recommend measuring the eval-time read first, then deciding whether the in-loss term is worth the retrain on that evidence. (Note thread 1 handles the boundary saddle by representation; this decision is about the interior-prior read of threads 4/6.)
