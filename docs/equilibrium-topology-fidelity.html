Equilibrium Topology Fidelity

Resolving the X-point and the limited↔diverted transition beyond EFIT. This plan owns the open frontier of the equilibrium layer: the magnetic axis is solved to referee grade (patch-equilibrium-wm-integration), but the plasma boundary and its topology class are honest negatives. It is the concrete home of the stage-2 done-when the driving plan machine-agnostic-latent-world-model (f-malwm-02) records as still open on the X-point/boundary, and it stays inside the North Star (physics-world-model-strategy) by construction: physics as coordinate system + soft priors, EFIT firewalled to referee-only.

§1 — The problem: a discrete decision on a continuous field, very finely judged

Deterministic equilibrium codes (EFIT and kin) spend their hardest effort not on the magnetic axis but on one decision: is the plasma limited or diverted? This is a discrete classification riding on a continuous field. The plasma boundary is the outermost closed flux surface that does not intersect material. There are two ways it can be set:

  • Limited — the boundary is fixed by physical limiter contact: the last closed surface just touches a material limiter or the wall, and the bounding flux is ψ at that contact point.
  • Diverted — the boundary is the separatrix, the flux surface passing through an off-axis ψ-saddle — the X-point, the field null that a divertor coil pulls in from the wall. The bounding flux is ψ at that saddle.

The boundary is whichever of these is the outermost closed surface — essentially max(ψlimiter, ψX). As the divertor coil sweeps the X-point inward, ψX rises and crosses ψlimiter. Near that crossing the two candidate boundary fluxes are nearly degenerate: a tiny error in the reconstructed ψ flips the classification, the plasma-facing footprint, and the whole downstream heat-load picture. This is what "very finely judged" means — the code is resolving a topology decision whose margin is a small fraction of the flux resolution. Getting the axis right (which we do) says nothing about getting this right.

§2 — Where we are (all measured, patch-equilibrium-wm-integration)

The equilibrium layer that this plan extends resolves the axis to referee grade and the boundary/topology not at all — and that split is measured, not asserted.

  • Axis is solved. The training-free per-slice inverse scores axis skill +0.019 > 0 on 160/160 held-out slices at a 2.8 cm median (flat-top skill +0.286 @ 2.09 cm, n=101); the corrected amortised encoder reaches a 4.3 cm axis median on the 5k-shot corpus. The flux-map comparison against firewalled EFIT maps calls the axis referee-grade across regimes (flat-top 2.4 cm median).
  • X-point and boundary are honest negatives. LCFS/boundary reads on the free-current ψ hit spurious saddles (skills ≈ −3 to −11 everywhere); the post-fix A4 baseline records LCFS skill −6.26 with the last-closed surface under-sized by 34.1 cm (all) / 37.2 cm (flat-top), and the flux-map report measures the flat-top LCFS under-sized 31.3 cm median. The X-point set is the first amortised quantity to go marginally positive (+0.058), but nowhere near the bar.
  • A4 proved this is NOT a readout-tuning artifact. All three candidate boundary-read arms were measured leakage-free (tuned on train, frozen, 160/160 held-out, invert-once grid isolating the readout as the only varying factor): the saddle-distance guard and ψ-smoothing are bit-identical to baseline across the full swept range, and current-smoothness moves every metric < 0.01 in the wrong/negligible direction. The deficit is interior-structure-driven — magnetics alone under-constrain the interior current near the X-point (a sensor null-space), so the last closed surface has no data pinning its size. This is the same ill-posedness EFIT faces; EFIT tames it with a current-profile parametrisation we deliberately dropped as over-constraining. The lever is training-time/amortised information, not a post-hoc readout fix.
  • Standing caveat (programme-wide). The per-slice variational inverse is chaotically sensitive; its absolute axis/LCFS cm figures carry device+run variance of order several cm (identical-config GPU 2.58 cm vs CPU re-run 6.4 cm). Comparative verdicts under one device/run are safe; the PASS/FAIL topology verdicts here stand, but the exact cm are not precise beyond a few cm and must cite their run.

The read is clean: the interior current near the X-point is where the information is missing, and it is exactly what magnetics cannot see. Every thread below adds information that the magnetics-only, single-slice, point-estimate read throws away.

§3 — The ambition: beat EFIT the way GenCast beat ECMWF

The north star for this plan is stated plainly: beat EFIT on the X-point, the boundary, and the topology class — first match, then exceed. Matching is nearly done on the axis; exceeding comes from information EFIT structurally ignores. The template is GenCast (Nature, 2025): it beat the ECMWF ensemble on ~97% of forecast targets not by being a better deterministic solver but by being a better probabilistic model of the state — generative, multimodal, dynamics-aware, carrying calibrated uncertainty. EFIT is a per-slice deterministic point solve that uses only magnetics + a fixed current-profile parametrisation and returns a single answer with no honest uncertainty at the finely-judged transition. We propose the same move EFIT cannot make:

  • Multimodal — fuse interior diagnostics (MSE, interferometer, SXR) that see the interior current magnetics cannot (thread 1).
  • A generative prior over states — a corpus-trained latent that knows the manifold of plausible equilibria and picks the physical X-point over a spurious saddle (thread 2).
  • Dynamics-aware — the transition is a smooth time process; use temporal coherence to disambiguate the instantaneous saddle (thread 3).
  • Uncertainty-carrying — return a calibrated limited/diverted probability, not a coin-flip, exactly where EFIT is most brittle (thread 4).

EFIT stays firewalled. Per the driving plan's locked firewall (code-outputs-only), EFIT's reconstructed boundary/X-point/ψ is a referee that only judges our readouts — never an input, never a training label. Sensor geometry (where diagnostics sit and point) is freely-used apparatus metadata and remains the token positional encoding. Beating the referee means beating it on raw held-out observables and calibrated topology reliability, not on agreement with its own reconstruction.

§4 — Thread 1: interior-diagnostic fusion (the missing information)

The A4 verdict points straight here: the boundary deficit is interior-current-null-space, so the fix is to add diagnostics that see the interior current. Carry them into the encoder as geometry-tagged token streams alongside magnetics, each with its integration geometry (line-of-sight chord endpoints for the integrated diagnostics), exactly as the driving plan's §5 representation prescribes:

  • MSE (motional Stark effect) — measures the magnetic pitch angle Bpol/Btor along the neutral beam, which directly constrains the internal field and current distribution. This is the physical information that pins where the X-point sits, because the interior current is what shapes ψ near the edge saddle. MSE is a raw diagnostic (never a solver output), so it is firewall-clean; it is also the same signal Stage-3 uses as its held-out validation key (beam-on slices).
  • Interferometer (line-integrated density) and SXR (line-integrated core emission) — add internal structure constraints along their chords, complementary to MSE and densely available when the beam is off.

Gate T1: with interior diagnostics fused into the encoder, held-out X-point and boundary skill rise measurably versus the magnetics-only baseline of §2 (LCFS skill −6.26, X-point set +0.058), per quantity, on the firewalled-EFIT referee. The honest success criterion is direction and magnitude of the rise attributable to the interior channels (ablate them out to confirm), not a fixed absolute cm target — the standing caveat forbids over-reading the absolute number.

§5 — Thread 2: a learned equilibrium prior (pick the physical saddle)

A per-slice inverse with no prior lands on any ψ that fits the magnetics, including one with a spurious off-axis saddle — that is the mechanism behind the ≈ −3 to −11 boundary skills. The GenCast insight is to replace the bare point solve with a generative prior over the state: the corpus-trained latent learns the manifold of plausible equilibria, so near the ambiguous transition it prefers the physical X-point over a numerical artifact. This is the concrete realisation of the driving plan's hybrid-latent design — the boundary/X-point emerge as readouts of a ψ that is regularised toward the learned manifold, never supervised.

Gate T2: on thin-flux-margin slices (the finely-judged band, selected by |ψX − ψlimiter| small on the referee), the prior-regularised topology read picks the correct class (limited vs diverted, and the correct X-point when diverted) more often than the raw ψ-saddle read of the un-regularised inverse. Report the fraction correct vs flux-margin, held-out, referee as ground truth.

§6 — Thread 3: temporal coherence (the transition is smooth in time)

The limited→diverted transition is not an instantaneous event: the X-point sweeps in from the wall as the divertor coil ramps, a smooth trajectory in time. A per-slice reader throws that away and re-decides the topology from scratch every frame, so it is maximally exposed to the instantaneous degeneracy. A dynamics-aware read — the X-point as a coherent track through the stage-3 closed-loop filter, with the flux-diffusion transport prior enforcing smooth ψ evolution — disambiguates the instantaneous saddle by borrowing strength from neighbouring slices. This is the natural bridge from this plan into the driving plan's stage-3 filter (f-malwm-03): the equilibrium topology becomes a filtered quantity, not a per-slice guess.

Gate T3: on transition shots (shots that cross limited→diverted in the held-out set), a temporally-smoothed X-point track beats the per-slice read — lower track error and fewer topology flip-flops through the transition — with the improvement attributable to temporal coherence (ablate the smoothing to confirm).

§7 — Thread 4: calibrated limited/diverted classification (honest at the knife-edge)

At the finely-judged crossing the physically honest output is not a decision but a probability: "60% diverted." EFIT returns a point estimate and hides the knife-edge; a probabilistic model carries the uncertainty forward to whoever consumes the topology (heat-load prediction, control, commissioning). Because the belief is probabilistic (North Star §3), the model can say how sure it is, and that certainty should collapse to a coin-flip exactly in the degenerate band and sharpen away from it — the signature of honest calibration.

Gate T4: the diverted-probability is reliable against the firewalled EFIT referee — a reliability diagram with flux-margin (|ψX − ψlimiter|) as the difficulty axis shows predicted probability tracking observed frequency, and the model's confidence rising with the flux margin. A calibrated 55% at the knife-edge is a pass; a confident-and-wrong point estimate is the failure this thread exists to replace.

§8 — Sequencing and relationship to the driving plan

The threads are ordered by evidence-directed priority. Thread 1 is first — A4 already proved the deficit is missing interior information, so fusing the diagnostics that carry it is the lever with the clearest measured mandate, and MSE availability (beam-on slices) is already characterised by Stage-3. Threads 2–4 layer on: the learned prior (2) and the calibrated read (4) are readout/loss-side and can be measured on the same trained latent; temporal coherence (3) rides the stage-3 filter as it comes online. All four report against the standing flux-map comparison cadence (patch-flux-map-comparison) and the firewalled referee. This plan is blocked-by patch-equilibrium-wm-integration (the equilibrium layer it extends) and multimodal-tokenisation-substrate (the geometry-tagged token schema the interior diagnostics need), and it blocks machine-agnostic-latent-world-model by finishing the X-point/boundary half of its stage-2 done-when (f-malwm-02 #2), the half the axis work left open.

§ Decisions

Which interior diagnostic does thread 1 fuse first?

MSE carries the most X-point-relevant information but is confined to beam-on slices (the same availability the Stage-3 held-out key exploits). Interferometer+SXR are always-on and de-risk the fusion plumbing on the full corpus before MSE's sparse, high-value slices are added. Recommend deciding at kickoff on the measured beam-on slice count vs the plumbing risk.

How is the topology read produced — a soft differentiable field-null term in the training loss, or an eval-time prior-regularised read?

The eval-time read reuses the existing trained latent and is the fast path to a first T2 number; the in-loss field-null term couples the topology objective into training and is the stronger long-run mechanism but costs a retrain. Recommend measuring T2 with the eval-time read first, then deciding whether the in-loss term is worth the retrain on that evidence.
