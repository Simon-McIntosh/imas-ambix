§1 — Question and answer

The dynamics arm beats the per-frame baseline and persistence (the matched-arm verdict holds), yet the ELM demonstrations remain visually poor — bulk/divertor brightening is reproduced, but the model does not capture crisp ELM structure or timing. The question: is this a capability ceiling or nascent v0 work? And specifically — is it a diagnostic-resolution problem, should we fold in higher-fidelity diagnostic streams, and can we exploit FFT/short-window spectral decompositions of the fast signals?

Answer: it is both, and the dividing line is the camera's own frame rate — not the tokenizer and not the model architecture. "Reproduce ELM structure" was never one problem; it is three, with three different, now-measured answers:

The world-model thesis is not capped. What the GIFs make you wince at — filament detail plus crisp ELM timing — is largely the capped half (the camera never recorded the sub-ms onset, and clips to white at the peak), while the genuinely tractable win is the ELM brightness/morphology response on the frame that contains the integrated burst, which the v0 fails only because it is structurally blind to the fast trigger.

§2 — How this was established

A four-lens read-only investigation (architecture / fast-diagnostic fusion / spectral features) with an adversarial synthesis-and-critique pass, plus two empirical probes run on SLURM against the real corpus:

  • Tokenizer round-trip (imas_ambix/camdyn/tokenizer_fidelity.py) — decode the stored ground-truth tokens of a real ELM window and compare to the raw frames, with no model in the loop. Isolates the tokenizer's contribution to filament loss.
  • Camera-sampling audit (imas_ambix/camdyn/elm_sampling_audit.py) — measure the rbb frame-interval distribution on ELM-bearing shots, test whether the camera resolves or integrates the burst, settle the contested fast-channel rates, and confirm the forecast statistical power at each horizon.

The adversarial critic was decisive: it rejected the initial synthesis (which had carried a resolution-cap premise) and re-scoped the problem onto the camera's sampling rate — the reframing this document records.

§3 — Measured finding: the tokenizer is exonerated

The original report claimed "the token grid does not resolve filamentary structure — a higher-resolution tokenizer would be needed." That is refuted by measurement. On shot 24446 (a dramatic ×41-baseline fast-Dα ELM, native 4 kHz camera), decoding the stored ground-truth tokens reproduces every macroscopic ELM structure: SSIM 0.996, 95% of the fine edge (high-frequency) energy retained, filament-transect contrast 99% preserved. MAST ELMs image as many-pixel coherent bright striations, exactly the structure an 18-bit LFQ patch tokenizer keeps. The decode is only marginally softened.

§4 — Measured finding: the camera undersamples the ELM

The prediction target is rbb camera tokens. If the camera integrates over the sub-ms ELM crash, the onset structure is gone at acquisition and no conditioning, context length, or spectral feature can recover it. The audit settled the contested rates (our own artifacts had spanned three orders of magnitude):

  • Camera frame interval is bimodal and shot-dependent — median 0.20 ms, range 0.01–16.67 ms. The sub-0.1 ms (≥10 kHz) shots are mostly tiny high-speed ROI readouts (e.g. 80×128 px), not full frames; full-frame shots run ~0.2–0.33 ms. 24% of ELM-bearing shots integrate outright (Δt ≥ 1 ms); even the "fast" full-frame mode is comparable to, not below, the 0.1–1 ms ELM width.
  • The camera captures the burst in 1–2 frames at best — it does not trace a multi-frame rise-and-fall. The genuine fast carrier steps ~16–25× finer than the camera.
  • Fast-channel identities settled: ada/dalpha_raw_full is a slow (696×1024) radial profile on a ~1.43 kHz axis — not a fast trace (the prior "fast ada Dα" label was wrong); the genuine sub-ms ELM carrier is xim/da_hm10_t (~67–250 kHz); xma/ccbv fast Mirnov is effectively ~5 kHz (confirming the earlier interior-information finding, refuting a 100 kHz reading).
  • Forecast power: valid ELM-window forecast samples are n=59 at 10 ms (powered, beats persistence, CI>0), n=19 at 50 ms (CI crosses 0), n=1 at 200 ms (powerless). Any lever aimed at 50/200 ms is underpowered by construction.

§5 — Verdict: the capped half and the fixable half

Capped (do not chase): sub-ms ELM onset, timing, and filament-resolved dynamics. These live below the camera's frame interval and are lost to integration (and, at the peak, to sensor saturation) — a property of the target, not of the tokenizer (§3), the model architecture (the only true architectural cap, the learned temporal_pos table, is narrow and RoPE-removable), or training. No amount of fast-diagnostic conditioning or spectral feature can predict information the camera never recorded.

Fixable (build this): the ELM brightness/morphology response on the frame that contains the integrated burst — "an ELM fired here; the divertor/edge brightens by this much, with this shape." The v0 fails this only because conditioning is slow-actuators-only and the model never sees the fast ELM trigger (xim Dα). This is legitimate to add — the fast diagnostics are not in the leakage ban (which protects only EFIT/esm/xdc; the camera tokens are the target here, not Dα). It is statistically testable only at the ~10 ms horizon (n=59).

So: the poor ELM demos are predominantly a target-sampling ceiling on the timing/onset half, with a real but bounded fixable win on the morphology-response half. Nascent — not a model-class wall.

§6 — Near-term plan and implementation

Ordered by leverage-per-effort, each gated by a cheap decisive test. The metric throughout is ELM-frame morphology/brightness fidelity at the 10 ms horizon (the only powered one) — not multi-ms forward forecast at 50/200 ms (dead and underpowered). Every conditioning test carries a shuffled-Dα control and a slow-only control to defeat two confounds the critic surfaced: (a) fast-Dα burst energy autocorrelates with the gas/density the model already sees, and (b) a causal interval-max of Dα over [t₋₁, tᵢ] is nearly a copy of the camera brightening at frame i — a within-interval near-label of the target, not a forecast.

Deprioritized / not pursued

  • Multi-rate / longer-context windows for 50/200 ms forecast — the dynamics edge over persistence has already converged by ~10 ms and those horizons are underpowered (n=19 / n=1). Building reach into a dead, fatally-undersampled regime buys nothing. Revisit only if a power audit finds surviving signal.
  • RoPE / Δt-keyed relative position — the only real architectural cap (temporal_pos[:f] cannot extrapolate), parameter-free to fix, but it costs a both-arm from-scratch retrain. Do it only if a cheap context probe shows more context helps ELMs — which the sampling cap makes unlikely.
  • Higher-resolution / Cosmos re-tokenizer for filaments — unnecessary: the current tokenizer already preserves the structure (§3). The filament-detail limit is the camera, not the token grid.

§7 — What would change the verdict

  • A usable population of full-frame ELM shots at tens-of-µs cadence (the audit found the fast band is dominated by tiny ROIs) would partially reopen the onset-timing half.
  • A different camera/diagnostic with genuine sub-ms full-frame imaging (the target sets the ceiling, so a better target — not a better model — is the only escape for onset fidelity).
  • A measured positive at step 2 with controls intact confirms the fixable half is real; a null with a genuinely fast, well-aligned, present Dα channel would push even the morphology-response half toward "capped."
