§1 — Proposition: what GS states is not how GS is solved

The Grad–Shafranov equation is a statement of force balance between fluid and magnetic pressure. Every restrictive assumption in our current stage-2 chain enters not from that statement but from the way it is solved: parametrise two flux functions on normalised flux, choose a closed plasma boundary, distribute current as jφ(ψN; β0, α) inside the separatrix only, and Picard-iterate the nonlinear fixed point with topology extraction (axis, X-points, core mask) inside the loop. The Nova approach to coil and plasma modelling never solves the GS operator on a grid at all: the vessel interior is filled with finite-element patches carrying piecewise-constant currents; flux and field everywhere are Green's-function superpositions assembled once into interaction matrices and decomposed into coil and plasma blocks. Fields built this way are exactly solenoidal — div B = 0 holds by construction — and with the analytic finite-area kernel (imas_ambix/gs/cylinder.py, ported from nova.biot) a rectangular conductor is a single patch: smooth self-field, no sub-discretisation, no self-inductance regularisation.

The sharp observation this plan is built on: in the current-patch basis, Ampère's law and div B = 0 are identities — the only physics content remaining in GS is the flux-function structure of the current,

jφ(R, Z) = a(ψ)·R + b(ψ)/R   (a = p′, b = FF′/μ0),

i.e. on each connected ψ level-set, jφ is linear in the pair {R, 1/R} — or, in the cleanest fitting form (multiply through by R): R·jφ is affine in x = R², with slope p′ and intercept FF′/μ0, so force balance means the scatter of (R², R·jφ) along any single flux surface falls on a straight line. A grid-collocation Δ*ψ-residual (the PINN formulation) conflates solving the elliptic problem with force balance; in the patch basis the elliptic problem is free and exact, and force balance becomes a standalone differentiable residual that never needs p′/FF′ basis functions, never needs ψN normalisation, and never needs a hard topology extraction — level-set grouping enters softly (the per-connected-component caveat and its mitigation are in §4). Assumptions become soft, ablatable regularisers — precisely the North-Star language (“physics enters as the coordinate system and soft regularisers, never as a hard law”) that the current hard free-boundary solve inside the decoder was flagged as being in tension with. Coefficients and closures are recovered from data; known physics is supplied without over-constraining the solution.

§2 — Scoping evidence (landed 2026-07-03)

Four scoping studies ran before this plan was written (scripts/patch_basis_studies.py: assemble, validate, oracle, discriminate, identify; commits 7607fb1…8df985f). Artifacts under imas_ambix/latent/artifacts/patch_scoping/.

2.1 The substrate exists and validates to the numerical floor

The patch→grid interaction matrix (5084 in-limiter patches × 6305 grid points, finite-area kernel near the patch, point filament far) assembles in 8.5 s and is cached per campaign signature. Validation: far-field agreement of the finite-area kernel with the point filament to 2.7×10−4; finite-difference Δ* of the exact Green's field reproduces the source at the FD truncation floor (0.4% RMS); and the Dirichlet FD solve agrees with the Green's superposition to 0.02% RMS on real fitted currents and 0.018% on a smooth manufactured current. One forward evaluation (full ψ grid + all sensors) costs 138 ms single-threaded on CPU against 14.4 s for a bootstrapped Picard solve (×104 before any GPU batching; the matrices are fixed buffers, so batched GPU throughput scales as matmul).

2.2 The scoping found and fixed a real defect the old chain could not see

The first FD-vs-Green's cross-check disagreed by 23% RMS on a smooth manufactured current. Kernel-level probes (flux-consistency integral ∫2πrBzdr against the returned ψ; the flux integral of Δ*ψ over a patch, which measures exactly 2π·I) pinned the cause: the free-boundary solver paired a per-radian GS source (−μ0Rjφ) with total-flux Green's functions (Φ = 2πRAφ) — the plasma's own flux well was 2π ≈ 6.3× too shallow against the coil field in every Picard iteration (fixed in 5148a4d with a regression test; 58/58 latent tests green). This defect is upstream of every gate-2 number recorded to date, and it plausibly explains why the Gaussian seed had no interior O-point under the exact coil field — the fragility the continuation bootstrap was built to work around. The methodological point for this plan: a representation in which Ampère's law is an identity cannot hide a source-scale error; a Picard chain that rescales to Ip every iteration can, and did.

2.3 Under the corrected physics, the restrictive model breaks honestly — and measurably

Re-measuring the training-free gate-2 protocol with the corrected solver (job 1224801): the scored fraction collapses from 122/160 to 12/160 while the scored (mostly early-time, limited) slices improve from 8.3 to 5.9 cm median axis error. Flat-top / diverted slices fail the cost-3 honesty threshold at cost 8–15, and an extended 35-candidate profile grid does not rescue them — the optimum pins to the family corner (β0=0.05, α=0.5) with the axis in an unphysical outboard attractor at R≈1.7 m. The variational inverse below shows free patch currents fit the same sensors to well under 1σ without passive conductors, so the binding constraint is the two-DOF profile family itself, not missing conductors. The prior 122/160 @ 8.3 cm baseline was partly an artifact of the 2π-weak (hence diffuse, coil-shaped, accidentally permissive) plasma response. This is the over-constraint thesis of this plan, with receipts.

2.4 The structure residual discriminates exactly what the data cannot see

The profile-free force-balance residual (soft ψ-binning of patches weighted by jφ2, closed-form per-bin weighted least squares of jφ against {R, 1/R}, reported as the unexplained fraction of current-weighted variance) was scored on the corrected-solver oracle equilibria and perturbation classes:

The scoping regression fit jφ against {R, 1/R} directly; external review pointed out the cleaner equivalent — R·jφ affine in R² (slope p′, intercept FF′/μ0), which is the best-conditioned design (no 1/R blow-up at the centre column) and was re-verified to discriminate identically on the same fixtures (oracle floor 0.0036; null-space 0.0205 = 5.7× floor; permuted 0.0742 = 21× floor; artifact discriminate-affine-r2.json). P2 adopts the affine form.

The complementarity is the design insight: the data term pins what sensors see (position, low-order moments); the physics term penalises the null space (structureless and invisible perturbations). Neither over-constrains profile shape, boundary topology, or the existence of currents outside a separatrix — halo or vessel-adjacent currents remain representable, and a persistent residual stays visible as a discovery signal (where force balance breaks: rotation, dynamics, un-modelled conductors) rather than being fitted away.

2.5 Training-free patch inverse: scores every slice, beats the baseline where Picard masks

A per-slice variational inverse (Adam over 5084 patch currents; whitened sensor misfit + Rogowski Ip anchor + λ·structure residual; no Picard, no topology in the loop; conductor interiors excluded as factual geometry) on 12 held-out slices spanning early ramp to flat-top:

  • Scores 12/12 slices where the corrected Picard chain scores 4/12 — there is no convergence to fail;
  • On the 8 flat-top slices the Picard chain masks, the physics weight helps monotonically: at λ=10 the axis lands at 1.3–5.8 cm (median ≈3 cm), beating the 4.7 cm train-mean median in the one regime where the clustered train-mean has been unbeatable;
  • Across all 12 slices: median 4.5 cm vs 8.7 cm train-mean and 5.8 cm Picard-where-it-fits;
  • On early limited slices the data term alone is strong (λ=0 best: e.g. 4.4 cm vs Picard's 5.1 cm) and large λ over-smooths — the optimal physics weight is regime-dependent, which motivates the adaptive-λ decision below;
  • Misfits stay at 0.01–0.13 (≪1σ) everywhere — the PF+patch observation model reproduces the measured magnetics without passive conductors on these slices.

§3 — Deep research: where this sits in the landscape

Forward-solve fragility is not local to our stack. Pentland, Amorisco, Farrell & Ham 2025 demonstrate multiple solutions to the static forward free-boundary GS problem in MAST-U geometry (deflated continuation on FreeGSNKE), including deeply and shallowly confined states — independent evidence that Picard/Newton chains on this machine's geometry carry attractor ambiguity, consistent with our corner/outboard attractors and the continuation bootstrap that §2.2 shows was compensating for a physics defect. (IOP version)

Residual-as-loss has precedent, but on the wrong representation. PINN GS reconstruction (multi-stage PINNs, 2025; data-free PINN equilibria; EAST/JET applications) trains a network representing ψ(R,Z) against the collocation residual Δ*ψ + μ0Rjφ. On a free ψ field that residual conflates the elliptic solve with force balance and enforces Maxwell only softly. The patch basis inverts the split: Maxwell exact, force balance soft — no other published equilibrium-ML work we found trains the flux-function structure residual on a current basis.

Current/filament bases are the classical representation for reconstruction. EFIT's response functions, filamentary LCFS reconstructors, and current-tomography approaches (Blum et al., JET real-time current-density identification with Tikhonov + B-spline nonlinearity bases; D-shaped tokamak identification methods) all exploit linear filament bases and confront the same Hadamard ill-posedness we measured in §2.5(a); their regularisers are generic smoothness. The closest kin in spirit is Bayesian current tomography (Svensson & Werner's MINERVA-class current-beam models at JET), which also treats the current distribution as the fundamental unknown with fields as exact linear maps. The novelty claim here is narrower and sharper: imposing the flux-function structure nonparametrically as a differentiable training residual — penalising exactly the unphysical part of the null space and nothing else — rather than through p′/FF′ basis functions or generic Tikhonov smoothness.

Differentiable-equilibrium ecosystems are converging on the same architecture. FreeGSNKE (static+evolutive, passive structures as circuits, JAX-ification planned), TORAX (JAX transport), and Nova's own nova/jax layer (differentiable fieldnull/topology + basis.py p′/FF′) all assemble expensive Green's matrices once and differentiate through cheap forward evaluations. Nova is the direct blueprint: its biot matrices ship the same coil/plasma block decomposition (target_plasma, plasma_source, plasma_plasma) and its Operate.update_turns pattern changes the plasma current distribution without recomputing any Green's function. Import is impossible (numpy<2 pin); porting kernels is the established path (gs/cylinder.py is already golden-pinned to Nova at 10−6).

Rotation and generalised GS. With toroidal flow the equilibrium condition generalises (pressure no longer a pure flux function; NIMEQ/HELENA-class solvers and EFIT-with-flow formulations add known R-dependent terms). In the structure-residual family this is an additive basis column per flux bin (e.g. a rigid-rotation R³-class term), not a new solver — the residual family is extensible exactly where the classic chain is rigid. Amortised / real-time neural equilibrium surrogates (EFITNN, EAST deep-learning reconstruction) motivate P4's single-forward-pass readout, but all train on solver output; ours trains on raw magnetics with physics residuals — no EFIT in the loop, per the firewall discipline.

§4 — Architecture and design choices

Forward model (fixed per campaign, torch buffers): ψgrid = Gpg·I + Ψcoil·Ipf;  d = Msens·I + Mpf·Ipf;  ψpatch = Gcc·I + Ψcoil,cells·Ipf. All three matrices already exist or are one assembly call away (g_plasma/g_pf in gs/operator.py; patch→grid from the scoping script; the finite-area diagonal makes Gcc well-defined with no self-inductance fudge). Geometry enters only through the matrices — machine-agnostic by construction (North-Star requirement #1), and the coil block/plasma block decomposition is the Nova decomposition verbatim.

Force-balance structure residual (differentiable, profile-free): soft-bin patches by their total ψ (bin centres/bandwidth detached; the kernel weights themselves differentiate through ψ, so gradients flow through the level-set geometry as well as through jφ), weight by jφ2 so vacuum cells never pollute a bin, per-bin closed-form 2×2 weighted LSQ in the affine form R·jφ ≈ a·R² + b (slope a = p′, intercept b = FF′/μ0; the (a,b) are eliminated analytically — no inner optimisation, no profile basis), residual = unexplained weighted fraction ∈ [0,1]. Zero for any GS-compliant state regardless of profile shape or topology; extensible with rotation columns. Explicit contour tracing is deliberately avoided — it is non-differentiable and would sever exactly the joint jφ/level-set sensitivity this formulation exists to keep.

Connectivity caveat (the honest form of “no topology”): the flux-function property holds per connected component of a level set. In a diverted plasma one ψ value can label a core surface, a private-flux arc, and an SOL contour; naïve ψ-value grouping would wrongly demand the same (a,b) on all three. The jφ2 weighting already makes the constraint vacuous where patches carry no current; where disconnected components both carry current, P2 adds a connectivity-aware soft weight — an (R,Z)-locality kernel factor, or no-grad connected- component labels refreshed periodically outside the gradient path. Topology enters softly, never as a hard separatrix extraction in the loop. Genuine halo/SOL current that breaks force balance still reports as residual — that is signal, not error.

Integrability of F (soft rung): a recovered b(ψ) only corresponds to a real toroidal field if F²(ψ) = F²vac + 2μ0∫b dψ′ stays non-negative (Fvac = R·B t,vac from the known TF current). The per-bin intercepts {bk} make this a cheap penalty, relu(−F²), on the cumulative sum — one of the ablatable soft regularisers, alongside optional ψ-smoothness (second-difference) penalties on the {ak}, {bk} sequences.

Closure recovery for free: the fitted per-bin coefficients are the profiles — p(ψ) = ∫a dψ, F(ψ) = √(F²vac + 2μ0∫b dψ) — recovered from data with per-bin regression uncertainties, no basis functions anywhere. This is the plan's answer to the project goal of recovering coefficients and closures while supplying known physics.

What stays hard: the Rogowski Ip anchor (a raw measurement — also kills the trivial zero-current minimum), absolute calibration, and factual geometry (conductor interiors excluded from plasma patches). Everything else — profile family, boundary closure, current-free SOL, staticity, axisymmetric passive currents — is soft or absent.

Topology demoted to evaluation. Axis / X-point / LCFS reads become deterministic reads of the assembled ψ at eval time (existing latent/topology.py, conductor-clear masks unchanged). Nothing in training depends on them, which removes the defect class §2.2 documents (seed sensitivity, corner attractors, continuation bootstrap). Nova's differentiable jax/fieldnull is the blueprint if a later phase wants soft topology reads inside a loss.

§6 — Risks and honest limits

  • Residual permissiveness: smooth flux-coherent blobs score near the floor (§2.4) — the structure residual alone cannot certify a physical pressure profile, and it does not by itself enforce F²≥0 for the recovered toroidal-field function. Mitigation: it never runs alone (data + Ip + transport); the F² integrability penalty and positivity priors are documented soft rungs (§4/P2), enabled on evidence.
  • Disconnected level sets: the flux-function property holds per connected component; a diverted plasma with genuine current in the private-flux region or SOL is the real edge case where naïve ψ-binning cross-contaminates. Mitigation is P2's connectivity-aware soft weighting with its adversarial fixture; the claim throughout this plan is “no hard topology extraction in the loop”, not “no notion of level-set connectivity anywhere”.
  • Regime-dependent λ: fixed weights help flat-top and hurt early limited slices. Mitigation: P2 policy arms; P4 lets the encoder learn the weighting implicitly. Decision left open below until P2/P3 evidence.
  • Small scoping sample: n=12 slices, 4 shots. P3 is the powered re-measurement; no claim graduates until it lands.
  • Null space remains vast: ~20–60 constrained modes out of 5084 DOF. The inverse's interior detail beyond what data + structure pin is prior-driven; report readouts (axis, boundary, moments), never raw interior current maps, as deliverables.
  • Stale downstream artifacts: fit_targets/, the amortised profile head, and recorded gate-2 numbers all predate the 2π fix — P3/P4 regenerate them; the gs-grounded plan carries the correction note.

§ Decisions

Plasma representation for the stage-2 equilibrium readout?

User-proposed Nova representation; scoping validated the substrate to the numerical floor and the training-free inverse beat both references (§2). div B = 0 and Ampère exact by construction; geometry enters only through matrices.

How does force balance enter training?

In the patch basis the Δ* residual is an identity (vacuous) and parametrised profiles are the over-constraint §2.3 measured. The structure residual discriminates null-space and structureless perturbations (5.5×/17× floor) without profile, normalisation, or topology assumptions, and extends to rotation.

Force-balance weight policy for the inverse and the amortised loss — fixed ladder, discrepancy-principle adaptive, warm-start annealing, or learned (P4)?

P4 encoder head: direct 5084-patch currents, or low-rank learned basis plus free residual currents?
