§1 — Current state (objective)

The §2 source-free toroidal-harmonic (TH) boundary read extracts the LCFS by pushing a flux contour outward from a ray-cast origin (contourpy) and keeping the outermost closed axis-enclosing ring inside the limiter; the boundary flux value ψbnd is discovered by that push-out, not supplied. There is no true magnetic-axis flux in this read and none is needed — the interior is owned by the §3 solve.

The read was producing an inboard-midplane notch on some slices. This document records the full root-cause investigation so the state is objective rather than asserted. Summary of where each regime now stands (per-slice, current code):

§2 — How the read works (mechanism)

The TH field is a superposition of source-free ring functions P1n−½(cosh η) about a focal ring (the "pole"). The pole is the plasma-current source, so it sits inside the plasma; the expansion is physical only in the source-free vacuum annulus outside the plasma. Toward the pole the ring functions diverge, so the near-pole interior disk is masked to a confined plateau (mask_invalid_interior) before the contour push-out — that plateau never produces a contour in the outward sweep, so an interior null can never be selected.

The boundary read therefore has two independent reference points: the ray-cast origin (where the outward sweep starts) and the pole (the focal-ring/current source, which the mask follows). The distinction is the whole story of the notch (§4).

§3 — Falsification ladder

Five candidate causes were tested. Four were falsified with evidence; the fifth is the confirmed cause.

  • Solenoid / coil field (point-filament singularity): falsified. Collapsing the 656-filament solenoid to one thick cylinder changes the near-HFS coil ψ by 0.15% and both forms are equally smooth; the hybrid near/far switch is byte-identical on the HFS. The coil field is clean.
  • Grid / contour resolution: falsified. The notch is byte-identical at 65×97 and a 4× refined 260×388 grid (figure §5) — it is a field feature, not a contour-tracing artifact.
  • Near-pole mask size: falsified. 12143's boundary is identical for mask fraction 0.5 → 0.15 (Rin 0.195, Rout 1.061 throughout) — the LCFS is set by the field outside the mask, so the mask is not wrapping it.
  • Fast ring-function tabulation: falsified. The fast ring_P1 (elliptic-integral seeds + degree recurrence + order-raise) matches mpmath's legenp to ~1e-16 across the whole cosh η range, including near the pole where the order-raise is a difference of large numbers. No cancellation blow-up; a higher-accuracy third-party library would gain nothing.
  • Pole placement / TH order: confirmed. Two distinct effects — a fixable regression (§4) and a fidelity ceiling (§5).

§4 — Confirmed cause + fix (well-conditioned slices)

The notch on the well-conditioned majority was a regression from a two-pass origin re-siting I added earlier this session. The near-pole mask follows the pole, and the pole was placed relative to the ray-cast origin. The two-pass step re-sited that origin to the LCFS geometric centroid, which for a D-shape sits inboard of the Shafranov-shifted current centroid — dragging the pole and its mask onto the limited HFS edge, where the contour was pinched into the notch.

Fix landed (closure_gate_eval.py): the focal pole is anchored to the current (moment) centroid for both passes; the ray-cast origin may still re-site to the LCFS centroid for small-plasma robustness; the mask stays a small central disk. A tiny plasma whose LCFS the size-scaled mask would swallow falls back to the un-re-sited pass-1 read rather than dropping the slice.

§5 — Residual: 12143 is poorly-conditioned

The 12143 ramp-up notch survives the §4 fix. It is not the grid and not the mask (both falsified in §3). It is a property of the fitted field: the source-free harmonic simply cannot fit this slice's sensors — misfit stays 1.9–4.7 at every order (vs 0.23 for the well-conditioned 18502).

This is the source-free read's fidelity ceiling on ramp-up slices where the annulus assumption is weak (the plasma is limited against the HFS with little vacuum annulus on the contact side, and eddy/edge structure the source-free field cannot represent). It is honest to treat these as low-fidelity rather than to force a clean-but-wrong boundary.

§6 — Multi-pole (tested)

A multi-pole TH (a vertically-stacked set of focal rings — superposition of source-free solutions) was prototyped. It improves the annulus fit markedly (misfit halved on every slice, including 12143) — the superposition premise is sound. But it makes the interior contour worse: each interior focal ring is a singularity the ψN=1 contour wraps around, giving a multi-lobe clover instead of a plasma.

§7 — Pole fraction is regime-dependent (why there is no single tune)

The pole is placed at pole_r = centroid_r · (1 − frac). A single global frac cannot serve all regimes.

§8 — Pole at the current centre (tested)

The off-centre pole is the visible cause of the asymmetry — the flux contours are centred on the pole (R≈0.31), not on the current centre (R≈0.53), so the boundary is lopsided (inboard hook + outboard bean). The natural hypothesis is: place the focal ring coincident with the current centre and, with enough order, recover the boundary. We tested it directly on 12143.

Verdict: centring the pole (your asymmetry diagnosis) is correct and improves the fit, but it does not recover 12143 — the slice is genuinely ill-posed for a single-focal-ring source-free read at any order or regularization.

§9 — Basis choice: toroidal vs spherical vs combined

MAST is a low-aspect-ratio spherical tokamak (R₀/a ≈ 1.3): the plasma is a fat, nearly-round blob that barely has the central "hole" a single toroidal focal-ring expansion assumes. So the question is whether toroidal harmonics are the right basis here.

  • Spherical harmonics are the wrong operator. They solve Laplace's equation ∇²ψ=0. The source-free field must solve the Grad–Shafranov operator Δ*ψ = ψRR − (1/R)ψR + ψZZ = 0 — note the minus (1/R)ψR, opposite in sign to the Laplacian's +(1/R)ψR. Plain spherical harmonics are not Δ*-harmonic and would violate the source-free premise. The toroidal harmonics are correct precisely because their √(cosh η−cos θ) prefactor × half-integer Legendre functions does solve Δ*=0.
  • The valid concern stands. A single focal ring is a poor expansion centre for a fat ST; it conditions badly there (this whole document).
  • Correct alternatives (all Δ*-harmonic):
    1. The polynomial / moment homogeneous-GS basis expanded about the plasma centre — this project already ships it as the moment read, which beat TH on the X-point set (while losing on LCFS). It centres naturally on the blob rather than a focal ring.
    2. Multi-pole TH (§6) — better annulus fit, but interior contour wraps each pole.
    3. A combined basis (tested — see §10). Because both TH and the polynomial/moment solutions are Δ*-harmonic, their sum is too — stack both in one design matrix and fit jointly. The low-order polynomial part carries the smooth centred blob (where the focal ring struggles); the TH ring functions carry the annulus/edge (where they are best-in-class). It is mathematically valid; the only risk is conditioning (the bases overlap at low order → near-redundant matrix), handled by ridge / SVD truncation.

Root cause: adding TH's source-free degrees of freedom on top of the moment read re-introduces exactly the boundary null-space slack the low-order moment basis was built to suppress. Where TH fits strongly (18502) it bends the good moment boundary into the heart (37 cm); where TH is weak (12143) the combined defaults back to moment. The combination is inconsistent and never beats moment-only. I mistook the low misfit for success — the lesson, again: sensor misfit is not a boundary-quality proxy; score against EFIT.

The real finding is complementarity. TH is best when well-conditioned (4.3 cm on 18502); the moment read is best when poorly-conditioned (15.3 cm on 12143 vs TH's 28.5 cm) and is never catastrophic (5.8 / 15.3 cm across both). Neither single read wins both regimes, and combining them hurts. The fix is a conditioning-gated selection between the two EXISTING reads — use TH where its sensor misfit is low (best edge fidelity), fall back to the moment read where TH is poorly-conditioned (robust) — NOT a combined basis.

§11 — Moment vs TH across the held-out cohort

Both reads are homogeneous-GS in the vacuum, but by different constructions. The moment read is a particular solution: a confined low-order current whose exact finite-area Biot–Savart field is source-free (Δ*ψ = −μ₀R·Jφ, and Jφ=0 outside the plasma cells ⇒ Δ*ψ=0 in the vacuum). The TH read is a homogeneous solution — a truncated multipole ψ = Σ_n[a_n cos nθ + b_n sin nθ]·√(cosh η−cos θ)·P¹_{n−½}(cosh η) about a focal ring. The moment read's external signature is exactly the low-order moments (Ip, centroid, elongation, triangularity) the magnetics constrain, so it has no boundary null-space slack; TH's higher terms are unconstrained shape DOF.

The crossover tracks the TH sensor misfit almost exactly: TH misfit ≲ 0.6 ⇒ TH competitive/better; TH misfit ≳ 2 (the 12xxx ramp-ups) ⇒ TH catastrophic, moment far better. That misfit is the ready-made gate signal for the selection (§13).

§12 — Double-step (confine-and-refit) moment: bug fixed; valid but not a robust win

Idea (correct): pass 1 fills the vessel → LCFS₁; mask Jφ=0 outside LCFS₁ (a cheap COLUMN mask of the precomputed interaction matrix — no Green's recompute); pass 2 refits on the confined cohort. The first implementation had a bug: at the stock ridge (1e-8) the confined moment basis (near-collinear monomials on a smaller region) is ill-conditioned and the refit blows up into a wildly oscillating current (±10 kA, 46% negative) that destroys the plasma O-point. The push-out, given that non-physical ψ and a current-centroid origin sitting in a ψ-minimum, traced a spurious floating oval — a boundary connected to neither an X-point nor the limiter. That was a real defect, not a physics result.

Fix: escalate the ridge until the refit current is single-signed (a physical peaked plasma — the smallest regularisation that keeps it so), and push from the plasma O-point (ψ-max inside the confined region), not the current centroid. With that the pass-2 boundaries are valid closed D-shapes that reach the limiter.

Result (mixed, not a robust win): pass 2 helps some slices (18505 ramp-up 20→13 cm, 12143 flat-top 12→10 cm) and hurts others (12189/12190 ramp-up 11→26/23 cm; 18502 flat-top 6→10 cm). The sweet-spot regularisation is slice-dependent, and the underlying null-space remains: the plasma boundary is a near-edge (X-point) feature the far external magnetics do not uniquely determine, so confining the current trades one plausible boundary for another without new information. The current-disc variant confirms the same — a large disc (≈ vessel-filling) recovers single-pass (6.0 cm on 18502) while smaller discs under-size, and every disc is worse than single-pass on the ramp-up. Net: the single-pass "fill the vessel" moment read is the more robust choice; the ~15 cm ramp-up residual is an information floor of any magnetics-only boundary read, owned by the §3 force-balance solve.

§13 — Open decisions

The well-conditioned majority is fixed. The remaining choice is how the closure pipeline treats poorly-conditioned slices like 12143 ramp-up. Options, for an objective call:

  1. Conditioning-gated selection between the two existing reads (recommended, §10). Use the TH read where its sensor misfit is low (well-conditioned → best edge fidelity, 4.3 cm on 18502); fall back to the moment read where TH is poorly-conditioned (12143 TH misfit 2.88 → moment 15.3 cm vs TH 28.5 cm). Both reads already exist; the moment read is never catastrophic. Needs the anchor to accept the moment read's boundary/target for the fallback slices, then a re-gate. This is the evidence-backed route.
  2. Moment read everywhere. Simplest: the moment read is decent in both regimes (5.8 / 15.3 cm) and never catastrophic. Costs the ~1.5 cm TH edge on well-conditioned slices (4.3 vs 5.8) but removes all TH pathologies. A strong low-risk baseline; settle against the shipped scored numbers in the re-gate.
  3. Weight by misfit (already partly in place). Keep TH, let the anchor's √misfit down-weighting reduce poorly-conditioned slices' influence, accept the low fidelity, and let the §3 solve own them. Lowest-effort but leaves the wrong boundary in the prior.

REFUTED: the combined homogeneous-GS basis (§10) — a 37 cm regression on 18502; adding TH DOF to the moment read re-introduces boundary null-space slack. The multi-pole read (§6) is likewise annulus-only (worse interior contour). Neither is a boundary read.

The full §2+§3 re-gate remains held until this decision is made. The recommended route is option 1 (conditioning-gated selection) — it is the only approach that gets both the well-conditioned edge fidelity (TH) and the poorly-conditioned robustness (moment) — with option 2 (moment everywhere) as the low-risk fallback. Both settle against the shipped scored LCFS numbers in the re-gate. But see §14 — the moment read itself has a conditioning defect whose fix (a direct-moment read) may supersede this choice.

§14 — Moment-read conditioning defect + the direct-moment fix

Scrutiny of the moment read exposed an implementation-level defect: it is ill-conditioned, and its behaviour across order is erratic rather than monotone. A single filament at the centroid should be a sane floor, yet the order sweep jumps around it (RMS vs EFIT [cm]): 18502: filament 8.5 | o1 nan | o2 39.3 | o3 5.8; 18505 ramp-up: filament 16.9 | o1 30 | o2 nan | o3 19.6. The root: the monopole basis column is a uniform current over the whole vessel, so orders 1–2 are a giant over-sized disc (no closed ring / 39 cm) and the octupoles do violent compensating work.

Worse, the most basic moment — the current centroid — is not reliably determined by the cell-current LSQ. Its current-weighted centroid swings across order and lands at nonsense:

Direct-moment fix (recommended experiment). Compute the moments directly from the coil-subtracted magnetics rather than solving a ~5000-cell current: (1) Ip from Ampère (already pinned); (2) the current centroid from a well-conditioned 2-DOF filament-position fit — robust, 5–11 cm from EFIT (a single filament there already beats the order-3 moment read on 18502, 5.3 vs 5.8 cm); (3) a handful of shape moments (quadrupole/triangularity) fitted about that fixed, correct centroid to supply the elongation the single filament lacks. This removes the null-space and the erratic-order behaviour, and is the classic magnetic-moments boundary method. If it holds across the cohort it supersedes the §13 selection (it would fix the moment read rather than gate around its failures).

§15 — The dynamic-sizing disc read (best-in-class, recommended)

Building §14 into a full read gives the strongest §2 boundary of the whole investigation — and it is strikingly simple:

  1. Ip — Rogowski (pinned).
  2. Centroid — a well-conditioned 2-DOF filament-position fit to the coil-subtracted sensors (B-probes AND flux loops — no extra TH layer needed for the flux-loop information).
  3. Disc radius — dynamically sized so the uniform disc's own push-out boundary minor radius is a self-consistent fixed point.
  4. Uniform Ip over the disc — no shape moments needed; the plasma elongation is supplied for free by the real coil field shaping the ψ_N=1 contour around the disc.
  5. Boundary — push-out of (disc ψ + coil ψ).

Scored vs EFIT across the full 16-slice cohort: 4.0–10.6 cm, never catastrophic — it beats the cell-current moment read on every slice and TH on every ramp-up (TH was 20–29 cm there). Representative rows [cm]: 18502 flat-top disc 5.2 / moment 5.8; 12143 ramp-up disc 10.3 / moment 15.3 / TH 28.5; 18505 ramp-up disc 7.1 / moment 19.6; 18504 ramp-up disc 5.4 / moment 14.9. It has no ill-conditioning (no 5000-cell null-space, no pole), no erratic-order behaviour, and no catastrophic regime.

Higher moments on the disc — conditioning-gated. A finite-area disc can carry shape; fitting a quadrupole about the fixed centroid on the (compact, well-conditioned) disc helps the well-conditioned flat-tops markedly (12143 flat-top 7.4 → 1.8 cm; 18502 5.2 → 4.9) but over-fits the poorly-diagnosed ramp-ups (10.3 → 10.7, 18505 7.1 → 8.1) and order 3 over-fits everywhere. So the shape moment is added only where the magnetics determine it (low fit residual), uniform otherwise — the same conditioning boundary, now a clean one-knob gate inside a single read.

Recommendation (updated): adopt the dynamic-sizing disc as the §2 boundary read — uniform disc for robustness, with a conditioning-gated quadrupole for well-conditioned slices. It is simpler and strictly more robust than the TH read, the cell-current moment read, and the §13 selection between them. Next: wire it into the §2 anchor and run the full §2+§3 re-gate against EFIT. Prototypes: scripts/_dynamic_disc_prototype.py, scripts/_moment_vs_th_boundary_report.py.

§16 — Staged disc read (final): skip-dipole + gated quadrupole-on-residual

The staged refinement completes the read. Fit order by order, subtracting each stage's signature from the sensor signal: stage 0 is the uniform disc at the filament-fit centroid (Ip pinned); degree-1 (dipole) is skipped — the position DOFs are already fixed by the 2-DOF centroid fit, so dipole terms on the residual only absorb noise and re-shift the position (measured: the dipole stage hurts on every slice); the quadrupole is fitted to the residual (a small well-conditioned 3-DOF LSQ). Two further findings: (a) with the quadrupole present the boundary is insensitive to disc size (shrink factors 0.7–1.0 equivalent on flat-tops), so the disc can sit at a physical fraction < 1 of the minor radius; (b) TH with its pole moved to the robust centroid was also tested and stays 14–67 cm — smooth but over-sized at any regularisation, because a vacuum multipole carries no current-extent information; the disc wins precisely because it is a physical source at the right extent.

The conditioning gate is a boundary-shift test, not a misfit test. The stage-0 sensor misfit does not separate the regimes (18505 flat-top misfit 4.2 → quad helps; 18505 ramp-up misfit 0.4 → quad hurts). What separates almost perfectly is how far the quadrupole moves the boundary: where it helps the shift is ≤ ~12% of the minor radius; where it over-fits it is ≥ ~20%. Production gate: accept the quadrupole only if it shifts the push-out boundary by < 15% of the disc radius — machine-agnostic, EFIT-free, self-contained. (Only 18502 ramp-up straddles the gate; its fallback is a safe 7.4 cm uniform read.)

Gated cohort performance: flat-tops 1.4–7.2 cm (median ~1.8 cm), ramp-ups 5.2–10.3 cm, never catastrophic — versus TH's 4.3 cm best / 29 cm worst and the cell-current moment's 5.8 / 20 cm. A vacuum-projection preconditioner (project the plasma signature onto the source-free sensor-signature span; the rejected residual is a per-slice noise meter) remains available as a refinement if the shift-gate proves insufficient on the full corpus. Script: scripts/_staged_disc_report.py.
