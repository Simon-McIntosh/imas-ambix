§1 — The problem: axisymmetric reconstruction from a non-axisymmetric machine

Every equilibrium solve in this project fits an axisymmetric field model to sensors that are (a) non-axisymmetrically placed 3D objects, (b) operating inside non-axisymmetric applied fields (RMP/ELM coils, error-field correction coils), and (c) coupled to non-axisymmetric passive conductors the frozen geometry model does not contain. The per-era magnetics scorecard (findings) measured the cost: the P4/P5 flux loops lose 30–50 % of their modelled vacuum coupling across the campaigns that installed the 12→18 in-vessel RMP coils, and that bias propagates through the current-centroid pin into every late-band reconstruction. The remedy is not to discard sensors or retire physics priors — it is to model the 3D machine and subtract what is not axisymmetric, so the axisymmetric solve only ever sees axisymmetric data.

Three distinct layers hide inside "non-axisymmetric contamination", with different physics, different sensors affected, and different validation data:

§2 — Evidence base (research landed 2026-07-22)

Scorecard drift anatomy. Per-family medians of |Δgain vs M5| / |offset/σ| show the failure is spatial, not sensor-type: centre-column loops 0.00–0.01 / 0.01 (stable to <1 % over 8 years); centre-column probes 0.01–0.05; outboard obr 0.01–0.02; outboard obv 0.11–0.16 / falling offsets; P-coil bay loops 0.28 (M7) → 0.58 (M8) → 1.54 (M9) with offsets shrinking 0.12→0.01. Gain change at constant-or-shrinking offset is a coupling/effective-area change, not electronics drift. In M9 the bay loops' vacuum gain is ≈ 0 (−0.11…+0.10) — the M9 vacuum flux-loop records are uncorrelated with the drive, so M9 has effectively no usable vacuum magnetics at all (pickup probes were already known to carry no vacuum data there).

Prototype (validated, in-tree: scripts/segment_biot_sensor_coupling.py). A 3D finite-segment Biot-Savart (field B and vector potential A, flux linkage by ∮A·dl) checks to 10⁻¹⁶ against the square-loop analytic and to 9×10⁻⁶ against the Maxwell coaxial-circles mutual. Applied to a MAST-like n=3 picture-frame RMP set at 5.6 kA·turns with catalogued sensor positions:

Consequences: (i) the energised layer B is a probe correction — loops don't need it (unless a loop is broken/partial, which the geometry ingestion must record); (ii) the big loop signal in the scorecard is layer A (passive) and/or C (instrument), so the 3D upgrade must be circuit-coupled (mutual-inductance L/R dynamics), not just field-from-known-currents; (iii) at ~0.4–0.9 % of signal, layer B matters at the precision of our residual fits and grows with RMP current — worth subtracting, cheap once the geometry exists.

Loop immunity confirmed empirically (scripts/flux_loop_ivc_immunity_check.py). On the hardest-driven plasma-free IVC shots (the 27394–27560 M8 commissioning series + 25836: error-field, ELM/RMP and horseshoe coils all live), the PF currents explain 99.9–100.0 % of every healthy flux-loop signal, and the IVC-coherent fraction of the residual is bounded at median 0.9 %, max 1.1 % of signal over 93 healthy loop-shot pairs — the full loops do NOT respond to energised in-vessel coils, exactly as symmetry predicts (artifact flux_loop_ivc_immunity.json). The one violator, fl_cc04 (PF-R² 0.006–0.11 in every shot tested), is a dead/faulty channel, not a coupling — routed to the scorecard's flagged-channel ledger. This cleanly separates the two RMP effects: the scorecard's 30–50 % loop-gain collapse comes from the coils being installed (layer A, passive), not energised (layer B).

Probes and saddles DO respond to energised in-vessel coils — measured. The same vacuum-IVC shots, refit jointly on PF + IVC drive terms (ΔR² = the variance the IVC terms actually explain — robust to collinearity inflation): saddle sensors ΔR² = 0.18–0.32 (up to a third of their total variance is energised CC-coil field; amplitudes to 12.7 σ), obv probes ΔR² = 0.063–0.065 (to 5.6 σ on obv09/obv11 via error_field_05), obr/ccbv 0.002–0.011, healthy full loops 0.0007–0.007. The control shot (25836, error-field not driven) drops every family to noise. The measured exposure ladder — saddles ≫ obv > obr/ccbv > loops ≈ 0 — is exactly the ordering the 3D geometry predicts: both halves of the validation (zero where zero, nonzero where nonzero) now exist empirically. Since the error-field coils are energised in essentially every shot, this probe contamination sits in every reconstruction today, unmodelled; and because the scorecard's vacuum fits did not carry the EF currents at all, part of the probe-family era drift (obr10, the strongest EF responder, was a flagged drifter) may be EF-usage aliasing. The EF currents are archived corpus-wide in calibrated kA·turn (channels error_field_a/b in M5–M6, relabelled error_field_02/05 by ~18500), so this is not a statistical nuisance to regress away — it is real, modelable physics: the CC coils get Biot-Savart Green's columns in the forward model exactly like the PF coils (a stronger physics spine), with the fitted per-sensor EF response columns serving only as the interim empirical stand-in (and later the validation target) until the D2 geometry lands. No CC coil geometry exists in-tree or in efm — D2 sources it from drawings/literature. Measured CC-current behaviour (430-shot sweep): energised in 52–80 % of shots in every era (not always on — good: on/off contrast is identifiability), median 1–3 kA·turn with large within-campaign variation (M7: ef_a median 0.20, max 14.3 kA·turn) that tightens to a clamped programme in M8 (max 1.6 / 3.9); energised in vacuum shots and pre-breakdown windows alike, so the validation seams cover it directly.

Step-vs-ramp test at the install boundaries — the passive-layer story does NOT survive it. If installing the IVC conductors changed MAST's passive response, the bay-loop vacuum gain must step at 19031 and 25404. A 430-shot per-shot-resolved sweep (dense ±600-shot windows at both boundaries) shows neither a step nor a ramp: the bay-loop gain is bimodal in every era including M5–M6 (two co-existing populations at ≈1.6 and ≈0.6, controls tight at 0.95–1.0 throughout), with a mid-M7 median "recovery" to pre-install values that no hardware story can produce. The scorecard's era-median drift is therefore a population-mixture shift: campaign programming changed which drive families dominate the coil-only windows, and a per-shot scalar gain lands on whichever mode the drive mix selects.

Prime suspect re-routed: a time-invariant, coil-specific coupling error (layer D). Two stable modes for the same sensor mean the loop's modelled response to a specific drive column is wrong by a large factor at all times — drive-side channel semantics (the amc coil/case/feed "pseudo coil current" definitions) or a model-column error for the adjacent P4/P5 circuits — and shot programming decides how often it is exposed. The early 85-shot audit already measured case-circuit scale factors of 0.66–0.71 and −0.04. First D3 rung, redefined: a per-era, per-(sensor, coil) G_emp decomposition on the sweep cohort to identify WHICH column carries the factor, separating drive-data error from geometry error from any genuine passive term. The passive circuit model remains in scope for what it can genuinely do (ramp-phase eddy on all conductors), but it is no longer the presumed explanation of the loop anomaly — gate G-A now adjudicates layer D first.

Flux-loop drift question (answered). Loops and probes do not suffer the same problem: the degradation is a property of location (proximity to the new conductors), not of the sensing principle. The era-stable far set (centre-column probes + obr + centre-column loops) can therefore anchor an axisymmetric vacuum fit that predicts each bay loop's flux and recalibrates it per era — a bay probe cannot correct a bay loop, but the far set can. This cross-instrument correction is layered under §5's discriminator.

§3 — What nova provides (capability survey, 2026-07-22)

The nova codebase (~/Code/nova, HEAD ae6385c0) carries the complete 3D kit:

  • Kernels (nova/biot/, registry in solve.py:31): line (finite straight segment), arc (circular arc in an arbitrary 3D plane, elliptic-integral), beam/bow (straight/arc with finite rectangular cross-section), plus the axisymmetric circle/cylinder/ polygon (the cylinder kernel is what we already extracted as imas_ambix/gs/cylinder.py). All return the vector potential Ax/Ay/Az and field Bx/By/Bz; axisymmetric kernels add ψ. Filament kernels are numpy/scipy-only — cleanly extractable.
  • RDP line/arc defeaturing: nova/geometry/polyline.py — an RDP-like decimator for mixed linear/arc polylines (PolyLine(points, arc_eps, line_eps, rdp_eps, minimum_arc_nodes, …).decimate()): greedily grows best-fit arcs (SVD plane-align + least-squares circle), demotes short arcs to chords, merges collinear runs via plain n-D RDP (nova/geometry/rdp.py, numpy-only). Output: a list of Line|Arc elements that feed the kernels directly. Tested in tests/test_polyline.py / test_biotsavart_3d.py.
  • Sensors & circuits: Point.solve(xyz) gives the B-vector at arbitrary 3D probe positions; flux through an arbitrary 3D loop is ∮A·dl over the kernel A-vector (the prototype demonstrates the pattern); Inductance.solve() yields circuit-level mutual inductances across 3D source elements — exactly what layer A's L/R model needs.
  • Worked ITER precedent: nova/imas/coils_non_axisymmetric.py ingests the coils_non_axisymmetric IDS (line/arc conductor elements) and nova/field/elm_3d_field.py assembles CC/VS3/ELM/CS/PF/TF 3D coilsets and computes fields — the direct analogue of the MAST RMP/EFCC workflow, and the bridge to the IMAS-DD pivot: author the MAST IVC geometry as a coils_non_axisymmetric IDS and both nova and this repo read it (connectivity-topology-reader f-ctr-16).
  • Portability: filament kernels + rdp are numpy/scipy-only; zeta (cylinder/bow) has a pure-Python fallback when numba is absent. No MAST 3D coil geometry exists in nova — we supply centrelines.

D3 — Passive layer (A): circuit-coupled eddy model, validated on vacuum ramps

Modeling idiom: single-turn 3D passive loop circuits. Each candidate passive path is represented as a closed single-turn 3D loop that mirrors the driving conductor's geometry — the RMP coil circuit itself (if electrically closed when idle), each coil's case/support frame, and the saddle structures — built from the same line/arc elements as the drivers. This is the 3D generalisation of the axisymmetric passive sidecar already in the engine (toroidally-continuous rings): a passive loop couples to the PF drive, to the other passive loops, and to every sensor exactly as an energised loop does, but its current is an internal state, not a measurement. The ported kernels supply everything needed: self-inductance L of each loop, the mutual matrix M (driver↔passive↔sensor) via ∮A·dl, and the sensor couplings; the only fitted quantities are the loop resistances R (equivalently time constants τ = L/R) — few parameters, identifiable from ramp data. State-space: L·dI_p/dt + R·I_p = −M_pd·dI_d/dt, sensors see M_sp·I_p. Upgrade path if discrete loops fail the gate: thin-shell mesh / eigenmode expansion of the conductor surfaces — but the discrete-loop rung comes first because its parameter count matches what vacuum ramps can actually constrain.

Mutual-inductance matrix between PF circuits, IVC circuits (including the idle-circuit boundary condition — see the decision below), and every sensor, from D1+D2. L/R dynamics fit on the per-era vacuum PF-ramp pool (the scorecard machinery already stratifies quasi-static vs dI/dt). Validation gate G-A (make-or-break): the passive 3D model must quantitatively reproduce the measured P4/P5 loop gain collapse (−30…−50 %, era-resolved) from first principles. If it reproduces the pattern → layer A confirmed, subtract it. If it cannot (e.g. predicted screening is 10× too small) → the loop collapse re-routes to layer C (instrument), and the correction becomes per-era calibration anchored on the stable far set — either way the pin/prior confound closes with a mechanism.

§ Decisions

Does this plan model the plasma response to non-axisymmetric fields?

Locked: no — vacuum taring only. The objective is to tare the diagnostic set back to the axisymmetric ideal: model only what is linear in known currents in a vacuum sense (drive columns, mutuals, measured screening constants), identifiable and validated plasma-free. The ~3× vacuum-vs-empirical discrepancy in Kirk et al. lives at rational surfaces inside the plasma (MARS-F territory) and never enters sensor-level vacuum subtraction. For plasma-on windows with IVCs energised, the applied vacuum field is subtracted and the unmodelled plasma n≠0 response is handled by flags + σ inflation on the exposed probes/saddles — absorbed by the learned layer, never fitted in the spine. Rationale: every uncorrected systematic becomes a biased label the world model learns as machine-specific physics, killing transfer; every plasma-entangled term fitted in the spine is unidentifiable physics smuggled into calibration.

In what order do the diagnostic-error levers land?

Locked ranking (impact × identifiability ÷ effort): (1) name the bimodal column — per-(sensor, coil) decomposition, the factor-2.7 bay-loop systematic, no new geometry needed; (2) coil-circuit semantic ledger — what each amc feed/coil/case pseudo-current channel means per era (the 0.66–0.71/−0.04 case-k class); (3) EFCC vacuum Green's columns from the Kirk et al. geometry, validated on EFCC-off/on contrast and locked-mode ramp shots; (4) per-era channel-health mask (fl_cc04, M9); (5) IVC-energised window flags + σ inflation; (6) passive ramp model anchored on the published τ = 31 ms; deferred — saddle disambiguation, full RDP port for complex conductors, all plasma-response work. The top two levers are drive-side data semantics, not 3D fields: the 3D modeling is real but second-tier.

Do the n=2/n=5 error-field correction coils belong in the modelled 3D set?

Locked: yes. The correction coils are energised in essentially every shot (error_field_05 median 3.2 corpus-wide) including the pre-breakdown vacuum window, so their n≠0 field contaminates the probes in all operating scenarios; they are also the most abundant validation source. The 3D field set = RMP/ELM coils + saddle structures + error-field correction coils + documented leads.

What is the electrical boundary condition of the in-vessel coil circuits when not energised — open circuit (no eddy path), crowbarred/shorted (full L/R eddy circuit), or era/shot-dependent?

This changes layer A qualitatively: an open coil contributes no circulating eddy (only its structure/case does); a shorted coil is a strong screening circuit.
