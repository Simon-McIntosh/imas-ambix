Design analysis answering four lead questions (2026-06-30): (1) why learn our own codebook rather than use a pretrained model like TimesFM 2.5; (2) confirm the video tokeniser; (3) how toroidal geometry should drive diagnostic grouping/placement; (4) ensure the tokenisers capture space phasing (rotating MHD), not only time phasing. Companion to absolute-geometry-token-substrate.

§0 — TL;DR

  • Video tokeniser = Open-MAGVIT2 (confirmed in code: tokenizer/frames.py OpenMagvit2Tokenizer, LFQ codebook, weights at /work/.../v1/open-magvit2).
  • "Learn our own" was never really the question. What failed for magnetics was a naïve per-scalar uniform quantiser, not learned tokenisation. But the deeper point is that no pretrained time-series foundation model captures the structure we need: they are all univariate / channel-independent temporal models. The thing that resolves the boundary — the cross-sensor relationship around the torus — is plasma-specific and is ours to build, whatever temporal backbone we adopt.
  • Toroidal geometry dictates the grouping. Diagnostics live on arrays: a poloidal array (varying θ at one φ) resolves the poloidal mode m; a toroidal array (varying φ) resolves the toroidal mode n and rotation. We currently tokenise only the single-φ poloidal probes — the toroidal array is missing, so we physically cannot see toroidal rotation.
  • Space phasing is the missing axis. A rotating MHD mode is B ∝ exp(i(mθ − nφ − ωt)). Resolving (m, n, ω) needs the relative phase between sensors — a magnitude-quantised, per-channel tokeniser destroys exactly this. The tokeniser must be geometry-aware and phase-preserving.

§1 — Why not just a pretrained foundation model (TimesFM 2.5, …)?

It is a crowded field, and the honest answer has two parts.

(a) The naïve quantiser, not "tokenisation", is what lost the boundary. Our staged magnetics path bins each sensor's scalar value, each timestep, independently into 256 uniform levels, then looks the bin id up in a learned embedding table — an unordered categorical code with no built-in ordinal/sign/metric. That is why continuous beat it ~2–3× (axis_Z +0.18→+0.58). A learned codebook (VQ/FSQ over patches, the video recipe) is the fair comparison, and is tracked separately (segment-codebook).

(b) But pretrained TS foundation models do not model the relation we need. TimesFM, Chronos/Chronos-Bolt (we already wrap chronos-t5-small), Moirai, MOMENT, Time-MoE, Toto and the rest are univariate / channel-independent: they embed one series at a time and have no notion of where a sensor sits or how it relates to its neighbours around the torus. They are trained on generic (retail/energy/weather) series — MHz Mirnov oscillations are out-of-distribution. They can be an excellent per-sensor temporal encoder (saving us from training that from scratch), but the cross-sensor spatial-phase structure that encodes the toroidal mode number is invisible to every one of them. So the recommendation is not "pretrained vs ours" — it is:

(optionally) a pretrained temporal backbone per sensor  +  OUR geometry-aware spatial-relational layer over the sensor array. The relational layer is the irreducible, plasma-specific part; no off-the-shelf model provides it.

§2 — Toroidal geometry → how diagnostics group

A tokamak is a torus: coordinates (R, Z, φ) — major radius R, height Z, toroidal angle φ; the poloidal angle θ runs around the (R,Z) cross-section. Magnetic diagnostics are deliberately laid out as arrays whose geometry sets what they can resolve:

  • Poloidal arrays — many probes spanning θ at one toroidal angle φ. They sample the poloidal structure → the poloidal mode number m. MAST's ccbv is exactly this: 40 Bpol probes spanning Z = −1.53…+1.45 m at fixed R≈0.18 m, all at a single φ.
  • Toroidal arrays — probes at the same poloidal location spread around φ. They sample the structure around the torus → the toroidal mode number n and the rotation. MAST has b_field_tor_probe_cc: 36 probes across 12 distinct toroidal angles (10°–350°).

Implication for grouping/placement in the tokeniser: sensors must be organised by their geometric role — each token tagged with its (R, Z, φ, orientation) so attention knows the angular separations, and arrays kept intact so the model can read the pattern across θ (for m) and across φ (for n). Grouping co-located sensors of different modalities (magnetics + SXR + interferometer sampling the same (R,Z,φ) region) is the natural next layer once geometry is the shared coordinate.

Concrete gap to close: extend the staged magnetics set (currently ccbv/obr/obv/flux_loop/ip — all single-φ poloidal) to include the toroidal array (b_field_tor_probe) and carry φ in its geometry, so the substrate can represent toroidal mode number and rotation at all.

§3 — Space phasing: the rotating-MHD axis we must capture

Fast MHD activity (tearing modes, kink/ballooning, ELM precursors) has the form B(θ, φ, t) ∝ exp(i(mθ − nφ − ωt)) and rotates toroidally (and poloidally) with the plasma. The information about which mode it is, and how fast it spins, lives in the relative phase between sensors at different φ (and θ), tracked through time — space phasing, the spatial analogue of the temporal phasing we already flagged.

Why our representations threaten this:

  • Per-channel independence discards cross-sensor relationships entirely — you cannot read a relative phase from channels coded in isolation. (This is also why every univariate foundation model is insufficient on its own.)
  • Magnitude quantisation throws away the fine phase: the multimodal-tokenisation study already measured that VQ/FSQ destroyed the cross-coil phase on xma (Mirnov), and continuous+mask won — precisely the space-phase fragility, now with the boundary at stake.
  • Pooling (the original probe's sin) averages the array into one vector — the rotating pattern is gone.

§4 — Design: a geometry-aware, phase-preserving relational tokeniser

The requirements above converge on one tokeniser design, treating every diagnostic like the cameras:

  1. Tokenise space-time SEGMENTS, not single scalars — patches across sensors (ordered by geometry: θ within a poloidal array, φ within a toroidal array) and across time, with a learned codebook (VQ/FSQ) or a continuous bottleneck, decided by measurement. This is the diagnostic analogue of Open-MAGVIT2's space-time patches.
  2. Geometry as positional encoding — each token carries (R, Z, φ, orientation) so attention can compute angular separations and resolve (m, n).
  3. Phase-preserving — keep the signed, complex/continuous structure (e.g. complex-STFT lift, cross-array spatial-DFT mode-number features — which patch_transformer.py already does per array for xma); do not collapse to magnitude bins.
  4. Optional pretrained temporal backbone — a TS foundation model can provide the per-sensor temporal embedding under the relational layer, but the relational/spatial structure stays ours.
  5. Include the toroidal array (§2) so n and rotation are representable at all; extend toward cross-group geometry-organised segments.

The acid test stays the firewalled diagnostics→equilibrium oracle (vs the raw-float ceiling axis 0.57–0.68 / xpt_R 0.50), now also asking whether the relational tokeniser resolves rotation and lifts the X-point, which the per-scalar/continuous probes could not.
