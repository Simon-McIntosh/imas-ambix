One reader for both engine families

imas_ambix/agent/engine_metrics.py now owns the mapping from a family's series names to canonical quantities for sglang: and vllm: alike. Before it, the same knowledge lived in three places: bench.py resolved vLLM names only, the workstation watcher carried both by hand, and lane.py::parse_lane_capacity carried a working SGLang reader of its own. The recorded consequence of the split is in the plan: fourteen of the recorder's twenty fields were null in every one of 13,791 rows against a healthy SGLang serve.

The module is a reader, not a probe: it takes exposition text and returns quantities, and it neither fetches nor decides. Three properties are deliberate and each is asserted by test.

Absence is omission, and a published zero survives

A quantity a family does not publish is left out of a reading rather than recorded as null or 0.0, because a null that reads as a reading is the defect being repaired. The rule was shown to be load-bearing rather than decorative: a mutated copy of the reader that records every quantity regardless reproduces the defect exactly, emitting prefix_cache_queries: None for the recorded SGLang scrape — precisely the assertion the new test forbids. Beside it, a quantity genuinely published as zero is kept: the SGLang fixture's gauges are a measured zero and its host cache tier answered nothing, so dropping those would make omission indistinguishable from a missing measurement.

Which samples count, decided once

SGLang repeats every device-pool measurement on each tensor-parallel rank, so only the rank-zero sample is one shared pool rather than N pools to sum. vLLM publishes reason-labelled partial series beside their family total, and folding a breakdown into its total double-counts. It also pairs every counter with a same-named _created gauge — a creation timestamp, not a data point — which a substring test folds into the token total. All three shapes are covered by the new tests, and the _created and reason exclusions are exact-name and exact-label matches rather than substring tests.

Evidence

A live SGLang scrape populates every canonical quantity. The recorder was run once, for one sample, against the running DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash serve on 98dci4-gpu-0003:18810. The prefix-cache keys were absent from that section, which is correct: SGLang publishes no such counter. In the same row the flat vLLM-shaped fields beside it stayed null, which is what a vocabulary with no meaning here looks like — and is why the canonical section, not the flat fields, is the record.

A recorded vLLM fixture yields the same field set through the same code path. tests/test_engine_metrics.py drives a recorded-shape vLLM scrape through the same engine_metrics.read_metrics call and asserts the full canonical set: three gauges, four counters, both histograms, the per-tier cached split with the uncached remainder, and the speculative-decode triple. A test whose presence rule is read from the family tables — not from a branch in the reader — checks that each family's section contains exactly the quantities its own tables publish.

Serve telemetry spine — execution record

§6 — Per-request receipts from the router

One append-only row per completed request, appended by the router as it relays. imas_ambix/agent/request_receipts.py holds the accounting and the sink; imas_ambix/agent/router.py tees its own relay into them. The row carries timestamp, model, upstream, prompt tokens, cached prompt tokens, completion tokens, time to first token, duration, the caller hint the router already derives, an outcome label, and the sampling fraction in force when it was written.

The tee never buffers the body the client receives: it is fed each relayed chunk and keeps only a bounded per-line remainder plus a bounded head for the non-streaming fallback, so a chunk the router forwards is never held; 8 MiB per line and 4 MiB of head are the two bounds, and neither limits what the client gets. Field names and the first-token definition are not re-derived — they follow bench.py::_stream_chat, and a test drives one SSE payload through both readers and asserts the agreement (1,234 prompt and 7 completion tokens from each), so a relay figure and a benchmark figure stay comparable rather than merely similar. The parse of usage.prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens is new: nothing else in the repository read that field.

The latency figure is the work the receipts add to one request, not end-to-end latency, and deliberately so: the row is written after the final response byte, so an end-to-end figure would be dominated by engine and socket jitter that no change here moves. Two bounded guards are exercised rather than assumed — a body truncated past the head bound is refused instead of parsed as a fragment, and a destination whose parent is a regular file logs once, disables the stream, and leaves the relay serving.

§6 — The outcome the relay observed, and who answered

Those rows were only as honest as the earliest moment their outcome could be set, and three ways the record disagreed with what happened had shipped. Each is repaired here, and each fix carries a control that was made to fail without it.

A 200 is what the engine answered, not what the caller received

The status was promoted to completed as soon as the upstream answered 2xx — before the body loop — and an exception out of the body read escaped the handler that rewrites the status for a cancelled caller, while the finally wrote the row anyway. An engine that answers 200, promises a content-length and then aborts its transport therefore left a completed row for a relay that delivered a fraction of the bytes.

The headers are now held rather than acted on, and the outcome is promoted only after the body has been forwarded whole. The control: with status = STATUS_COMPLETED set early again, the truncation test reddens with assert 'completed' == 'failed'; reverted, it passes.

A request the router answered itself left no row

Only _relay recorded anything, and the refusals and the catalog listing return before it, so the requests a caller reports as broken were absent from the record entirely — their only trace a log line. Every refusal the router produces now leaves through one funnel that also records it, so a new refusal path cannot widen what the router answers without widening the record; the catalog listing records after its 200. Rows carry a self-answered upstream sentinel rather than an engine origin, so a reader attributing traffic per upstream never folds them into a sink's share.

The sampling ceiling had no operator surface

The production sink was built with neither max_rows_per_s nor window_s, so the ceiling was fixed by source with no way to raise it. Both are now settings resolved from an explicit argument, then the environment, then the default, matching the environment surface the router already carries. The value in force is logged at construction, because a bound inferable only from behaviour is one that a later reader measures from scratch. A malformed value — not a number, zero, negative, or nan, which no arrival count can stay under — stops the launch rather than silently falling back to a default nobody chose.

The review round — three holes the repairs left

An independent review read the change and found three adjacent defects, each of which the owner of this section had left while fixing the original three. All are repaired, and each carries a control that was made to fail without it.

A caller that leaves while still uploading its request left no row at all. The handler read the body, found the caller gone, and returned — before any recording branch. That is the one outcome this section exists to record rather than drop: the request did reach the router and died there, and without a row its only trace is a server-side connection teardown. The disconnect branch now records a self-answered row, and because no engine was asked anything it carries the self-answered upstream sentinel and no model id. Nothing about what actually happened is lost, and the row's status names that outcome as aborted, which is what the label already meant — the caller went away before receiving its answer — widened rather than replaced.

The self-answered paths took their status as a literal. Every relayed response derives its outcome from what the caller received, but the two sites that record a router-composed answer passed the status they had just composed. Their answer may be unsent: a caller whose receive channel already holds http.disconnect still gets a full catalog response from _serve_catalog, and the row said so. The self-answered reply is now sent through the same watch the relay uses, which reports whether the caller had gone before the answer was handed over, and the status follows that rather than the composed code.

The self-answered sentinel was held by nothing. The test asserted the row's upstream against the constant imported from the module under test, so it followed any change to the constant — including changing it to an engine origin, which is exactly the confusion the field exists to prevent, since a reader summing traffic per upstream would fold the router's own answers into an engine's share. The value is now written out literally in the test, and one separate test asserts the constant equals it, so a change to the constant is a failure rather than a silent pass.

The watch itself needed care and the reason is worth stating: an ASGI server reports a completed response on the same receive channel as a departure (uvicorn's receive() returns http.disconnect once self.disconnected or self.response_complete), so a watch allowed to outlive the send cannot tell a caller that left from one that simply finished. The reply's watch is therefore read between the two sends and stopped at hand-over, which is why a caller still present still records completed.

The focused gate was taken on the all_debug partition, not the login node.

What a completed row promises, and the window it cannot see

The self-answered reply sampled the caller's departure one event-loop turn after the status line had been sent and before the body was sent, so a caller that aborted inside that window was still recorded completed. Sampling later does not close the window and was measured, not assumed: uvicorn marks a response complete inside the send that writes its body, and its receive channel then answers http.disconnect for a completed response exactly as it does for a caller that left, so a watch read after the hand-over reports every caller as gone. The send reports nothing either way, because a server that finds the caller gone when it writes the body returns from that send without raising. The row's word was therefore narrowed as the record's word, and the residual window named.

Row's wordDoes it mean
completedthe answer was sent whole, with no departure reported before it changed hands
abortedthe caller had gone when the departure was sampled: mid-relay, before its answer was sent, or while still uploading its request
failedeverything else: an upstream error status, a refusal handed to a caller that had not gone, or a relay that raised

Sent is not received, and the count is of sends. Nothing on the serving side of the exchange can state that a caller read its answer, so a reader summing completed rows is counting answers delivered into the record of departure. The docstring on the self-answered reply, the outcome-label documentation and this table each state that limit, so the sum has one meaning wherever it is read. A departure that lands between the sample and the hand-over is unobservable from here and is named rather than relied on silently.
