SLURM-Hosted Agent Fleet — Measured Evidence

Cumulative execution record for SLURM-Hosted Agent Fleet — take the clive lane off the four-core login slice. One anchor per landed node. The diagnosis of why the fleet dies with the connection that started it is a separate record and is not duplicated here: the session-scope teardown record.

Throttling baseline of the login account slice

This is the reading the fleet move is judged against, taken before anything moves. It comes from the account's own control group on the login node, /sys/fs/cgroup/user.slice/user-39486.slice, whose quota line is 400000 100000 — four cores for the entire user slice, not per session. Node uptime at the first reading of the series was 43 days 20 h 52 m (3790349.80 s), so the cumulative counters have been accumulating for the whole of that period.

What the share measures

The share is stalled CPU thread-time over the thread-time the ceiling permitted. Four cores allowed once per 100 ms period permits four times the period in thread-time, so the permitted total over the accounted periods is the quota multiplied by nr_periods with the period cancelling out. Thread-time was chosen over a simple period count because it is the quantity that compares a four-core slice against a twenty-eight-core allocation directly.

The share can exceed one, and it does. The throttled_usec counter sums over every task that was stopped, while the budget is one quota per period: once more tasks are runnable than the quota admits, several of them stall together for the remainder of each period, and the thread-time lost passes the thread-time the ceiling allowed. A value above one is therefore a statement about oversubscription, not an arithmetic fault, and the sampler leaves it unclamped.

Figure — the flat cumulative share against the five window readings. The cumulative figure moves by less than 0.0001 across the series because each window is a few minutes against 43 days; the windows themselves span a factor of 4.4.

A single window is a draw, not the level. The cumulative share is stable to four decimal places, while its windowed form ranged from 0.425 to 1.865 within five adjacent minutes — 325 to 553 of 600 periods throttled. Any comparison against one 60-second reading would be measuring which minute it landed in, so the baseline for the plan's step six is the cumulative ratio, and a working-day comparison needs a collector that samples across the day rather than a longer single window.

The consequences the plan draws from this follow directly: at 84–92% periods throttled the slice is stopped for most of every period it is busy, so a health probe or handshake that has to complete inside one is waiting on a queue that has four cores of throughput shared with roughly thirty session processes and everything else in the account.

The sampler, and how to repeat this

imas_ambix/agent/throttling.py reads cpu.max and cpu.stat text from a directory the caller supplies — never a fixed path — and prints the cumulative and interval readings as JSON. It carries its own _parser() / main() entry point rather than routing through the operator CLI, so a monitor can read a slice without importing it:

# one reading
uv run python -m imas_ambix.agent.throttling sample \
    --directory /sys/fs/cgroup/user.slice/user-39486.slice

# a reading plus the share over the next 60 seconds
uv run python -m imas_ambix.agent.throttling interval \
    --directory /sys/fs/cgroup/user.slice/user-39486.slice --seconds 60

The unit gate is uv run --no-sync pytest tests/agent/test_throttling.py, covering the cumulative share, the delta share across two samples, the refusal of a counter that went backwards between samples, and the unquota-ed reading where cpu.max is the literal max 100000 and the share is undefined rather than zero.
