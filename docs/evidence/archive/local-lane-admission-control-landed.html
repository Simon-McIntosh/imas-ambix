The throughput knee, measured on the engine decode log

The instrument is the engine's own per-step log, not a window aggregate. The scheduler emits one decode step per line carrying the running request count, the speculative accept length, and that step's aggregate generation rate. The reader groups those intervals by exact running width and takes the file's per-width median, with a percentile bootstrap interval, so a width bin is a width and not an average over a window in which the width moved.

Two serve logs were read on a compute node, never on the login node. The five-day job (1273253) yielded 79,436 decode intervals with 0 unreadable, spanning 2026-09-17 22:01:09 to its cancellation at 2026-09-23 06:12:04; 36 widths cleared the 20-interval threshold. Its live replacement (1276246) is a snapshot taken while it grows.

The aggregate rate rises steeply to about width sixteen and is then flat from width 17 to 32. Over that plateau the per-width medians sit between 365.8 and 389.9 tok/s. The observed operating band, widths 29 to 31 pooled, reads 379.1 tok/s against a plateau median of 378.5 — a ratio of 1.0. The two counts are the same point.

The maximum-median width is the engine's cap, and it is confounded

The largest per-width median anywhere in 1 to 32 is 389.9 tok/s at width 22, a mild local high inside a 380 to 390 band; widths 23 to 32 then sit at 366 to 384, not at 340. The overall maximum is 436.7 tok/s at width 36 (24 intervals, 95% interval 418.5 to 448.9) — the engine's running cap, so no wider bin exists. The ratio of the operating band to that peak is 0.868. On the live replacement job the peak sits at width 35 and the same ratio is 0.868.

That peak is reported as measured, not as a knee. Every interval at width 29 and above was logged in one mid-run window, 2026-09-18 11:xx to 2026-09-21 21:xx, and the width-36 bin carries a higher speculative accept length (3.82) than the operating band (3.65). Its throughput advantage therefore tracks the acceptance of that window rather than width alone. No width above 36 was ever logged, so no knee below the cap appears in this instrument, and the engine log does not contradict the profile's reason for setting the cap at 36.

Which earlier claim the data supports, and why they disagree

The data supports the profile's flat claim, not the ~22 knee. The flat profile claim covers widths 17 to 32, and the measurement reproduces it there (band to plateau ratio 1.0). The later claim that aggregate output peaks near 22 concurrent at about 500 tok/s and falls to about 340 past it is not reproduced: the maximum median in 1 to 32 is at width 22 but only 389.9 tok/s, and nothing on this lane reaches 500 tok/s at any width. The two disagree because they measure different objects. The ~22 knee is a window mean: its ordinate is a window-averaged aggregate rate and its abscissa a mean concurrency (one such window reads 23.05 with a peak of 34), so one bin mixes many instantaneous widths and the knee it finds is where per-client rate collapses, not where the engine's aggregate rate stops rising. The decode-log instrument pairs a per-step rate with the exact instantaneous width; it separates widths cleanly and finds a flat band with no interior peak.

The pipeline is checked against a planted peak

The same reduction, run on a synthetic log with a generation peak planted at width 12 and again at width 25, reports back width 12 and width 25. A pipeline that reported a peak regardless of its input would fail this; it is asserted on every run and the run exits non-zero on a mismatch.
