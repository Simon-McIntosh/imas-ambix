Accept length is the measurement that forced the change, and it was assumed flat. The published prediction carried 3.31 at every width. Measured here it is 3.585 at width one, peaks at 4.640 at width two, and falls with width to 4.030 at width eight, 3.970 at width nine and 3.615 by width eleven. Because every ratio in the published table is linear in that number, the assumption understated the low-width gain and overstated the high-width one.

The refit does not reproduce the predicted collapse. Against the non-speculative baseline measured on the previous serve (job 1272167), the refitted ratio falls from 4.753 at width one to a shallowest 1.312 at width 12.7 and recovers; it never falls below 1.2 at any width. The original model predicted 1.06 near width eight. Ratio at width 34 is 1.932.

What this fit does not license. The widths in the fit span GB 42.6 to 277.5. Widths above nine are extrapolation, and this serve never exceeded width eleven, so the high-width recovery and the "never below 1.2" statement are model behaviour, not measurements. The plan's own §4b asks for width 1, 8 and 24 or above for exactly this reason. The fixed floor is not well identified as separate from the slope on this data: refitting over different width subsets moves it from 12.44 to -3.16 ms, while the slope only moves from 0.267 to 0.356 ms/GB. Report only the slope change as the substantial finding; the floor should be treated as small and poorly determined. The width-one residual (+8.58 ms) also shows the two-term linear form over-predicts the single-stream gain (4.75 modelled against 3.055 measured at width one).
