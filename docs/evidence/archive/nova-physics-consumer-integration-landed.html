One version skew explains all four failures — and the pin advance is the fix

The four baseline failures are not four problems. They are one artifact-versus-campaign version skew, and the repair is the pin advance already recommended on independent grounds. Read-only diagnosis, no commit.

The pinned Nova revision publishes a superseded machine description. Its physical digest is ca06c8f64481114f, carrying four obsolete flux-loop positions; the target revision carries b55c5bb005a2cb67. Nova commit f8fc19ce, an ancestor of the recommended target aeffde05, is what republishes the restored positions.

The failure chain is mechanical, and Ambix behaves correctly at every step:

  1. Ambix applies the vacuum-adjudicated positions fl_p4l_1 = (1.5984, −1.04443) and fl_p4l_4 = (1.4005, −1.14403) (imas_ambix/data/geometry_adapter.py:750-766).
  2. The pinned Nova publisher reads its registry geometry directly (nova/imas/mast_geometry.py:235-245) and still carries the obsolete positions.
  3. The two disagree by 0.370151 m and 0.437069 m, far past Ambix's 0.05 m fail-closed mapping bound (imas_ambix/gs/geometry.py:782-789), so the channels are dropped — giving the observed missing set {fl_p4l_1, fl_p4l_4, fl_p6u_1} where only fl_p6u_1 is expected.
  4. The ratio fixture uses only artifact-present shared rows, so losing those two rows perturbs the p6l and solenoid columns downstream.

That fail-closed bound did its job: it refused to map a channel onto a position 37 cm away rather than silently accepting it.
