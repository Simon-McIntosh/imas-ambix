What landed, and what did not

The wiring landed and is proven. The engine did not: four bring-up attempts each found a different real defect, and the checkpoint the plan named turned out to be unusable. Every attempt is recorded below, because the sequence is the result — each failure was upstream of the trap the plan had warned about, and none repeated another.

Four attempts, four distinct causes

Job 1260688 — no tokenizer. The community INT4 requant ships 241 weight shards and no tokenizer assets at all, while the official release on the same disk carries tokenizer.json and tokenizer_config.json. The engine died 59 s in during tokenizer construction, before any weight or KV initialisation — upstream of the lazy first-decode trap the plan warned about. Fixed by letting a deployment borrow a tokenizer from another released checkpoint, verified rather than assumed: vocab_size 154880, pad 154820 and eos [154820, 154827, 154829] identical across both configs, zero mismatches.

Job 1260703 — the serving interpreter. Died two seconds in on a SyntaxError in a repository module. Full account in the router record.

Job 1260712 — the ceiling was arithmetic. Loaded all 241 shards in 861 s at 94.38 GiB per card, then aborted at KV init: the configured 262144 tokens need 22.61 GiB against 20.74 GiB available, with the engine putting its own ceiling at 240384. The profile comment had predicted "a ceiling near 400K". The configured value became 131072, deliberately not the engine's 240384 — at the measured cost per token that needs about 11.3 GiB and leaves about 9.4 GiB for the sparse-MLA kernel's roughly 8 GiB per-card lazy claim on the first real decode. A ceiling at the engine maximum would have reached ready and died on its first request.

Job 1260720 — served, and useless. The headroom choice was right: 241 shards in 859 s, 20.71 GiB pool, GPU KV cache 240,064 tokens, 1.83× concurrency at the configured ceiling, Application startup complete, an anonymous catalog carrying glm-5.3 with an intact metadata block naming count 4 and precision int4, and a first real decode that survived. But every decoded output was incoherent and two tool attempts produced zero tool_use blocks.

The borrowed tokenizer was therefore correct and necessary, and the fault lies in the community requant's weights or their execution path. That checkpoint is no longer reachable from the profile.

Which GLM-5.3 artifact can be served

Settled against the serving vLLM 0.28.0 rather than the project virtualenv's 0.23.0 — an earlier answer used the wrong engine and had to be re-taken.

  • GLM-5.3-Flash: not serveable. The registry returns zero entries for Glm5NextForConditionalGeneration, the architecture its config declares.
  • camel-ai/GLM-5.3-W4A8: serveable. Its GlmMoeDsaForCausalLM architecture is registered, both compressed-tensors W4A8 loader classes import from the serving package, the complete manifest is 154 files totalling 399,754,189,403 bytes, it fits four 141 GB cards with about 164 GB aggregate headroom, and it ships its own tokenizer — so the borrowed tokenizer source was removed rather than carried over.

Transferred as job 1260756 into its own weights directory, completing in ten minutes.
