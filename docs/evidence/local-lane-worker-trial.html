§1 — What was measured, and the standard applied

Two coordinators ran the locally-served deepseek-v4-flash lane (clive) inside real sprints on 2026-09-05 — nine nodes across imas-codex and nova — and compared it against the two metered lanes on the same kinds of work. The instruction that shaped the method was that viability must be judged on reviewed code receipts, not exit or pass rates.

So every landed diff was read hunk by hunk before promotion, and each row records six fields rather than a verdict:

  1. the diff read line by line, not the gate verdict;
  2. whether any refusal was a correct refusal;
  3. whether the worker separated its own failures from pre-existing ones;
  4. throughput, taken from the run's own records;
  5. defects found by review that the node's own gate could not see, split into structural (the gate could not reach the change — the orchestrator's error) and assertion (it could have, with a better check — the worker's);
  6. gate ran on the target surface: yes/no — did the named gate exercise the surface the change writes to (a backend, a device, an excluded marker).

Two further columns were added once they proved to matter: reviewer provenance (coordinator-read or independently-read) and manifest honesty (whether the worker's own account of what it did survives checking).

Every lane was reviewed to the same depth. Reviewing only the trial lane and accepting the metered lanes on their gate verdicts would attribute review-detectable defects to the trial that are not model-specific. That decision is what produced the trial's most useful result, in §3.

§2a — Three reporting defects that make these numbers hard to read

The local lane reports no per-message output usage. Every assistant message carries output_tokens: 0. The terminal result record does carry totals and crew complete reads them, so the lane is measurable at promotion and blind in flight — a coordinator cannot see a running node's consumption while deciding whether to let it continue. The first version of this report called the gap absolute; it is not.

But "measurable at promotion" has an exception, and it is not a rare one. A run that ends while a background task is still live exits without emitting the terminal result record at all. One node in this trial did exactly that: its ledger row carries a passing gate and a 7192-second wall, and null for cost, generated tokens and tok/s. Its only figures are stream-derived — 265 assistant turns, and 302,231 thinking characters against 1,542 of text, 99.5% thinking, the highest of any node here. Its throughput is recorded above as unavailable rather than estimated, which is the honest entry.

So the ledger's cost column on this lane is not merely imputed — it is sometimes absent, and a router reading it would see a free node. That is the same "a worker's process ends with its turn" failure the shared guidance already carries, now with a bookkeeping consequence nobody had connected to it: the briefing error that loses a gate also loses the accounting.

Client-side cache figures for that lane are wrong, and this correction reversed a conclusion. Client usage records report zero cache reads, from which imas-codex concluded the lane had no prompt caching and wrote a cost section on it. Sampling the live two-card engine's own metrics gave prefix_cache_hit_rate = 0.3389: vLLM automatic prefix caching is active and hitting about a third. So it is substantially a reporting gap rather than a compute gap.

The comparison survives and the recommendation sharpens. Against 97.9% cached on the codex node and 99.0% on the claude node, this lane re-reads roughly two thirds of its context every turn rather than all of it. That still explains the throughput and why a long node does not accelerate as context accumulates — but the lever is cache configuration, not cache introduction.

And roughly a third is the steady state, not a warm-up artefact — which one engine could not establish and had been left open here. A freshly started four-card engine measured 28.0% (3.56M hits / 12.70M queries) and climbing, against 33.9% on a four-day-old two-card one, both reported by the serving project. So the gap against the metered lanes' 97–99% is structural rather than a matter of letting the cache settle, and the headroom is real.

The lane's ceiling is not generation, and a router reading generation throughput would read the wrong quantity. Peak generation on four cards measured 418.5 tok/s against 120.5 on two, with a mean of 83.9 across three concurrent agentic nodes — beside the 22–33 tok/s wall rates in the table above. Most of an agentic node's wall clock is not generation, so more generation capacity moves it less than the cache does.

Speculative decoding is live on the four-card serve and accepting. Three samples taken independently across the day, on two engines: 46.2%, 46.4% (94,320 drafted / 43,772 accepted, mean acceptance length 3.32 tokens per decode step, per-position 0.744 / 0.580 / 0.443 / 0.320 / 0.223), and 45.3% cumulative over one engine's two-hour life (770,075 drafted / 348,971 accepted, 503,076 generated, five nodes, five of five inside their fences with zero corrective redispatches). Counters reset when that engine was replaced, so each figure belongs to the engine that produced it. The claim that survives the restart is the stability, not any single number.

And adding cards buys compute, not context. The live KV pool on the four-card serve measures 1,313,935 tokens against 1,372,204 recorded for the two-card profile — the same, because this model's single compressed KV head does not shard across tensor parallelism. max_model_len is a per-request ceiling rather than a reservation, so raising it costs nothing at rest; what it costs is headroom when a very large prompt actually arrives, with concurrency at maximum length falling from about 2.51 to 1.25. Three concurrent agentic sessions were using 19% of the pool, roughly 83K each — nowhere near it.

So the routing conclusion, stated plainly because it is easy to expect the opposite: more cards will not make an agentic node finish sooner. The lane's ceiling is turn count and thinking volume, not generation, and the speculative-decoding gain shows up in the acceptance counters far more clearly than in anyone's wall clock. Nobody should expect the card count to halve a 130-minute node.

Ledger cost for an unmetered lane is imputed, and it inverts the comparison. Measured across 619 ledger rows by the reckon session: clive at a median $21.59 per node against claude's $1.61 — the free lane ranks about thirteen times dearer. Reckon shipped a routing view whose routing quantity is cost per durable node, so the surface built to make routing legible currently inverts it; that section's implementation figure has been reduced rather than left reading as landed. A ledger pricing an unmetered backend should write zero or flag the figure as imputed.

Scraping caution, if any of this is re-measured. Matching vLLM metric names by substring silently sums each counter's _created timestamp gauge and the external_ cross-instance counters into the real totals. Match exact bare names. On this engine they are vllm:kv_cache_usage_perc — not gpu_cache_usage_perc — vllm:prefix_cache_queries_total and vllm:prefix_cache_hits_total.

§3 — Reviewed code receipts

Nine clive nodes, all promoted, zero corrective redispatches to a frontier lane. One needed a stop-and-resume for a brief defect described in §4. That is the headline number, and it is not the interesting one.

The interesting one: the coordinator was wrong more often than the local lane was. On imas-codex, six coordinator errors were caught during this trial — by workers on three different lanes, and by one guard:

The consequence for method: a control column built from coordinator review inherits the coordinator's errors. Nova's independent read-only Opus reviewer, with no stake in the nodes it graded, is the better instrument, and rows should carry reviewer provenance so the two are not read as equivalent.

§3a — What the metered lanes' green gates hid

A sonnet node returned a green, mutation-proven gate on a change raising stamped write sites in one module from 3 to 118. Reading the diff found three real defects its checker could not see, because the checker's predicate asked whether a SET block mentioned the stamp anywhere rather than per alias: the one statement in the file that provably modifies nothing was stamped twice, and a genuinely modified alias was left unstamped by the same statement that stamped its sibling. Alongside them, one structural hole owned by the orchestrator: the gate ran under default markers that exclude the marker requiring a live database, so none of the 118 modified statements was ever executed.

Nova's independent Opus review of three codex-merged commits, each with a green gate no human had read, found one HIGH correctness defect, two LOW, and two coverage holes — and judged that none of the three gates could structurally have seen any of them, because each gate is a bit- or byte-identity comparison over inputs drawn from the regime the change preserves. The HIGH one: a census takes the first 2*maxsize admitted origins via jnp.where(size=) and publishes overflow False, so a field admitting more than 60 origins silently drops the rest. The gate's own fields admit 2 each; a probe on the gate's own lattice admitted 147 and changed 31 of 39 published keys.

Nova's own disclosure belongs here: roughly thirty codex merges that day were audited from manifest, gate log, diff stat and hygiene grep without line reads. Those rows carry no measured error rate only because nobody has measured one; the review's roughly one-in-three defect rate on the sample it did read is the honest prior for them.

§3b — What the local lane returned

Three behaviours are worth more than the pass count, because each is a worker declining to make itself look better:

  • It declared its own headline measure vacuous rather than banking it. A node asked to drive virtual-environment rows in a scan to zero measured 101 entries before and 101 after, and said plainly that the release dry run never executes the check in question, so neither run could have surfaced such a row. Having lost its measure it built a replacement the brief had not suggested: four fixture tests, one proving a genuine divergence is still reported and one proving a nested environment file can never become a comparison authority, plus a counterfactual probe confirming the first fails if the scan is narrowed to nothing.
  • It refused to weaken a gate to pass it. Nova's census repair was asked for byte identity in every case; it found the compiled path drifts from the eager authority by a last ULP in one array on a marginal noise field, so it asserted byte identity on every structural and telemetry key, a stated tolerance on the value arrays, kept the analytic test byte-exact, and declared the split in both the manifest and a named key set in the test.
  • It reported a base it was not given. Two nodes were handed a suite baseline of 5 failures, measured 19 and 27 respectively at their own revisions, and said so instead of adopting the figure. That is how the finding in §5c surfaced at all.

One new local-lane failure mode, and it is the orchestrator's. A pin re-expression node landed a clean +42/-23 diff over two of its three declared paths — correctly leaving the third alone because both its pins were green, as briefed — and its reasoning survived a hunk read: it found why the old fixture broke (a wall offset put the inboard wall pole above the axis value under the spline read), fixed the fixture rather than the pin, and re-derived the value keeping the seam identity exact. But its three-file after-gate never completed: it backgrounded the run and its process ended with its turn, so the log truncates after two passes.

That is the documented hazard, and the guidance names the owner: a done-when that asks for a long suite result invites the worker to background it and lose everything after it, which is the orchestrator's error rather than the worker's. It scores against the model only lightly on manifest honesty, because the manifest says pending rather than claiming a pass. The repair is in the briefing — keep a long measure in the foreground under a bounded budget, or say in the done-when that it may be backgrounded and that the manifest must be written first.

Instruction adherence, scored separately from code quality: one node piped CLI output through tail against an explicit repository ban in a file its brief told it to read; one left a stub manifest at in_progress through termination, which did not recur once the rule was stated; and one attempted harness-native sub-agent fan-out three times, each refused by the crew guard, after which it did the work itself. That last is a guard-that-spoke datum: the contract is enforced, not merely stated.

§3c — Defects surfaced incidentally by exercising a path

Distinct from defects in a node's own output, and worth its own count: three nodes exposed problems they were not looking for, simply by running code nobody had run that way recently.

  • A dry run that is not dry. Exercising the release path showed sn release --dry-run mints a release-shaped roster into the repository and advances the release-candidate counter, because the artifact freeze runs three steps before the dry-run flag is consulted. It probably explains three earlier rosters sitting in that directory.
  • A hard-coded gap kind in shared enforcement machinery. A preflight compared against a literal "self_contradiction", so it silently refused the graph write for every resolution cohort that was not the one it had been exercised against. Found by the first node to need a different kind.
  • A production regression, on the parallel project. A pin node found that on today's head the fixed-design class margin of an 81×81 persistent-saddle fixture reads NaN with diverted False across all 17 sweep shifts, where the test expects positive infinity — so that test now fails upstream of the pin the node was sent to fix. Not yet bisected; two candidate merges. The node reported it and did not touch production.

None was in a brief. Two of the three came from the local lane. The pattern is that a lane cheap enough to point at neglected paths finds things there, and that is worth counting separately from whether the node's own gate went green.

§4 — What decides whether the local lane succeeds

The governing variable is brief specificity, not task difficulty. Four nodes on the same model make the case, two of them hours apart in one project:

NodeBrief shapeFirst materialisationOutcome

It compounds with the cache finding: a lane that re-reads two thirds of its context every turn gets dearer the longer it explores, so a brief inviting exploration is its worst fit and a fixed target its best.

The cost of the lever, stated honestly. A brief can only name the target when the coordinator already knows the answer's shape, so it trades worker exploration for coordinator work — and the six coordinator errors in §3 are that trade appearing on the other side of the ledger. A brief specific enough to make this lane succeed is specific enough to be confidently wrong, and one of them was. So the recommendation is not "write more specific briefs" alone: write the target-naming brief, and expect the worker to contradict it. The nodes that corrected their coordinator did so because their measures were checkable and their fences held.

§5 — Three defect classes both projects found independently

§5a — The regime-bound check

A check whose inputs never leave the regime its correctness assumes. It passes, it is honestly written, and it is silent about everything outside that regime.

SeverityDefectThe regime it never left
HighA census truncates admitted origins at a fixed size and publishes overflow FalseThe gate's fields admit 2; a probe admitted 147 and changed 31 of 39 keys
LowA Cypher SET-clause boundary scanner ends the clause at the first WHERE, including one inside a list comprehension, truncating the very statement holding the fault soughtEvery statement it had run against had no list comprehension in its SET body
LatentNew code reads four keys off a live diagnostics dict; the tests monkeypatch that dictThe gate never reads the real host, so it cannot say the keys exist there

The remedy is one question, and it found all three: before trusting a green gate, name one input that leaves the regime and ask whether the gate would notice. None came from reading the diff harder. This belongs beside the fail-open guard rule, which is its sibling: a fail-open guard reports a protection it does not provide, and a regime-bound check reports a coverage it does not have.

§5b — Implicit test contracts on incidental structure

A test can silently make something incidental into a contract, so a change that is semantically free breaks it — or a change that is semantically real passes.

  • Query text. Tests that mock a graph client and dispatch on the literal leading token of a SET clause. Comma-separated assignments to independent properties are order-independent in Cypher, so prepending one is semantically free and cost 22 test failures across a suite nothing had measured for hours.
  • Field-name sets. Tests that monkeypatch a host diagnostics dict, making the set of key names a contract: a renamed key passes the gate and fails in production.

Opposite directions, same invisibility. The remedy is a line in the repository's own guidance wherever it holds: these tests pin query text / these tests pin key names.

§5c — A base that belongs to the run rather than the revision

Three measurements of one suite disagreed: 5, 19 and 27 failures, the 5 a strict subset of the 19 at the same revision with freshly generated models in both trees. The extra 14 concentrate in six export and catalog-ledger files.

This undermines the instrument every node is gated on. "Added failures against a stated base" is only a measurement while the base is a property of the revision; where it is a property of the run, a node handed a base of 5 that measures 19 cannot distinguish a regression from the weather. The rule that follows: a base belongs to a revision only if it was measured at that revision in the same session. Nova checked its own gates against this and found none quoting a carried figure; its known non-determinism sources are timing tests and CPU-versus-GPU identity assertions, both outside its gate sets, so the same rule protects against different things in the two projects.

§5d — Reading a proxy as the thing itself

Six instances across three sessions in one day, and it is the sibling of the regime-bound check rather than the same thing: a regime-bound check is wrong about the inputs it was given; a proxy reading is wrong about the field it consulted.

Proxy consultedThing it stood forReported by

Each proxy is usually faithful, which is exactly why substituting it is invisible. In all six the authority was cheap to consult — the backend field, the object database, argv, the whole file, the engine's metrics, an ordinary client. Nobody was blocked from checking; each of us stopped one step early because the proxy answered. So the remedy is one question: what is the authority for this, and did I read it?

The class discriminates, which is what makes it a class rather than a mood. Tested against the same day's other errors, it correctly excludes: a stale figure quoted from another node's gate (fix by deriving, not quoting); a gate started against a moving tree (re-measure in the same beat); a guard scoped to a repository instead of one tree (scope); and a fixture carrying a field its parser never reads. A frame that absorbed all of those would be useless.

One operational rule, and its justification is asymmetry rather than frequency. When a check contradicts a peer's measurement, suspect the check first. The tempting evidence for this — that in most of the escalated disagreements here the peer was right and the checker's method was flawed — does not support it, because the sample is selected on disagreements that got escalated: cases where the checker was right resolve quietly and never enter the tally. There is a counter-instance from the same day, where a peer named a symptom precisely and attributed it to the wrong cause while the check was sound.

The rule survives on cost instead. Re-checking your own method when contradicted costs one measurement. Propagating a wrong record costs whatever is built on it — and in the cache case it would have replaced a real zero with a figure belonging to another lane, inside a report already driving config changes.

A seventh instance, and it says how the class is actually caught. About an hour after this section was written, its author committed it again: checking whether a ledger row existed, by looking for two plausible top-level key names, finding neither, and coming within one call of reporting that a promotion succeeds while recording nothing. The row was there, one level down. The defect did not exist.

What stopped it was not knowing about the class — the section was already written — but asking its question at the moment of claiming: what is the authority for this, and did I read it? Applying that to the check itself took one call. So the practical form of this finding is a habit bound to an event, not an insight: the moment before you report a defect in someone else's system is the moment to re-read your own instrument. Knowledge of the class did not prevent the error; the question caught it before it left the session.

§7 — What the shared routing config should say

Applied to this workstation's flight.yaml during the trial, from these measurements:

  • sandbox: worktree-full and session_reuse: true declared on the local backend — their absence was an omission, not a decision; it had been the least completely specified backend and, by node count that day, the highest-volume one.
  • usable_input_window: 492288, the endpoint's own advertised figure, so nodes are sized against what the server accepts. Peak per-turn input observed was 356,574 tokens, 72% of it, never clamped.
  • time_budget: 90m, advisory and deliberately not enforced. A launcher fence was recommended and then withdrawn: the long walls were all measured on the two-card deployment, and a fence calibrated on them would be calibrated on a configuration that is being replaced. Plan waves on wall clock the coordinator keeps itself.
  • effort stays pinned and is not a calibration axis here. The endpoint returns a thinking block at every budget including none, so the value is declared rather than effective, and a shadow run substituting it compares nothing. Thinking is 83–95%+ of emitted characters regardless.
  • Spec level is the setting that matters, and it has no schema key, so it is recorded as a routing comment: exact — trust unsupervised; guided — only when the measurement is handed over as established fact; open — never route here. Keep open design, decide-when-measurement-is-done, and merged-diff review on a frontier lane.
  • Roles trusted unsupervised at exact: implement, and test or measurement with a read-only fence, where the lane was strongest.

Two further recommendations for the machinery rather than the file: fence on unifiedWindows.<period> against its own reset, never on the overage line; and declare the account a backend belongs to, so one observation serves every preset over it.

§8 — Method, and what this report gets wrong

Recorded because a trial that hides its own corrections is not evidence.

  • The cache claim was reversed. This report first stated the local lane has no prompt caching, from a client-side zero read as a fact about the engine. It caches about a third. The engine's metrics were one query away.
  • The throughput gap was overstated. First called unmeasurable; the terminal result record carries totals, so it is blind in flight and measurable at promotion.
  • The messaging mechanism was wrong on the first reading — reported as a silently lost message, which would have sent the fix to the delivery path. It is an approval gate with no approver.
  • Two of four review findings in one diff were misattributed, judged from a diff hunk that ended before the statements did.
  • Nova's clive cost figures and imas-codex's differ by magnitude ($41–$162 against a $21.59 median over 619 rows) because the samples differ. Both are imputed. The direction is the only load-bearing part.
  • Nova's rows carrying a scheduler leg report model turn time separately from job wait, so their tok/s is comparable with imas-codex's; rows without that separation are not.

The single sentence this trial would leave behind: a green gate is a statement about the inputs the gate was given, and on nine nodes the local lane's failures were more often the coordinator's than the model's.
