Root cause of the JT-60SA vacuum prediction mismatch

The vacuum prediction the adjudication scored is under-scaled by the driven coil's ampere-turns per ampere. Every tracking channel is off by exactly that factor: the operator is built with turns = 1 (its own docstring: “turns=1 so amp-turns = amps”), while the catalogue's drive topology declares 40 A·turn/A for each CS coil and 16 for each EF coil, and nothing on the JT-60SA path applies it. Feeding the raw MMSYS winding current straight to ForwardOperator.vacuum_prediction therefore predicts the flux and field of a one-turn coil. Multiplying each coil current by its declared A·turn/A before the prediction moves every channel that tracks from |slope| = 6–19 to |slope| = 0.4–1.0 — a reduction of 16 (EF-dominated) to ~40 (CS-dominated), the declared turn counts.

The signals themselves are sound: a free per-coil least-squares fit of every measured loop and probe against the ten coil currents reaches r ≈ 0.99 for all 44 channels, so each raw channel is a clean linear function of the drive. The mismatch is in the operator's per-coil response pattern and scale, not in the acquisition. Loop FL7 is the one loop that tracks on all three vacuum shots (r = −1.00/−0.97/−1.00) at slope ≈ −14 because its operator response is dominated by the high-turn CS coils, so its under-scale is the largest of any loop.

Check 1 — MMSYS and MDAC time bases (E100595)

Both time bases are 4 kHz and uniform; the only differences are a 0.2 ms origin and the record length. MDAC fully covers the MMSYS window, so np.interp of a raw channel onto the MMSYS base is a 0.2 ms resample with no extrapolation. This check is not discriminating for the mismatch — it gives the same (clean) result under either hypothesis, which is why it rules the time base out rather than in.

Check 2 — loop pre-drive baseline and drift (E100595)

The records are already mid-discharge at t=0: CS1 holds ~2978 A at the first sample, decays to ~100 A by t≈3.8 s and to the noise floor by t≈10 s, so there is no flat pre-pulse window inside the cached record. The meaningful zero is the tail (coils off), where every loop returns to ~0. Baselines below are the median of the first 0.5 s; drift is the full span of the record. No integrator drift stands out as a cause: every loop's span is dominated by the initial flux and settles to ~0, and FL7's large negative baseline is carried by its large signal, not by a ramp.

Check 3 — drive topology A·turn/A against the MMSYS units

imas_ambix/data/machine_maps/jt-60sa.json declares, per circuit, Σ(turns × current_weight × direction) over its series elements: 40 A·turn/A for each of CS1–CS4 and 16 A·turn/A for each of EF1–EF6. The MMSYS channel that feeds that circuit is in amperes (unit 'A'), not ampere-turns, so the physical drive of each CS coil is 40 × the channel value and each EF coil 16 × it.

The adjudication passed these weights into CircuitDrive, but build_operator never uses ampere_turns_per_ampere: in classify_circuits/_declared_classes the field appears only in a flag message, and G_pf is built from the filaments' xmult with the module's turns = 1 assumption. For a machine whose geometry normalises Σxmult = 1 independently of the winding's turn count (as the JT-60SA SELENE deck does), the turn count is the missing factor. The operator's alternative assembly path, assemble_pf_currents, applies only a kA→A factor of 1000 (assuming kA·turn raw units); it is not on the adjudication's path, and applying 1000 would over-scale by ~60×, so it is not the correction either.

Check 4 — why loop 7 alone tracks, and at what factor

On all three vacuum shots FL7 is the only loop whose measured flux tracks the prediction (r −1.000 / −0.967 / −0.999) and its slope is −13.86 / −14.28 / −13.55. The magnitude is the under-scale seen through the drive: FL7's operator response is dominated by the high-turn CS coils, so dividing its raw slope by the imposed correction (~36–40, effectively the 40-turn CS factor diluted by its EF terms) brings it to ≈ −0.4, while EF-dominated probes sit at ≈ 1 after their 16-fold correction. Loops 20–26 track only on E100595 (r ≈ −0.98) and not on E100579/E100642, consistent with a second, position/axis-dependent residual rather than a common scale. FL7's identity is also the loop most consistently matched by the flux-loop PID channels in the E101154 join, so its declared geometry is the one the measurement agrees with.

Cause, and what stays inconclusive

Cause the checks support: the vacuum forward prediction omits the declared ampere-turns-per-ampere, so it under-predicts each channel by the driven coil's turn count (40 CS, 16 EF). This is the single factor that accounts for the whole shortfall in slope.

Inconclusive: after the A·turn/A correction the slopes sit at |0.4–1.0|, not cleanly at 1, and loops 1–14 (and FL20–26 off E100595) still fail to track. A residual ~2× scale and a per-channel position/axis inconsistency remain, and this node does not separate a declared-position error from a probe-axis/angle-convention error. The probe angles read from the emitted store are the corrected tangent angles (≈90° down at the outboard probe, ≈5° over the top), so an unrotated wall-normal angle is ruled out as the sole cause.

Negative control: the time-base check (Check 1) was expected to be discriminating and is not — both bases are 4 kHz and align to 0.2 ms, so it returns the same clean result under the unit hypothesis and under a time-base hypothesis. The discriminating checks are the per-coil least-squares fit (r ≈ 0.99 for all 44 channels) and the A·turn/A reweighting, whose per-channel reduction tracks the coil mix (16 for EF-dominated, ~40 for CS-dominated) rather than being constant.
