What the roll and its compaction got wrong

The recorder compacts its open record on a cadence, renames that record to the UTC day segment the day just ended, and closes the segment under its new name. The incremental pass read the rename as a new source: it reset the byte offset and the emitted-through bound to zero and compacted the whole segment again. Every window the cadence pass had already written was written a second time, so the minute tier gained a duplicate row at each roll and a reader of that tier counted those windows twice. Measured on a replica of the recorder's ordering, the minute tier after the roll held ['23:58', '23:58', '23:59'] before the repair and ['23:58', '23:59'] after it, and both tiers are now byte-identical to one full compaction of the same rows.

The same pass read the whole source before deciding where to resume, so its cost grew with the record rather than with what had arrived since the last pass. It now reads from the watermark offset to the end. Measured by counting the bytes read from the source during a second pass: the pass read the file's whole length before the repair and size - offset after it.

The name-to-job-id reader existed twice, once in the index and once beside the recorder, and only the index's was taught that a rolled segment carries the day it covers before its suffix. The recorder's copy therefore resolved deepseek-v4-1-flash-1280470.20261015.jsonl to no job at all. There is one reader again, re-exported by the recorder, and it resolves that name to 1280470.

The tier ladder was built by trimming a .jsonl suffix and then the roll day, which left a rotation count or a compression inside the stem: slug-9.jsonl.1 mapped to slug-9.jsonl.1.minute.jsonl rather than to the slug-9.minute.jsonl.1 it mapped to before the roll existed. A name is now split at its .jsonl marker, so those suffixes stay on the tier name while a day segment resolves to its job's single ladder, which is what keeps one serve's tiers one series across a roll.
