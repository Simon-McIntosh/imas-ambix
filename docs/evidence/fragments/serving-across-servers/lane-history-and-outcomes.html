The live watch document gained two blocks beside the unchanged lane, live and callers ones, both cut from the same single bounded read of the router's request record that the callers block already used.

  • history — thirty one-minute bins ending at the moment of the build. Each bin carries occupancy (the same time-averaged held-requests quantity the callers block publishes, so a bin is read against the lane's own width), requests, and the wait percentiles gate_wait_p50_s and gate_wait_p95_s over the requests admitted inside that bin, interpolated between order statistics and null for a bin that admitted nobody.
  • outcomes — a five-minute count of completed, aborted, failed and gate_expired, counted by when each request ended. The four figures partition the window: a request answered by the router after its gate wait ran out is counted as an expiry and not also as a failure.

Both the callers block and these two are built by lane_callers.record_blocks, which reads the record's tail once over the longest window (the half hour the strip needs) and returns all three levels. live_panel calls it instead of reading per block, so one poll of the document walks the growing record once rather than three times.

The record carries no field naming a gate expiry. It is read from the three facts that hold together on exactly those rows and on no others: the row's upstream is the router's self-answered sentinel ((router)), its status is failed, and its gate_wait_s is positive. A refusal the router answers before choosing an engine is self-answered with no gate wait, and a caller that leaves while its request waits or uploads records as aborted — so neither is counted as an expiry. A dedicated field on the expiry row would be cleaner than this inference, and §4's per-engine expiry row is where it would naturally live; this node's write scope does not include the router.

A fixture with a known schedule pins the arithmetic. Row A arrived 600 s before the build, waited 10 s and relayed for 300 s, so it fills five whole bins and ten seconds of the sixth; B spans −1500 s to −1380 s and C spans −1440 s to −1360 s, so one bin holds two requests at once and the next holds C for twenty seconds; four one-second rows admitted in another bin waited 1, 3, 4 and 8 s. The occupancy the strip reports, in order, is

and the fourth of those bins reports p50 3.5 s and p95 7.4 s (4 + 0.85 × (8 − 4)). The negative control shifts every bin edge by one minute: the occupancy list then reads 0 where 0.17 was, and the test fails on that element.
