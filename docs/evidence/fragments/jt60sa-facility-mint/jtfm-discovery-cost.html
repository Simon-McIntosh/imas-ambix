Bundle discovery adds about 0.2 % to the MAST map-range test

Node jtfm-discovery-cost · date 2026-10-07 · plan jt60sa-facility-mint §2 · base f1f0a5b0 (detached copy, PYTHONPATH pointing at it) · head 3d54ae6f (this worktree, packages/imas-alambic/src on PYTHONPATH, IMAS_ALAMBIC_MAP_PATH unset) · node 98dci4-clu-2018, job 1277272

At base the test reaches MAST through imas_ambix.data.machine_map's module-level PACKAGED_MACHINE_MAP_ROOT; at head the same call (load_packaged_machine_map("mast")) routes through imas_alambic.machine_map.discover_bundles, which reads the installed imas_alambic.bundles entry point and, with the environment variable unset, resolves ambix's bundle from the entry-point target. The measured question is whether that indirection made tests/data/test_machine_map.py::test_every_mast_map_range_is_one_geometry_transition slower.

Head-minus-base wall time

Each arm ran at least three times, serially and alone, with --timeout=900 on the same node. All runs passed (EXIT=0). One head sample (160.0 s) is an I/O-contention outlier: the test body is dominated by reading the MAST level-2 mirror and building geometry transitions, head samples otherwise sit at 100.4–112.1 s.

Share of head time spent in discovery

A cProfile of one head run (total 135.996 s under the profiler, test 132.80 s) reports, from packages/imas-alambic/src/imas_alambic/machine_map.py:182:

  • discover_bundles: 2 calls, cumulative 0.276 s (tottime 0.0001 s) — 0.20 % of the head run.
  • importlib.metadata.entry_points (the module-level function, importlib/metadata/__init__.py:999): 5 calls, cumulative 0.4735 s; two of those calls are the ones discover_bundles makes. The entry-point scan, not the directory walk, is the whole discovery cost.

Discovery is a single sub-second entry-point scan at the start of the test; the remaining ~99.8 % is the test body, which is unchanged between the arms. Bundle discovery did not meaningfully slow the MAST map-range test.
