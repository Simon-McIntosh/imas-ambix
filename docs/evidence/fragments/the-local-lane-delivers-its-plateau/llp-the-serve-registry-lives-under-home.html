Landed: the serve registry lives under home, and an empty publish no longer erases a document

  • imas_ambix/agent/registry.py — registration_directory() resolves $XDG_STATE_HOME/ambix/serve-registry, defaulting to ~/.local/state/ambix/serve-registry, and takes no site base: one spelling for the serve script that writes records, the router that publishes from them, and the CLI that reads them back. publish_registrations() keeps the existing endpoint document when the registry holds no registration while a serve job is running, and returns the reason (PublishResult.reason) for the caller to print. --released-job-id names an allocation whose record this publish is entitled to find absent.
  • imas_ambix/agent/profile.py — SiteConfig.serve_registry_dir is the site-facing spelling, delegating to the resolver so the two cannot drift.
  • imas_ambix/agent/slurm.py — the generated serve script's _REGISTRY_DIR is the home directory, its exit-trap republish passes --released-job-id "$SLURM_JOB_ID", and the generated router script's publish_address names --directory explicitly rather than relying on the job's environment.

Why the released-id clause exists. A serve removes its own record and then republishes, and at that moment its allocation is still listed as running. Without the clause the guard would read a legitimate final shutdown as a lost registry and keep a document naming an unregistered allocation. The caller is the only party that knows which of the two it is doing, so the fact is passed rather than inferred.

What the guard covers that the change alone does not. The live engine (job 1294597) was launched from a script generated before this change, so its exit trap still publishes against the old base-directory registry, with no released id. It reads an empty directory while its own job is running, which is the lost-registry shape exactly: the guard keeps the document naming deepseek-v4.1-flash instead of emptying it. A regenerated launcher writes and reads the home directory from its first launch.
