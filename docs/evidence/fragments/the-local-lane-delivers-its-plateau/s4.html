The lane document's live_runs, capacity, margin and worker_slots now come from one _AdmissionLedger record. A run is live while a request of it is in service, while it waits at the gate, or while it made a request inside the working horizon the per-session share uses (WORKING_HORIZON_SECONDS, 180 s); a run that ended inside that horizon holds a slot, one that ended earlier holds none. Capacity is floor(margin * width / offered_load), the margin is read from the gate's control file and defaults to 1.5. and slots are the capacity less the live runs, zero on a paused verdict. The old capacity sized from every run in the averaging window is gone, not kept beside the new one.

Measure, the ledger. tests/test_agent_router_worker_slots.py builds fifteen live runs whose observed span is 405 s each and nine that ended 4 500 s before the snapshot, at width 12. The document reports 15 live runs, a capacity of 47 and 32 slots; by hand from the same ledger the load is 150 / 395 = 0.380 and 1.5 x 12 / 0.380 = 47.4, so the hand figure is 47 and the document is within one of it, and 47 - 15 = 32. The same file covers the margin at 1.0, 1.25 and 1.5, a paused verdict, a queueing gate, the gate wait left out of the load, the negative control that measures duty with the wait in (capacity 324 at width 12, asserted over 200), and the per-session split built from the same live map.

Measure, the simulation. tests/test_agent_lane_sim.py runs the router's own ledger over four hours on both committed think-time regimes. At the default margin of 1.5 the rule delivers 445.7 tok/s on the fed window, 100.0% of that model's fixed-pool plateau of 445.8 tok/s, and 490.3 tok/s on the starved one, 101.6% of its plateau of 482.6 tok/s; the 90th-percentile gate wait is 9.50 s and 10.50 s. Over seeds 0, 1 and 2 the same figure is 445.7, 445.4 and 444.0 tok/s on fed (99.6-100.0%) and 490.3, 492.9 and 490.9 on starved (101.6-102.1%), so the criterion does not rest on one seed's draw. The negative control, duty measured over the live span including the gate wait, is run for eight hours at margin 1.25 because the runaway is a drift, not a step, and its last-hour mean exceeds 200 workers.

Stability. All six stability runs keep every ten-minute mean after the first hour within 15% of the run's last-hour mean: the worst bucket is -12.9% on fed at margin 1.0, -10.2% on fed at 1.25, +10.1% on fed at 1.5, -13.0% on starved at 1.0, +6.0% on starved at 1.25 and +10.4% on starved at 1.5. The ramp was the capacity's own estimator: the median of the live runs' duties moved in waves of about forty minutes because run lifetimes are about 2 500 s, so the live set at any instant is one cohort whose think draws are correlated, and the starved margin-1.25 capacity read 29.0, 28.9, 28.6, 27.8, 26.9, 26.4, 25.1, 26.4, 25.5, 24.5, 26.8, 29.1 across buckets. Averaging the runs' duties with one vote each, instead of taking their median, holds the pool's ten-minute means to within 13% of the run's own level.

Why the default margin is 1.5. At margin 1.25 the same measure reads 463.0, 466.7 and 477.4 tok/s on the starved window over seeds 0, 1 and 2, which is 95.9%, 96.7% and 98.9% of its 482.6 tok/s plateau, and 438.1, 436.9 and 433.9 on fed, 97.3% to 98.3%. The shortfall is the share of time the lane has fewer than twelve requests in service, and it is a variance effect the mean cannot size: the mean of the live runs' duties sizes the pool to 27.6-28.1 workers while a fixed pool of 32 is what reaches 482.6 tok/s, so a pool offering 1.25 x 12 = 15 requests on average leaves the lane short of its width part of the time. At 1.5 the pool settles at 33.3 and the lane at its plateau, with the wait inside the bound on both windows. No threshold in the measurements was changed to reach this: the 97% share, the 15 s wait bound, the 15% stability band, the four-hour run and the eight-hour control arm are the section's own.
