The lane document's live_runs, capacity, margin and worker_slots now come from one _AdmissionLedger record. A run is live while a request of it is in service, while it waits at the gate, or while it made a request inside the working horizon the per-session share uses (WORKING_HORIZON_SECONDS, 180 s); a run that ended inside that horizon holds a slot, one that ended earlier holds none. Capacity is floor(margin * width / offered_load), the margin is read from the gate's control file and defaults to 1.25, and slots are the capacity less the live runs, zero on a paused verdict. The old capacity sized from every run in the averaging window is gone, not kept beside the new one.

Measure, the ledger. tests/test_agent_router_worker_slots.py builds fifteen live runs whose observed span is 405 s each and nine that ended 4 500 s before the snapshot, at width 12 and margin 1.25. The document reports 15 live runs, a capacity of 39 and 24 slots; by hand from the same ledger the load is 150 / 395 = 0.380 and 1.25 x 12 / 0.380 = 39.47, so the hand figure is 39 and the document is within one of it, and 39 - 15 = 1.25 x 12 / 0.380 rounded down. The same file covers the margin at 1.0, 1.25 and 1.5, a paused verdict, a queueing gate, the gate wait left out of the load, the negative control that measures duty with the wait in (capacity 270 at width 12, asserted over 200), and the per-session split built from the same live map.

Measure, the simulation. tests/test_agent_lane_sim.py runs the router's own ledger over four hours on both committed think-time regimes. At margin 1.25 the rule delivers 438.1 tok/s on the fed window, 98.3% of that model's fixed-pool plateau of 445.8 tok/s, and 463.0 tok/s on the starved one, 95.9% of its plateau of 482.6 tok/s; the 90th-percentile gate wait is 4.75 s and 4.00 s. The negative control, duty measured over the live span including the gate wait, is run for eight hours because the runaway is a drift, not a step, and its last-hour mean exceeds 200 workers.

Stability is met; delivery is not. All six stability runs keep every ten-minute mean after the first hour within 15% of the run's last-hour mean: the worst bucket is -12.9% on fed at margin 1.0, -10.2% on fed at 1.25, +10.1% on fed at 1.5, -13.0% on starved at 1.0, +6.0% on starved at 1.25 and +10.4% on starved at 1.5. The ramp was the capacity's own estimator: the median of the live runs' duties moved in waves of about forty minutes because run lifetimes are about 2 500 s, so the live set at any instant is one cohort whose think draws are correlated, and the starved margin-1.25 capacity read 29.0, 28.9, 28.6, 27.8, 26.9, 26.4, 25.1, 26.4, 25.5, 24.5, 26.8, 29.1 across buckets. Averaging the runs' duties with one vote each, instead of taking their median, holds the pool's ten-minute means to within 13% of the run's own level, where the pool fills toward the capacity the record publishes.

The delivery criterion is missed by one point. Starved at margin 1.25 delivers 463.0 tok/s against a 97% bar of 468.15, which is 95.9% of the 482.6 tok/s plateau. The shortfall is the share of time the lane has fewer than twelve requests in service, and it is a variance effect the mean cannot size: the pool settles at 27.6-28.1 over three seeds (tok/s 463.0, 466.7, 477.4, or 95.9%, 96.7% and 98.9%), while a fixed pool of 32 reaches 482.6. The evidence for the smallest change the endpoint can take: at margin 1.5 the same rule delivers 490.3 tok/s on starved (101.6% of the plateau) and 445.7 on fed (100.0%), with last-bucket deviations of +10.4% and +10.1% and gate waits of 10.5 s and 9.5 s, all inside the section's own bounds.
