§1 — Objective and thesis

The capability. A person watching a replayed MAST shot changes a poloidal-field coil current at time t and, with no perceptible delay, sees the rbb camera stream respond: the plasma column moves, the boundary reshapes, the divertor legs swing, and the shot continues from the edited state. The response is not a learned guess about what coils do; it is the Grad–Shafranov (GS) force balance, solved by nova, rendered into the camera view.

Why physics-carried. The learned actuator-conditioned camera world model (playable-plasma-wm-v0, camera-dynamics-wm-v0) produced a coil-edit dream that moved the plasma on one shot (18502, P6-upper edit) but was not cross-shot robust, and the control-conditioning diagnosis found the model largely ignoring the actuator plan. The command → geometry sensitivity is exactly what GS force balance gives for free and exactly what a learned latent must be coaxed into. So the split is: nova carries the physics transfer (coil currents, plasma current and flux-function profiles in; the full solved flux map with its plasma-relevant topology labels out), and ambix learns only the rendering (the labelled flux map plus a short frame history in; the next rbb frame out).

The transfer object is the whole labelled flux map, not a boundary curve. What crosses from nova to the decoder is ψ(R,Z) on the full solve grid and its normalised form, together with the topology nova reads from it: the O-point (magnetic axis), the primary X-point and any secondary X-point, the LCFS, the strike points where the separatrix legs meet the wall, and the domain labels the solve resolves on its unstructured hex mesh — core, private flux region, wall shadow, and open field everywhere else outside the LCFS — plus the nested interior flux surfaces. The scrape-off layer is deliberately not a label: it is thinner than a hex cell and the mesh cannot resolve it, so the decoder learns edge emission from ψN and the boundary position, not from a class the physics cannot supply. The camera sees emission from all of these regions (divertor legs and strike points are the brightest features in the rbb view), so a decoder given only the LCFS would be asked to hallucinate the rest. Given the labelled map it renders what the physics determines and learns only brightness. The map arrives on nova's hex mesh; any rasterisation or camera projection is ambix's job and happens after the boundary, never inside nova. The decoder never sees a coil current, so it cannot learn to ignore one.

What "physics transfer" buys, concretely. The same decoder renders any equilibrium nova can solve. A coil edit the corpus never contained still produces a physically consistent frame, because the geometry it is asked to render came from force balance rather than from interpolating the training distribution of actuator vectors. This is the mechanism by which the demo becomes cross-shot robust, and it is the mechanism that later carries to a different machine: a new device changes the nova machine description, not the rendering model's relationship to flux geometry.

§3 — The physics channel: nova as the equilibrium engine

What nova already provides (handover from the nova S19 coordinator, 2026-09-02, main f0a785e7). Free-boundary forward solve on a cached 33×33 MAST grid with 37 wall nodes; coil and circuit currents enter as external_current; profiles are p′(ψN) and FF′(ψN) as domain-profile functions, so "fixed flux functions, varying coil currents" is exactly the supported construction. Why a current change costs anything at all. Coils do not move; only their currents change. The vacuum flux they produce is linear in the current vector — one precomputed Green's matrix times the currents, a matmul at microsecond cost — and nova's operator already holds it in that form. Two things cost more. First, a JAX tracing artefact: at the current pin the current vector is a field on the operator, so the jitted solve bakes it in as a program constant and a new value is a new program with a 340–390 s compile. Nothing physical is being recomputed; making the current vector a traced argument removes this cost entirely and is the first demand below. Second, the plasma response: when the vacuum field moves, the plasma current redistributes to restore force balance, and that is the nonlinear GS solve. Warm-started from the previous edit it is a handful of Newton–Krylov trips at ~0.8 ms each, which is the physical floor of an interactive edit and the reason the loop is milliseconds rather than microseconds.

What this plan asks of nova, recorded on nova:forward-solve-api as a consumer-facing section. (i) external_current as a traced argument of the jitted solve with static geometry, so a coil edit hits the compilation cache instead of recompiling; measured warm per-edit cost for one member, target tens of milliseconds. (ii) A warm-start policy seeding each edit from the previous edit's converged state, with convergence measured across a coil sweep. (iii) The full topology read carried in the solve receipt at no extra compile: O-point, primary and secondary X-points, LCFS, strike points, and the per-cell domain labels the authority resolves on the hex mesh (core, private flux, wall shadow; no scrape-off-layer class), so the consumer never re-derives topology from ψ. These three are nova's forward-solve-api §5, which the lead has made critical path; this plan depends on that section specifically and waits for it rather than building a precomputed-sweep substitute. Until (i) lands, the interactive lane in §5 runs on precomputed sweeps; the moment it lands the same wrapper switches to live solves.

Ambix-side deliverable. A thin equilibrium-engine wrapper in imas_ambix/physics/ that, for a MAST shot and time, assembles the nova operator from the cached carrier, takes the measured plasma current and the shot's efm p′/FF′ at that time as frozen flux functions, and exposes one call: coil-current vector in, labelled flux map out — ψ on the full hex mesh, ψN, the O-point, primary and secondary X-points, LCFS polygon, strike points, the per-cell domain labels (core, private flux, wall shadow, open), the interior flux-surface contours at a fixed set of ψN levels, the topology class, and the nova receipt. The wrapper also owns the resampling of the hex-mesh map onto whatever frame the decoder conditions on (§4), so the mesh geometry stays a nova detail. It enables the persistent compilation cache and pins the nova revision at or above 588578a0. It runs under JAX_PLATFORMS=cuda,cpu set inside the sbatch body. Done when, on the four converging frozen-six shots, the wrapper reproduces the efm LCFS at measured coil currents within the §6 physics tolerance and logs per-call cost.

§4 — The flux-conditioned camera decoder

Conditioning representation (decision flux-conditioning). The decoder must see the labelled flux map in a form that generalises across coil edits. In every candidate the map is carried whole: ψN as a continuous channel and the domain labels (core, private flux, wall shadow, open) as categorical channels, with the O-point, X-points and strike points as marked positions. The candidates differ in the frame: (a) the channels resampled from the hex mesh onto a regular (R,Z) raster as an image stack; (b) a compact geometry vector (LCFS Fourier moments, O/X-point and strike-point coordinates, class) — the lossy option, kept only as an auxiliary token; (c) the channel stack projected into rbb camera pixel space through the camera calibration, so the conditioning lives in the same frame as the output. Recommendation: (c) if §2a finds a usable rbb calibration, because a projected boundary aligns spatially with the emission it explains and lets a small decoder learn a local mapping; otherwise (a) with (b) as an auxiliary token. The decision is locked on §2a's finding, not before.

Training labels (decision label-source). The decoder needs (flux geometry, frame) pairs at scale. Efm EFIT flux surfaces exist for the whole camera corpus; nova solves at measured currents exist only where a machine carrier exists (six frozen shots today) and cost ~0.4 s per slice until warm-started batching lands. Recommendation: train on efm geometry for coverage, and make the §6 physics gate prove that nova-solved geometry at measured currents lies inside the decoder's training tolerance, so a nova-rendered edit is in-distribution for the decoder. Re-label from nova once traced external_current makes bulk solving cheap; the plan records the swap as its own node, not as a rewrite.

Architecture. Reuse the frozen rbb tokeniser and decoder from the playable demo. Replace the actuator-conditioning path with a flux-geometry conditioning path, and keep the short frame-history bottleneck so the decoder carries emission texture (filaments, divertor brightness) that flux geometry does not determine. Train with the same context-corruption and sampling recipe the playable demo validated; classifier-free guidance on the flux condition so its weight is tunable at play time. Done when the held-out decoder gate in §6 passes.

§4b — The camera topology labeller: a robust judge

The problem with the current judge. Every camera-side position measurement so far has been a brightness centroid of the frame. It moves with divertor brightness, reflections, filaments and gas-puff emission as much as with the plasma column, so a "the plasma moved" verdict built on it is not robust, and a gate scored on it can pass or fail for the wrong reason. This plan builds the label capability from the camera images themselves and makes it the judge.

What it is. A camera topology labeller: an rbb frame (or a short frame window) in; the plasma-relevant topology labels out — the O-point position projected into the camera frame, the primary X-point, the strike points, the LCFS trace as seen by the camera, and the topology class (limited, single-null upper/lower, connected or disconnected double-null). It is trained on frames paired with the equilibrium labels already on disk (efm for coverage, nova where the carrier exists), projected through the rbb camera calibration where §2a finds one and regressed as image-frame positions where it does not. It is a separate model from the decoder, trained on a disjoint shot split, and never sees a coil current or a decoded frame during training: it is the referee, and a referee that shares weights with the player is no referee.

Its own gate comes first. Before it judges anything, the labeller is scored on held-out real frames against the equilibrium labels: O-point and X-point position error, strike-point error, class accuracy, and a robustness sweep under the confounders that break centroids (brightness scaling, divertor-only bright frames, puff emission, frames from a different campaign). The counterfactual gate in §6 is then scored with this labeller, and the old brightness centroid is reported beside it once, to show what it would have concluded.

Reuse beyond this plan. The same labeller is an observation model: it reads topology from video for any shot, including shots without magnetics, which is the observation the magnetics-free recovery plans lack. Done when the labeller gate in §6 passes and the counterfactual gate is scored with it.

§5 — The interaction loop

One lane: live solves. The drive surface from the playable demo (edit a waveform, watch the stream) is kept. Behind it, each coil edit is one warm-started nova solve on the resident operator, with the coil currents passed as a traced argument so the compilation cache hits, followed by one resample of the labelled map and one decode. The operator, the persisted carrier and the compiled program are loaded once per session, never in the loop. Target edit-to-frame latency is set in §6. This lane exists only once nova's forward-solve-api §5 has landed; the lead's decision (2026-09-02) is to wait for it rather than ship a precomputed-sweep substitute, because §5 is critical path and scheduled for immediate implementation. A solve that refuses (target-current band exceeded returns NaN by design) or stagnates is shown as a refusal on the surface, with the last good frame held, never papered over with an interpolated geometry.

Shot selection (decision demo-shot). The shot must be in the nova frozen-six carrier and in the converging set, and have a bright, dynamic rbb video. 18502, the shot the old demo used, is not in the carrier. §2a reports the candidates; the lead locks one.

Done when a recorded screen capture shows a coil edit at time t followed by the camera stream moving within the §6 latency budget, on the locked shot, with the per-edit solve wall time and cache-hit status logged beside each frame.

§7 — Risks and honesty

  • Emission is not flux. The rbb camera sees edge and divertor emission, filaments and reflections; flux geometry fixes where the boundary is, not how bright it is. The frame-history path carries texture; the counterfactual gate scores position, not brightness, so the claim stays inside what physics determines.
  • Frozen profiles under a coil edit. Holding p′, FF′ and plasma current fixed while a coil moves is the honest "what does this coil do" experiment, not a prediction of the shot's future; the plan says so in the surface and defers profile response to the transport plans.
  • Convergence on marginal arms. Stagnation at ~5×10-4 residual on some pure diverted arms is under active repair in nova (solver-convergence-regression). The demo shot is chosen from the converging set and a refused or stagnated solve falls back to the sweep lane visibly.
  • Interaction depends on nova's forward-solve-api §5. Traced coil currents, warm start and the receipt topology are the whole interaction path, and the lead has chosen to wait for them rather than build a substitute. The dependency is declared at section granularity so the cross-project roadmap shows exactly what this plan is waiting on; the physics wrapper, the labeller and the decoder do not wait and are built in parallel.
  • The judge can be wrong in the same direction as the player. The labeller and the decoder are trained on disjoint shot splits with no shared weights and the labeller never sees decoded frames in training; its own held-out gate is measured and reported before it scores the counterfactual gate.
  • Label mismatch. Training on efm and playing on nova is safe only if the physics gate holds; if it fails, the decoder is retrained on nova labels and the gate is re-run, and that is recorded as a result rather than hidden.
