§1 — Objective and thesis

The capability. A person watching a replayed MAST shot changes a poloidal-field coil current at time t and, with no perceptible delay, sees the rbb camera stream respond: the plasma column moves, the boundary reshapes, the divertor legs swing, and the shot continues from the edited state. The response is not a learned guess about what coils do; it is the Grad–Shafranov (GS) force balance, solved by nova, rendered into the camera view.

Why physics-carried. The learned actuator-conditioned camera world model (playable-plasma-wm-v0, camera-dynamics-wm-v0) produced a coil-edit dream that moved the plasma on one shot (18502, P6-upper edit) but was not cross-shot robust, and the control-conditioning diagnosis found the model largely ignoring the actuator plan. The command → geometry sensitivity is exactly what GS force balance gives for free and exactly what a learned latent must be coaxed into. So the split is: nova carries the physics transfer (coil currents, plasma current and flux-function profiles in; the full solved flux map with its plasma-relevant topology labels out), and ambix learns only the rendering (the labelled flux map plus a short frame history in; the next rbb frame out).

The transfer object is the whole labelled flux map, not a boundary curve. What crosses from nova to the decoder is ψ(R,Z) on the full solve grid and its normalised form, together with the topology nova reads from it: the O-point (magnetic axis), the primary X-point and any secondary X-point, the LCFS, the strike points where the separatrix legs meet the wall, and the domain labels the solve resolves on its unstructured hex mesh — core, private flux region, wall shadow, and open field everywhere else outside the LCFS — plus the nested interior flux surfaces. The scrape-off layer is deliberately not a label: it is thinner than a hex cell and the mesh cannot resolve it, so the decoder learns edge emission from ψN and the boundary position, not from a class the physics cannot supply. The camera sees emission from all of these regions (divertor legs and strike points are the brightest features in the rbb view), so a decoder given only the LCFS would be asked to hallucinate the rest. Given the labelled map it renders what the physics determines and learns only brightness. The solve lives on nova's hex mesh, but the map the decoder sees is evaluated by nova on a rectangular (R,Z) grid built once for the Biot operation: coil circuits and plasma cell currents map onto that grid through their Green's functions directly, so ψ on the grid is exact and nothing is interpolated in ambix (decision flux-conditioning). The decoder never sees a coil current, so it cannot learn to ignore one.

What "physics transfer" buys, concretely. The same decoder renders any equilibrium nova can solve. A coil edit the corpus never contained still produces a physically consistent frame, because the geometry it is asked to render came from force balance rather than from interpolating the training distribution of actuator vectors. This is the mechanism by which the demo becomes cross-shot robust, and it is the mechanism that later carries to a different machine: a new device changes the nova machine description, not the rendering model's relationship to flux geometry.

Why a current change costs anything at all. Coils do not move; only their currents change. The vacuum flux they produce is linear in the current vector — one precomputed Green's matrix times the currents, a matmul at microsecond cost — and nova's operator already holds it in that form. Two things cost more. First, a JAX tracing artefact: on the production MAST route the full 101-circuit current vector (13 active, 88 passive) is held by a prescribed-current field on the operator whose flux is computed with no argument, so the jitted solve bakes it in as a program constant and a new value is a new program with a 340–390 s compile. The generic solve does accept a traced current argument, but on this route it would be added on top of the frozen field rather than replace it, double-counting the active coils (§2b survey). Nothing physical is being recomputed; making the complete prescribed-current vector a traced argument with replacement semantics removes this cost entirely and is the first demand below. Second, the plasma response: when the vacuum field moves, the plasma current redistributes to restore force balance, and that is the nonlinear GS solve. Warm-started from the previous edit it is a handful of Newton–Krylov trips at ~0.8 ms each, which is the physical floor of an interactive edit and the reason the loop is milliseconds rather than microseconds.

§4 — The flux-conditioned camera decoder

Conditioning representation (decision flux-conditioning). The decoder must see the labelled flux map in a form that generalises across coil edits. In every candidate the map is carried whole: ψN as a continuous channel and the domain labels (core, private flux, wall shadow, open) as categorical channels, with the O-point, X-points and strike points as marked positions. The candidates differ in the frame: (a) the channels resampled from the hex mesh onto a regular (R,Z) raster as an image stack; (b) a compact geometry vector (LCFS Fourier moments, O/X-point and strike-point coordinates, class) — the lossy option, kept only as an auxiliary token; (c) the channel stack projected into rbb camera pixel space through the camera calibration, so the conditioning lives in the same frame as the output. Recommendation: (c) if §2a finds a usable rbb calibration, because a projected boundary aligns spatially with the emission it explains and lets a small decoder learn a local mapping; otherwise (a) with (b) as an auxiliary token. §2a finding (2026-09-02): no rbb pixel-to-(R,Z) or line-of-sight calibration exists anywhere in code or data; the camera-boundary contract records that the corpus carries no camera matrix. Option (c) is therefore blocked until a calibration is imported and validated (§4c), and the scout recommends (a) with (b) as auxiliary. The existing scalar conditioning projection broadcasts one vector over every cell and cannot carry a ψ grid, so the spatial adapter is new machinery whether (a) or (c) is chosen.

§4b — The camera topology labeller: a robust judge

The problem with the current judge. Every camera-side position measurement so far has been a brightness centroid of the frame. It moves with divertor brightness, reflections, filaments and gas-puff emission as much as with the plasma column, so a "the plasma moved" verdict built on it is not robust, and a gate scored on it can pass or fail for the wrong reason. This plan builds the label capability from the camera images themselves and makes it the judge.

What it is. A camera topology labeller: an rbb frame (or a short frame window) in; the plasma-relevant topology labels out — the O-point position projected into the camera frame, the primary X-point, the strike points, the LCFS trace as seen by the camera, and the topology class (limited, single-null upper/lower, connected or disconnected double-null). It is trained on frames paired with the equilibrium labels already on disk (efm for coverage, nova where the carrier exists), projected through the rbb camera calibration where §2a finds one and regressed as image-frame positions where it does not. It is a separate model from the decoder, trained on a disjoint shot split, and never sees a coil current or a decoded frame during training: it is the referee, and a referee that shares weights with the player is no referee.

Its own gate comes first. Before it judges anything, the labeller is scored on held-out real frames against the equilibrium labels: O-point and X-point position error, strike-point error, class accuracy, and a robustness sweep under the confounders that break centroids (brightness scaling, divertor-only bright frames, puff emission, frames from a different campaign). The counterfactual gate in §6 is then scored with this labeller, and the old brightness centroid is reported beside it once, to show what it would have concluded.

Reuse beyond this plan. The same labeller is an observation model: it reads topology from video for any shot, including shots without magnetics, which is the observation the magnetics-free recovery plans lack. Done when the labeller gate in §6 passes and the counterfactual gate is scored with it.

§7 — Risks and honesty

  • Emission is not flux. The rbb camera sees edge and divertor emission, filaments and reflections; flux geometry fixes where the boundary is, not how bright it is. The frame-history path carries texture; the counterfactual gate scores position, not brightness, so the claim stays inside what physics determines.
  • Frozen profiles under a coil edit. Holding p′, FF′ and plasma current fixed while a coil moves is the honest "what does this coil do" experiment, not a prediction of the shot's future; the plan says so in the surface and defers profile response to the transport plans.
  • Convergence on marginal arms. Stagnation at ~5×10-4 residual on some pure diverted arms is under active repair in nova (solver-convergence-regression). The demo shot is chosen from the converging set and a refused or stagnated solve falls back to the sweep lane visibly.
  • Interaction depends on nova's forward-solve-api §5. Traced coil currents, warm start and the receipt topology are the whole interaction path, and the lead has chosen to wait for them rather than build a substitute. The dependency is declared at section granularity so the cross-project roadmap shows exactly what this plan is waiting on; the physics wrapper, the labeller and the decoder do not wait and are built in parallel.
  • The judge can be wrong in the same direction as the player. The labeller and the decoder are trained on disjoint shot splits with no shared weights and the labeller never sees decoded frames in training; its own held-out gate is measured and reported before it scores the counterfactual gate.
  • Label mismatch. Training on efm and playing on nova is safe only if the physics gate holds; if it fails, the decoder is retrained on nova labels and the gate is re-run, and that is recorded as a result rather than hidden.

How does the decoder see the solved flux geometry?

Nova can define a rectangular grid once for the Biot operation, so the coil circuits and the plasma cell currents map onto that grid through their Green's functions exactly; ambix never resamples the hex mesh and no interpolation loss enters. Camera projection stays blocked on the missing rbb calibration (§4c); a geometry vector alone would discard the spatial map. Where do the decoder's training flux geometries come from?

The decoder is trained on exactly the object it will be given at play time. No EFIT labels enter training; efm remains the held-out referee for the physics-fidelity gate only. Coverage comes from §3b, a bulk nova solve corpus batched on the H200 once traced currents land, not from the six frozen reference slices.

What happens to p′, FF′ and plasma current when a coil is edited?

Fixed flux functions with varying coil currents is the construction nova supports today and is the honest "what does this coil do" experiment; profile response belongs to the transport plans and is out of scope here.

Which camera stream is rendered?

The rbb corpus, tokeniser and decoder already exist and were validated by the playable demo; adding cameras is a later plan.

Matches nova's locked decision conditioned-slices-for-demo. A conditioned slice inherits one EFIT scalar (the current-centroid height), which is admissible for a demo but not for evidence scored against the EFIT referee; the flag keeps the ablation possible and the firewall enforceable on data.

absent-wall-contract

The MAST wall did not move between shots; nova's era carrier holds one 37-node wall for every shot. Reading it from a per-shot store is a storage convenience, so an absent group is a storage gap, not a missing measurement, provided identity across the cohort is measured rather than assumed. absent-flux-contract

Masking is never imputation, and a whole-shot absence of the flux map is the limiting case of a masked component. A pass that stops at each defective shot discovers the corpus one defect per hour; a pass that excludes and counts finishes once and reports the census of defects, which is the evidence the section needs.

vq-route

The lfqgan module imports its training losses at module top, so an in-process import needs lpips, timm, kornia, torchvision and taming-transformers, none of which the project declares or should declare for a frozen decoder it never trains.
