Reading note (research, read-only). This audit inventories the FAIR-MAST level-1 and level-2 mirror on GPFS (/work/projects/imas_gpu/mast/) and compares it against what the camera world model actually trains on, to answer one question the lead posed directly: are we massively under-using the available data? The short answer is yes, but not where the instinct pointed — the biggest unused assets are earlier campaigns and the nine signal streams already tokenised in the v2 substrate that the camera model fuses none of; raw infrared imaging, by contrast, barely exists (25 shots). All counts are from a full directory scan of 17,111 L1 and 11,573 L2 shot Zarr stores plus the curated training manifest; no frames were decoded and nothing was modified. Acronyms expanded on first use. The current full-shot re-train (job 1221297) continues on the high-bandwidth subset in parallel; this audit informs the playable-plasma sprint's next corpus.

§1 — Campaign coverage: all of MAST is on disk; we tokenise the late half

What exists. The L1 mirror holds 17,111 shots spanning shot numbers 11,695–30,473 — the full run of the last MAST campaigns plus MAST-U. L2 (the IMAS-ingested tier) holds 11,573 shots (11,766–30,471); this is the "last five campaigns" figure quoted in the FAIR-MAST literature. So L1 is the superset and the right source for camera work — and indeed every camera lives only at L1 (the cameras were never ingested into L2, confirmed by the source mapping).

What we tokenise. The camera token corpus has 11,012 shot directories but only 9,527 carry an rbb store (id range 15,085–30,473). The gap at the bottom is the binding fact: all 3,378 L1 shots below 15,085 are untokenised, which is the whole of campaign M5 (~1,850 shots) and the early part of M6. A further 2,721 shots inside the token id range have an L1 store but no token store (no rbb, or unreadable). The current full-shot run then keeps 2,857 of the 6,499 plasma-present excited windows (the 0.25 s-span filter, §4), so the model sees ~17% of the L1 population and ~30% of the rbb population.

Is this a real gap? Partly. M5/M6 are genuinely missing and recoverable. But the loss is not uniform — the late campaigns (M9, MU01, MU02+) are well covered (2,200–2,400 tokenised each), and those carry the richest auxiliary diagnostics (Thomson, CXRS, MSE all ramp up in the later campaigns, §3). The early-campaign backfill buys more shots but not necessarily more diagnostic diversity. The cheaper, higher-diversity win is fusing the signals we have already encoded for the shots we already tokenise (§3).

§2 — Cameras: five visible feeds tokenised, one used; IR imaging barely exists

The corpus is not rbb-only on disk. This corrects a common framing. A full scan of the camera token corpus finds five visible cameras already tokenised through Open-MAGVIT2:

  • rbb — Photron bullet camera B — 9,527 shots (tokenised + trained)
  • rco — Phantom colour camera (3-channel RGB) — 7,921 shots (tokenised, unused)
  • rgb — visible bremsstrahlung camera — 7,506 shots (tokenised, unused)
  • rgc — visible bremsstrahlung camera — 7,433 shots (tokenised, unused)
  • rba — Photron bullet camera A — 6,155 shots (tokenised, unused)

Every training, encode, and bench path hard-defaults to camera="rbb" (camdyn/dataset.py:DEFAULT_CAMERA, the curated manifest, all sbatch headers), so the other four are dead weight on disk. rbc (Photron bullet C, 2,982 shots) is not even tokenised. Adding rba/rco as extra views is the cheapest camera expansion — the tokens already exist for ~6,000–8,000 shots each; only a multi-camera dataset/loader change is needed, not a re-encode. The catch: rco/rgb/rgc only exist from M8 on (Fig 2a), so they widen views, not campaign coverage.

Infrared imaging is essentially absent. The lead asked specifically about divertor/edge IR. The picture is sobering:

  • rir — "Medium wavelength infrared camera" (an SBF125 InSb 320×256 focal-plane array) — is a genuine raw IR image stream (CLASS=IMAGE, data: (T, 256, 320)), and it would tokenise through the same Open-MAGVIT2 path with only a grayscale-replicate + resize. But it exists for 25 shots, and many of those are read out in degenerate strip modes ((T, 8, 320), (T, 32, 320)) — only a handful are full 256-row frames.
  • rit — "Long wavelength infrared camera" — 14 shots, almost all strip-mode ((T, 24, 256)).
  • ait — labelled "IR Camera" but stored as analysed divertor heat-flux products (etot_isp/osp, lampow* at the inner/outer strike points; 1-D time series + a 331-point profile), not image frames. 2,846 shots. This is valuable divertor physics but it is a signal, not something the camera tokenizer can ingest.

Verdict on IR: raw IR imaging is not worth pursuing — 25 shots cannot train or even meaningfully condition a world model, and the codebook-decision gate that was scaffolded for it (the v0-rbb-25shot bench config references a "§12.5 IR codebook decision, MAE ≤ 2× rbb") is moot at this sample size. If divertor thermography matters for the physics, the route is to ingest ait heat-flux as a conditioning signal (2,846 shots), not as camera frames. (Note: a distinct air source, 2,607 shots, also appears in the L1 inventory and is likely further IR divertor data — worth a look only if IR physics becomes a target.)

§3 — Signals: 4 of ~30 used; nine streams already tokenised and unfused

What the camera WM uses. The conditioning vector reads four L1 sources: amc (PF/CS coil currents + Ip), anb (NBI), aga (gas injection), ane (interferometer density). That is 4 of ~30 diagnostic sources present at L1, and 4 of 13 IMAS groups at L2.

What is already tokenised but unfused. The most actionable finding of the whole audit: the v2 multimodal substrate (mast-tokens/v2/signals_hf/, 15,361 shot directories) has already encoded nine signal streams, with 7,257 shots carrying the full 11-stream set:

  • xma — fast (MHz) magnetics — 12,045 shots — Mirnov/MHD activity
  • xim — visible spectrometer — 14,184 shots
  • xsx — soft X-ray (fast, multi-chord) — 13,002 shots — emission-profile / MHD
  • interferometer, pf_active, gas_injection, summary, pulse_schedule, soft_x_rays (L2-derived) — 10k–11.5k shots each

These were built for the multimodal-tokenisation substrate and the signal world-model track, but the camera world model conditions on none of them. Fusing even xma + xsx + pf_active into the camera model is a zero-encode change — the tokens are on disk for the same shots we already train on. This is almost certainly higher-value than any new camera or campaign: MHD/SXR structure is exactly the fast, spatially-structured information a "playable plasma" needs to respond to actuator changes, and it is sitting unused.

What is raw and untouched. Beyond the tokenised set, the L1 mirror carries roughly twenty more diagnostic sources the model has never seen, several of them high-value for plasma state:

  • amb — Mirnov / fluctuation coils — 15,149 shots (fast magnetics detail beyond amc)
  • abm — bolometer (radiated power) — 12,166 shots
  • atm / ayc / aye — Thomson scattering (core / combined / edge: Te, ne profiles) — 6,122 / 4,665 / 3,792 shots
  • act — charge-exchange recombination spectroscopy (ion temperature, rotation) — 7,991 shots
  • ams — motional Stark effect (current-profile constraint) — 4,882 shots
  • anu / ant — neutron camera / yield — 13,342 / 5,068 shots
  • ahx — hard X-ray — 10,048 shots; alp — Langmuir probes — 323 shots

Thomson (Te/ne), CXRS (Ti), and MSE (j-profile) are the canonical interior-state diagnostics; they are all present for thousands of shots and entirely outside the world model. They are the natural second wave after the already-tokenised v2 streams are fused.

§4 — Low-frame-rate: the filter drops the opposite of what was feared

The premise needs correcting. The lead asked to include low-fps feeds, on the worry that the n_frames=64 full-shot filter drops them. The data says the opposite. The current run does not gate on n_frames=64 directly; it gates on recorded span ≥ 0.25 s (n_frames / fps), keeping 2,857 of 6,499 windows. Because frame rate and recording duration are inversely correlated on MAST (the fast Photron triggers fire in short bursts around events; the slow feeds roll for the whole shot), the span filter throws away the HIGH-fps shots: 91% of the >3.5 kHz band and 77% of the 1.6–3.5 kHz band are dropped, versus 0% below 50 Hz and ~0.4% below 200 Hz. The pure n_frames<64 cut, by contrast, would drop only 128 windows (2%).

So what is actually being lost, and how to include it. Two distinct populations are under-served:

  • High-fps short bursts (the 3,642 dropped). These are not junk — they are the fast-camera recordings of fast events, just with <0.25 s of footage. The full-shot trainer time-subsamples each window to a fixed 64 frames over the plasma duration, so a short high-fps clip simply yields fewer usable frames. To include them, lower the span floor (e.g. accept ≥0.05 s) or add a burst track that keeps the native high-fps frames as a short window rather than forcing the 0.25 s horizon. The risk to watch is the dilution the excitation-corpus characterisation warned about — so gate these on the existing excitation score, not on raw inclusion.
  • Genuinely low-fps feeds (already mostly kept). Almost all <500 Hz feeds already pass. If the goal is to capture more of them — including the M5/M6 shots, which skew toward the older, slower cameras — the lever is a per-shot n_frames floor (e.g. min(16, available)) with the time base carried natively (the dataset already attaches per-frame dt and a valid_frames mask, camdyn/dataset.py), so a 12-frame 50 Hz shot trains as a 12-frame window rather than being padded or dropped. The native-Δt conditioning the loader already supports is exactly the mechanism that lets one model span 30 Hz–167 kHz without a separate low-fps track.

Recommendation: keep one model, drop the span floor to admit short high-fps bursts (excitation-gated), and add a per-shot frame floor so slow feeds train at their native length. A separate low-fps track is unnecessary given the native-Δt machinery.

§5 — Highest-value expansions, ranked

  1. Fuse the v2 signal streams into the camera model (no new encode). xma + xsx + pf_active + interferometer are tokenised for the shots we already train on. This adds fast magnetics, MHD/SXR structure, and density to the conditioning the model currently lacks — the information a driveable plasma must react to. Cost: a multimodal dataset/loader change. Likely the single biggest win per unit effort.
  2. Add rba / rco as extra camera views (no new encode). ~6,000–8,000 shots each are already tokenised; widens viewpoint diversity. Cost: a multi-camera loader. Caveat: late-campaign only.
  3. Backfill M5/M6 rbb (cheap re-encode). ~3,400 L1 shots below id 15,085 are untokenised. Encoding them is a single ≤1 GPU-hour in-process job (the encoder exists and is resume-safe). Buys shot count, not diagnostic diversity.
  4. Admit short high-fps bursts + low-fps floor (corpus-filter change). Re-admits the 3,642 currently-dropped windows under an excitation gate; see §4.
  5. Tokenise interior diagnostics (Thomson/CXRS/MSE, larger effort). Thousands of shots carry Te/ne/Ti/j-profile data, all raw. The natural second wave after (1).
  6. Raw IR imaging — do not pursue. 25 shots. If divertor physics matters, ingest the ait heat-flux signal (2,846 shots) instead.

How much more data is available? By shot count, the model trains on ~2,857 of 17,111 L1 shots — a ~6× headroom in raw shots before any new diagnostics. By information content the gap is larger still: of the camera feeds, four of five tokenised cameras are unused; of the signal diagnostics, the conditioning vector reads 4 of ~30 sources while 9 fully-tokenised streams sit unfused. The corpus is not the bottleneck — consumption is. The cheapest large step is to consume what is already encoded.
