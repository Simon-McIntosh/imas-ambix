This study reads one measurement, already taken. It re-runs nothing. Every figure below comes from docs/figures/local-lane-occupancy/occupancy_stats.json, which the measurement node produced from four serve logs and the router receipt stream, and from that measurement's five figures. It records why the router's global in-flight gate was lowered from width 16 to width 12 on 2026-10-08.

§1 — The question

The local DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash lane admits requests through a single global first-in-first-out gate at the router, up to a width W. W had stood at 16. The question this study answers is whether the lane's aggregate decode throughput still rises at 16, or whether it has already plateaued, so that a lower W would give each request more tokens per second without much aggregate cost.

The answer is that aggregate decode plateaus from about 10 running. Aggregate throughput at 12 and 16 sits within a few percent of its maximum, while the per-request rate falls as 1/width with no extra break. The width-16 bin is additionally biased high by the gate itself: at a full gate of 16 no new request is admitted, so its profile is not the profile a steady width of 16 would deliver. On that evidence the gate was set to 12 at the lead's request.

§5 — The decision: width 16 to 12

The coordinator lowered the router gate from 16 to 12 on 2026-10-08T05:06:36Z (imas-ambix agent width 12; the router confirmed an effective width of 12 at 05:07:03Z), on the lead's request. The reasoning, with the figures behind each point:

  1. Aggregate decode plateaus from about 10 running. Decode-step medians are 405 tok/s at width 10, 406 at 12 and 411 at 15. Receipt-derived totals (per-request rate times width) are 444, 438 and 456 tok/s. The run's aggregate knee is 10 (95% interval 10–16). Nothing in the aggregate rate rewards a width above about 10.
  2. The width-16 bin is biased high by the gate itself. At a full gate of 16 no new request is admitted, so no new prefill runs there. Its prefill-active second fraction is 0.25 against 0.84 at width 15, and its uncached prefill is 827 tok/s against 2,600 at 15. The prefill that width 16 avoids is displaced into the bins below it. So the bin's 424 by decode steps and 472 by receipts overstate what a steady width of 16 delivers — the aggregate figure at 16 is not a clean reading of the operating point.
  3. Per request falls as 1/width with no extra break. The per-request decode rate is 33.9 tok/s at width 12 and 26.5 at 16 — a 28% gain at 12 that a proportional-sharing decay predicts, not an additional cliff. The two-line per-request fit finds no breakpoint below 32.
  4. The lead's prefill hypothesis is confirmed in its mechanism, but not as cache thrash. Of 944 fall-over episodes, 763 coincide with an uncached-prefill surge, and the episode traces show decode falling to zero while uncached prefill spikes to 20–30k tok/s. But the cache-hit fraction holds at about 0.97 up to width 32 and collapses only at 33 and above, where KV sits past 0.80. The fall-overs below that are prefill interference: a cold or new context stalls decode for every request already running. Their frequency grows with width — the prefill-active fraction is 0.64 at width 10 and 0.84 at 15 — so a lower gate admits fewer of them.
  5. Why the run's own recommendation to keep 16 was overridden. The measurement's rule, quoted from its recommendation.basis: "lower gate only if aggregate knee upper CI, per-request break upper CI, and thrash onset all lie below 16; otherwise stay at 16." The rule is conservative to a fault here. It required three boundaries below 16, and one of them — the per-request break — does not exist: the fit returns no breakpoint, so that condition could never be satisfied and the rule always voted to stay. The second, the aggregate-knee upper interval, reached 16 because the biased width-16 bin pulled the spread open. Read with those two artefacts removed, the aggregate knee (10), the per-request decay (no break, rate simply falls as 1/width) and the thrash onset (33) all argue for a lower gate; only the rule's construction kept it at 16.
  6. The expected trade at 12. Roughly 4–7% of aggregate decode is given up, in return for about 28% more tokens per second per request and fewer prefill stalls. The occupancy histogram shows the lane spends most of its time at 1–5 running (61% of observed seconds), so the gate binds only in busy periods: about 13% of observed time sat at widths 12–16. The common case is unchanged; the busy case is cheaper per request.
  7. Causal limit. Every bin here is observational, pooling different hours, contexts and accept lengths — the bins above width 16 come only from the older jobs 1273253 and 1276246, with a median context near 97k tokens, while the 1–16 range pools all four jobs including the live job 1280470 at about 125k. So the behaviour above 16 is partly a context effect as much as a width effect. A controlled trial is the follow-on: alternate the gate between 12 and 16 in fixed blocks under live load and compare the counter-derived aggregate against the receipt per-request rate.

To revert: imas-ambix agent width 16. The gate reads its width from an operator-editable file, so no router restart is needed.

§6 — What would falsify this

The width-12 choice rests on the aggregate plateau holding under a controlled alternation. If a block-comparison trial shows the counter-derived aggregate at 16 materially above 12 while the per-request rate does not recover, the plateau is an artefact of pooled hours and the gate should return to 16. The revert above is the action that measurement would call for.
