§1 — What the challenge is

The Sophelio Fusion Equilibrium Challenge asks entrants to predict the EFIT poloidal-flux map ψ(R,Z) on a 65×65 grid, plus q95 and βN, at every labeled timeslice of held-out tokamak shots — from actuators and Thomson scattering only. No magnetic diagnostics are provided on either machine; the field names carrying a magnetics_ prefix hold coil currents and plasma current, not sensor measurements. Two sub-challenges: intra-machine accuracy on DIII-D, and zero-shot cross-machine transfer to MAST, for which no MAST training labels exist by design. The transfer score is the ratio of the MAST score to the DIII-D score, so an entry that memorises DIII-D and collapses on MAST is scored exactly as such.

All facts in §1–§4 were verified against the challenge site on 2026-08-11 (landing page, FAQ, and data pages). §5 lists claims that circulated in the design conversation but are not yet verified against the primary source; they are checked at ingest before anything depends on them.

§3 — Scoring and timeline (verified)

The DIII-D score is S = 0.55·R²_ψ + 0.15·R²_(q95,βN) + 0.10·(1 − D_LCFS) + 0.20·Consistency, where Consistency scores scalars the referee derives from the submitted map (including internal inductance ℓi). The transfer score is G = S(MAST)/S(DIII-D); entries predicting only DIII-D score zero on the transfer challenge. Development phase runs Jul 27 – Oct 18, 2026 with a public-test leaderboard (5 submissions/day, 100 total); a blind final runs Oct 19 – 26 with exactly 3 submissions on a hidden leaderboard. Prizes are $500 per challenge plus named co-authorship on a lessons-learned paper and invited NeurIPS talks (Dec 2026). A compute-light badge recognises models training end-to-end in under two hours on a single GPU or CPU.

§4 — Rules on external material (verified)

The FAQ states, verbatim: "Yes — other public tokamak archives, OMFIT-produced equilibrium tables, and publicly available pre-trained vision or scientific foundation models are all permitted, provided you disclose them in your methods report." External machine descriptions, archives and codes are therefore legal with disclosure. This is the fact our data doctrine (§6) is calibrated against — the doctrine is deliberately stricter than the rules.

§5 — Reported but not yet verified

The following circulated in the design conversation and are plausible but unconfirmed against the primary source; each is checked during ingest and this section updated: the total labeled frame count (~1.55 M diverted DIII-D frames); per-machine retention fractions after limited-frame deletion (≈88% DIII-D / ≈66% MAST); the count of distinct per-shot DIII-D Thomson layouts (≈22); the claim that submitted-map post-processing (e.g. 3-decimal rounding) measurably destroys MAST Consistency; and the pedigree of the ground-truth EFITs (magnetics-only vs MSE-constrained — the site says only that labels are "kept exactly as EFIT produced it"). The pedigree question is load-bearing for how hard the interior of extracted profiles can be trusted, so it is a first-week empirical probe in the entry plan (vacuum-region Δ*ψ receipts and basis-structure analysis of the labels themselves).

§6 — Our objective and data doctrine

The objective is not the prize. We enter this challenge to strengthen and externally validate the ITER data-analysis pipeline — the Nova/Ambix seam — in advance of ITER operations. The challenge deletes the magnetics design matrix and asks whether state estimation over an exact forward operator can replace it: a harsher version of the bet the seam architecture already makes, and the only funded, adversarially-scored cross-machine transfer benchmark available before first plasma. Compliance with the challenge rules meets this goal; a leaderboard placement bought by weakening our own evidence standards would not.

FAIR-MAST statement (deliverable disclosure). The FAIR-MAST archive is publicly accessible, including MAST magnetics and MAST EFIT reconstructions for the challenge-era campaigns. We could legally train against it with disclosure. We deliberately do not. Training on public MAST data and then claiming zero-shot transfer would make the transfer number scientifically meaningless to us — and the transfer number is the reason we are entering. Our permitted uses of FAIR-MAST material are limited to machine-description geometry (conductor and vessel descriptions, already validated in nova) and signal semantics (channel meanings, units, layouts). No parameter of the submitted pipeline may be derived from MAST data that is not discoverable from what the competition itself provides. MAST EFIT remains firewalled exactly as before: never read during development, never a tuning target, never a comparison surface.

Zero-shot, demonstrated for ourselves. Because we also want the transfer claim to be true in our own accounting, the MAST side of the pipeline is validated once, late, under a sealed protocol: the pipeline is frozen, a single rehearsal pass runs against nova's own MAST equilibrium reconstructions (built from magnetics outside the challenge, independent of MAST EFIT), results are recorded as evidence, and no parameter feedback flows back. If the rehearsal exposes a defect, the fix is made on DIII-D evidence and the seal is honestly reported as broken in the methods report.

The derivative-level firewall. EFIT-labeled maps are consumed only at the level of derived, physically-typed coefficients: applying the Δ* operator to a labeled map gives the toroidal current density, and per-surface radial separation (jφ = R·p′ + FF′/μ₀R) recovers the flux functions p′(ψ) and FF′(ψ) — strong signals for tuning transport-coefficient closures expressed in dimensionless, machine-portable forms (Sauter/Redl-class corrections), which is what transfers between machines. ψN-at-chord values may calibrate observation-model detectors. What is banned is map-level supervision of the physics stack with free-form absorbers. The only map-level EFIT contact in the entire entry is the quarantined referee-emission layer at the very end of the pipeline (convention projection plus a DIII-D-gated learned residual), which exists because the competition's scoring function is EFIT agreement — our own skill bar remains >1 against raw reality, which is the reason the firewall exists at all.

§7 — Architecture mapping and implementation path

The entry maps one-to-one onto the standing seam authority: nova owns every deterministic forward (free-boundary GS, limiter mode, passive circuits, response kernels including the extraction operator) and produces measurement-space operators it never fits; ambix owns measurement models, the windowed smoother, closure calibration, the amortizer, and the quarantined emission layer. The two entry plans are nova:diiid-forward-onboarding and magnetics-free-equilibrium-recovery (this project).
