Cosmos-Tokenizer evaluation (FSQ alternative)

Status: Active — research landed 2026-05-28; this is a contingency plan that activates only if the transient-fidelity v1 work fails to close the rFID gate on plasma data.

§1 — Why this exists

Production fine-tune plasma-decoder-finetune (job 1209004) is plateauing at rFID ≈ 12.5 vs a < 5 gate — a 22 % improvement from baseline 16.25 but far from the target. Continuing diagnosis points at a structural issue: the Open-MAGVIT2 LFQ codebook with 218 = 262 144 entries is only 2.5 % utilised on plasma data. The decoder is doing its best, but the encoder is mapping the narrow plasma distribution onto ~6 500 tokens out of 262 144 — a tiny corner of the vocabulary that may be too coarse to encode the visual structure faithfully.

Two architectural fixes that could close the gap:

  1. Better use of the existing codebook — train the encoder on plasma data (currently we freeze it). But this requires re-tokenising the 4.02 B-token corpus.
  2. Switch to a codebook architecture that doesn't have dead entries by construction. This is what NVIDIA Cosmos-Tokenizer offers via FSQ (Finite Scalar Quantization).

The user (2026-05-28) asked specifically about Cosmos-Tokenizer. This plan evaluates whether adopting it would actually solve the plateau — and the answer is not obviously yes. The research below shows Cosmos has a worse image rFID on natural-image benchmarks than Open-MAGVIT2. Its advantage on plasma would come from FSQ structural-utilisation guarantees, not raw fidelity. This plan therefore treats Cosmos as a candidate to evaluate alongside MGVQ and MambaVideo, not a foregone conclusion.

§2 — Cosmos-Tokenizer at a glance

Project status

  • Original repo github.com/NVIDIA/Cosmos-Tokenizer was archived 2025-02-11 (read-only). Development moved to NVIDIA/Cosmos v1 monorepo and again to nvidia-cosmos org (latest cosmos-predict2.5 v1.5.1 dated 2026-04-03).
  • Tokenizer weights have not been retrained — Cosmos 1.0 (Jan 2025) remains the latest released version. 16 + months frozen. No "Cosmos-Tokenizer 2.0" exists.
  • Tokenizer training code lives in cosmos-predict1; post-training guide is intact.

Variants released (12 total)

VariantTypeSpatialTemporalCodebookParams
CI8x8 / CI16x16Continuous Image8× / 16×—continuous 16-D latent~64 M
DI8x8Discrete Image8×—FSQ 64 K (6 levels × 6 dims)~64-79 M
DI16x16Discrete Image16×—FSQ 64 K~64-79 M
CV4x8x8, CV8x8x8, CV8x16x16Continuous Video8 / 16×4 / 8×continuous~105 M
DV4x8x8, DV8x8x8, DV8x16x16Discrete Video8 / 16×4 / 8×FSQ 64 K~105 M+

For our 256 × 256 plasma frames, DI16x16 is the natural drop-in: 256 / 16 = 16, so the encoder produces a 16 × 16 grid of int16 indices — identical spatial layout to our current Open-MAGVIT2 tokens. The DV variants would be a bigger pipeline change (temporal compression) but worth considering for a future world-model architecture iteration.

Weights, license, integration

  • Weights: HuggingFace at nvidia/Cosmos-1.0-Tokenizer-{variant}, distributed as encoder.jit + decoder.jit TorchScript files (~250-320 MB per image tokenizer file).
  • Model license: NVIDIA Open Model License — commercial use permitted, derivatives permitted, no output ownership claim. Friendlier than Llama-style RAIL. Code license: Apache 2.0.
  • Inference: BF16 on Ampere / Hopper; A100 / H200 sufficient. Compatible with our existing torch 2.5.1 + cu124 magvit2 venv.

§3 — The honest comparison (Cosmos is NOT obviously better)

NVIDIA's own reported numbers and the Open-MAGVIT2 paper's head-to-head comparison:

Benchmark (16× compression) Open-MAGVIT2 (LFQ, 262K) Cosmos DI16x16 (FSQ, 64K) Verdict
ImageNet-1k rFID (zero-shot) 1.17 [1] ~4.57 (their measurement) / 1.67 (ours) Open-MAGVIT2 wins
MS-COCO rFID (zero-shot) 6.76 [1] 11.97 / 12.25 (NVIDIA's own card) [2] Open-MAGVIT2 wins
Codebook utilisation (plasma rbb, our data) 2.5 % of 262 K (~6.5 K active) [3] ~100 % of 64 K by construction Cosmos wins structurally
Video reconstruction (DAVIS) n/a (image-only) CV4x8x8: PSNR 35.28, rFVD 15.93 Cosmos has video; not directly comparable

[1] Open-MAGVIT2 paper arXiv:2409.04410, Table 3. [2] HF nvidia/Cosmos-Tokenizer-DI16x16. [3] Measured by us at corpus encode time (imas_ambix/data/stream_encode.py).

Critical reading: on standard image benchmarks, Cosmos's discrete tokenizer is worse than what we already use. If our plasma plateau came from "the tokenizer is fundamentally bad on natural images," Cosmos would not help. The plateau is a domain-shift problem (plasma frames ≠ ImageNet frames the tokenizer was trained on). Swapping tokenizers without re-training the encoder on plasma data would just trade one out-of-distribution tokenizer for another.

The legitimate case for Cosmos is structural codebook utilisation. FSQ quantises each of 6 dimensions into 6 levels independently — every region of the latent space corresponds to a code, so there are no dead codes by construction. Our 2.5 %-utilisation problem cannot recur with FSQ. Whether that's the actual cause of our plateau is the empirical question this plan answers.

§4 — Alternatives also worth considering

Cosmos is not the SOTA as of mid-2026. A short survey suggests three other 2025+ tokenizers that should be on the comparison table before committing:

CandidateReported edgeSourceNote
MGVQ (2025) Multi-group quantization; claims SOTA on standard image benchmarks arXiv:2507.07997 Newer than Cosmos; published 2025-07. Need to inspect weights availability.
MambaVideo Tokenizer (2025) +0.9 dB PSNR over MAGVIT-v2 at equal compression; long-range sequence inductive bias arXiv:2507.04559 Mamba-based; potentially plasma-friendly for the temporal regime.
FlowMo (mode-seeking diffusion AE) Reports SOTA ImageNet rFID arXiv:2503.11056 Diffusion-based tokenisation; not a 1:1 drop-in.
MAR (Masked Autoregressive) Bypasses tokenisation entirely; continuous-latent AR (option-of-last-resort if "stop tokenizing" is on the table) Largest architectural change of all options.

The 2026-05-28 research did not deep-dive these — they're flagged for a focused literature review if we get to the decision point of switching tokenizers.

§5 — Implementation phases

Structured as an evaluation, not a migration. We don't adopt Cosmos unless empirical evidence on plasma data justifies it.

Phase 0 — Trigger conditions (do not start unless)

This plan activates ONLY when:

  1. transient-fidelity v1 has shipped (Phases 2-5 done) and the resulting rFID still does NOT clear the gate, OR
  2. The stratified rFID re-bench shows rfid_transient ≥ 15 while quiescent is low, indicating the codebook fundamentally cannot represent transient structure (i.e., adding loss-weight to transient frames won't help because the tokens aren't expressive enough), OR
  3. The user explicitly decides to evaluate the alternative ahead of v1 results.

Phase 1 — Set up Cosmos inference (no training)

  1. Clone cosmos-predict1 at the version that matches our torch 2.5.1. Verify the .jit files load on a single H200 in our magvit2 venv.
  2. Download DI16x16 weights from HF nvidia/Cosmos-Tokenizer-DI16x16 to /work/projects/imas_gpu/mast-tokens/cosmos/v1/DI16x16/.
  3. Wrap with a thin CosmosDI16x16Tokenizer class implementing our existing FrameTokenizer protocol (see imas_ambix/tokenizer/frames.py) so the bench harness works unchanged.
  4. Smoke-test on a single shot (the same 1-shot smoke we use for Open-MAGVIT2 in imas_ambix/bench/configs/_smoke-1shot.yaml).

Phase 2 — Head-to-head bench (zero-shot, no fine-tune)

  1. Run our existing 100-shot rbb bench (v0-rbb-100shot.yaml) with the Cosmos DI16x16 factory. This measures baseline Cosmos rFID on plasma, no domain adaptation.
  2. Compare to our Open-MAGVIT2 baseline (rFID 16.25 from job 1208918) and the post-finetune result (rFID ~12.5 from 1209004).
  3. Use the rfid_stratified metric (already landed 2026-05-28) — we need per-class numbers to attribute any difference.
  4. Report codebook utilisation: expect ~100 % for FSQ vs 2.5 % for LFQ. Confirms the structural claim.

Phase 3 — Decoder fine-tune (mirror our Open-MAGVIT2 recipe)

  1. Adapt imas_ambix/tokenizer/finetune_decoder.py to accept the Cosmos encoder/decoder split. The FSQ quantiser is parameter-free (just rounding), so freezing the encoder also freezes token IDs — same fine-tune contract as Open-MAGVIT2.
  2. Run the same 10 k-step DDP fine-tune on 4×H200 (same SLURM template from plasma-decoder-finetune).
  3. Re-bench with rfid_stratified.

Phase 4 — Encoder fine-tune (if Phase 3 still plateaus)

If decoder-only fine-tune does not clear the gate, train the encoder on plasma data. This re-tokenises the corpus (~7 h) and breaks back-compatibility with the existing token store — gated on Phase 3 results. Requires the cosmos-predict1 post-training framework (LazyDict configs, torchrun --nproc_per_node=8 default). Our 4-GPU reservation may need the single-node config adapted.

Phase 5 — MGVQ / MambaVideo comparison (if Phase 4 wins)

Only if Cosmos beats Open-MAGVIT2 on plasma after decoder fine-tune do we go through the engineering of evaluating MGVQ / MambaVideo too — at that point a 3-way comparison gives us the confidence to commit to a non-Open-MAGVIT2 pipeline.

Phase 6 — Adopt or reject decision

Lock decision frame-tokenizer-architecture in the parent tokenizers plan. If Cosmos wins, write a migration plan (plan-cosmos-corpus-encode.html) covering re-tokenisation of the 9 528-shot corpus. If Cosmos does NOT win, document the negative result here so future iterations have the evidence.

§6 — Acceptance criteria (when does Cosmos win?)

Cosmos is adopted (replacing Open-MAGVIT2 as our primary frame tokenizer) ONLY if ALL of the following hold after Phase 3:

  1. rfid_overall < 5 on the 100-shot plasma bench (the absolute gate), OR rfid_transient < 5 if we've switched to the transient-rFID gate per transient-fidelity §5.
  2. Cosmos rFID is at least 2 points lower than what Open-MAGVIT2 achieves on the same plasma corpus after its own decoder fine-tune. A tie or slight win doesn't justify the migration cost.
  3. Cosmos codebook utilisation ≥ 50 % across all 64 K entries (sanity check: FSQ should give this for free — if it doesn't, something is wrong with our integration).
  4. Cosmos token throughput at corpus encode time (frames / sec / GPU) is ≥ 80 % of Open-MAGVIT2's measured 308 fps/GPU. Slower is acceptable; much slower delays world-model training.
  5. License + dependency stack review confirms no blocker for ITER publication path.

Cosmos is rejected (we keep Open-MAGVIT2) if:

  • Phase 2 baseline rFID on plasma is > Open-MAGVIT2 baseline by > 3 points (Cosmos starts worse and never catches up).
  • Phase 3 decoder fine-tune plateaus at the same rFID as Open-MAGVIT2. The architecture isn't the limiting factor; the data / training recipe is.
  • Engineering blockers surface (JIT cross-version load failures, license conflict, etc).

§7 — Risks & unknowns

  • JIT brittleness. Cosmos ships encoder.jit / decoder.jit archives traced on a specific PyTorch version. Cross-version load failures are common. Must test load on our exact torch 2.5.1 build before designing Phase 1.
  • No plasma / scientific imaging community usage found. Zero hits on "Cosmos-Tokenizer plasma" or similar. We would be the first plasma group to evaluate this — no prior art, no community-confirmed gotchas to lean on.
  • Cosmos is frozen. 16 months no updates to the tokenizer. Any bugs we encounter, we fix ourselves; NVIDIA upstream is read-only. The active cosmos-predict2.5 is a higher-level world-model framework that reuses the 1.0 tokenizer.
  • FSQ utilisation claim is at the training distribution. Cosmos was pre-trained on natural images / video. On plasma data, FSQ still produces all 64 K codes (by construction), but the codes may be poorly aligned with plasma structure — encoder still sees an out-of-distribution input. This is the same domain-shift issue that's already biting us, just with different numerics.
  • SOTA has moved past Cosmos. Per §4, mid-2026 tokenizers (MGVQ, MambaVideo) report better numbers. Investing in Cosmos when newer alternatives exist needs justification. Phase 5 mitigates by requiring a 3-way bake-off if Cosmos becomes the lead candidate.

§8 — Out of scope

  • Cosmos video tokenizers (CV / DV variants). Switching to video tokenisation is a bigger pipeline change (token shape changes, world-model architecture changes) and is gated on a separate "should we tokenise in time" decision. Tracked in the tokenizers parent under equilibrium-2d-tokenizer and related decisions, not here.
  • Re-tokenising the entire corpus. Only triggered by Phase 6 if Cosmos wins; that work would land in a separate plan-cosmos-corpus-encode.html.
  • Multi-camera (rir / IR) experiments. Cosmos DI16x16 only takes 3-channel RGB; the IR-camera-codebook decision in the parent tokenizers plan determines whether we share or split.
  • Training a custom FSQ tokenizer from scratch. FSQ is a published technique (Mentzer et al. 2023); we could implement our own. Not in scope here — using off-the-shelf Cosmos avoids the training cost.
