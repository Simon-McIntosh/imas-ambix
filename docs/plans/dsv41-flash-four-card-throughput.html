§2 — The arithmetic that decides this before any work starts

The target is 80 tok/s with a loaded value of 50. Read per worker — which is the unit every baseline in this repository is expressed in, and what a worker actually experiences — V4.1 cannot reach it on four cards, and that follows from a measurement already taken. Loaded throughput cannot exceed unloaded throughput, and V4.1's unloaded ceiling here is 34.4 tok/s. A loaded target of 50 is above its idle rate.

§2a — What changed between authoring this plan and dispatching it

Five facts established on 2026-09-15 before any node was cut. They move the cost, the running order, and the premise, so they are recorded here rather than left in a session.

The checkpoint is already resident. /work/projects/imas_gpu/agents/deepseek-v4-1-flash holds 476 GiB of weights and imas_ambix/agent/profiles/deepseek-v4-1-flash.toml is committed beside it. The 511 GB download the decision rationale priced is already paid; the only remaining cost is lane downtime.

The committed V4.1 profile pins the wrong pool for this experiment. It carries max_total_tokens = 3485952, the smallest load-proven pool. §3's whole premise is the 5.3× pool advantage, and 3.49M against V4-Flash's 2,818,154 is 1.24× — serving at the committed value would answer nothing. §3 must serve at 14,999,808, which is the pool the landed evidence load-proved through a 32-way sweep with zero OOM. 20,000,000 starts cleanly and then dies under real load; do not reach for it.

The live V4-Flash serve is a pre-registered experiment in progress, not an idle baseline. Job 1271487 started 2026-09-15T15:40:11, forty-six seconds after the commit that disabled DSpark, and reports speculative_config=None, TP4/DP1, pool 2,818,154. It is the draft-group veto test. Note that the committed profile no longer describes it — that file now carries a TP2/DP2 replica split which this serve does not run — so the configuration to record beside the comparison numbers is the job's own, not the file's.

That test has already refuted its hypothesis, and the refutation raises this plan's value. The serve reads a 28.8% cumulative prefix hit rate. Its own pre-registration predicted 88.9–100% if the draft group were gating lookups and 17–40% if it were not; 28.8% is inside the no-veto band, and a 3.4–3.8× step cannot hide inside a cumulative figure. The drafter was not what held the hit rate down. With the veto eliminated and concurrency already ruled a floor rather than a slope, pool turnover is the leading surviving explanation for this lane — and pool size is exactly the variable §3 changes. The competing account of §3's premise died; the premise did not.

Consequently the harvest precedes the takedown, and that ordering is binding. The cumulative figure settles the direction but is the reading this plan already warns has misled four sessions; the pre-registered comparison wants a windowed per-interval distribution binned by width, and it can only be taken while job 1271487 is alive. Taking the serve down first destroys a measurement whose supporting counters were themselves recorded because they were about to become uncollectable. The lead's authority covers stopping the serve to make room for V4.1; it is not authority to discard data nobody can collect again. Harvest, then take it down.

The same harvest is what §6's last constraint asks for — it is the V4-Flash side of §3's comparison, measured on the configuration actually serving rather than on the stale bins quoted in §3's done-when. Those bins (27.0% / 10.1 tok/s and 26.3% / 6.9 tok/s) were taken with DSpark enabled and should be treated as historical once the harvest lands.

§3 — The one thing that was never measured

The retired plan's §4 was to drive real agent traffic through V4.1 with clive workers. It was closed unrun, correctly, because the serve had been taken down by then. It is the only section of that plan that did not land, and it is the only measurement that could overturn the close.

The reason it matters is specific rather than hopeful. This lane's throughput problem is not decode — it is that the prefix cache returns 25.7% where multi-turn agent traffic should approach 90%, so roughly three quarters of every prompt is recomputed, and that recomputation is what evicts the next session's prefix. Turnover is pool ÷ prefill rate: about 165 seconds on V4-Flash's 2.8M pool, which is inside a worker's think-and-tool gap, so a prefix is often gone before its own next turn arrives.

V4.1's pool is 5.3× larger. At the same prefill rate that is roughly 880 seconds of prefix survival instead of 165 — comfortably beyond an agent turn gap. If the hit rate rises accordingly, prefill volume falls, and per-worker decode under real load rises toward the idle rate rather than collapsing to a seventh of it.

So the genuine question is a trade with a measured cost and an unmeasured benefit: V4.1 gives up 2.5× on raw decode and buys 5.3× on pool. On real agent traffic, which wins? V4-Flash keeps 86.6 unloaded and delivers 6.9–10.1 loaded; if V4.1 keeps a larger fraction of its 34.4, it could deliver more under load while being far worse on paper.

Done when: real clive workers have run sustained multi-turn work against a V4.1 serve, with per-worker decode binned by concurrency and the prefill rate recorded beside it, and a windowed internal prefix-cache hit rate taken as a distribution of per-interval deltas at a stated concurrency — not a cumulative ratio, which has misled four sessions on this lane. The comparison bins are V4-Flash at 27.0% and 10.1 tok/s for 10–14 concurrent, and 26.3% and 6.9 tok/s for 15–19.

§4 — Two structural handicaps, and whether either is removable

The retired plan named three candidate causes for 34.4 tok/s against the 300–507 the vendor reports on their own stack. One has since been settled — DSpark cannot start. The other two are properties of this checkpoint on Hopper rather than settings, and they bound how much §3 can possibly recover:

  • MXFP4 experts dequantise rather than using native FP4 tensor cores, because H200 is SM90 and FP4 silicon arrived with Blackwell. Not removable on this hardware at all.
  • Engram tables gathered from host memory rather than HBM. The tables are 196.6B parameters at FP8, about 183 GiB. Four cards hold 561 GB against a 511 GB checkpoint, so they cannot be resident; eight cards at 1128 GB could. This is the concrete content of "needs eight cards", and it is the one handicap that a topology change would remove.

A third possibility is worth one cheap check rather than an assumption: the 09-14 image predates the upstream fix that sizes prefill sliding-window index rows to 128 columns instead of 1152 on a vision checkpoint served text-only. Without it, every prefill row carries nine times the index width for images that can never arrive, costing KV metadata and index bandwidth on every platform. Whether our image lacked it has not been established, only that it predates it.

Done when: it is recorded whether the serve used for §3 carries the SWA width fix, so the resulting numbers are attributable. This is a log check, not an experiment.

§6 — Constraints

  • Taking V4-Flash down stops the local lane. The lead has authorised it. Warn the peer coordinator sessions holding clive runs first; recovery is crew resume, never redispatch, and promoting a killed run destroys its resume path.
  • Lower max_num_seqs for the evaluation. Upstream reports a Triton illegal memory access in dsv4_topk under high concurrency, mitigated by max_num_seqs=256. This deployment runs 1024.
  • Do not re-run the synthetic ladder. The concurrency sweep is landed. A synthetic probe on this hardware once reached 8,479 tok/s aggregate at 1,023 concurrent and told us nothing about agent sessions, while killing a serve and four workers.
  • The V4-Flash baseline is moving. A data-parallel replica split is staged and unmeasured; a memory-fraction return to 0.85 and a smaller prefill chunk are committed and unlanded. Record which V4-Flash numbers the comparison used.

§ Decisions

Is the 80 / 50 tok/s target per worker or per card?

Locked on the plan's own argument, which needs no measurement: per worker is the unit every baseline in this repository is expressed in, it is what a worker experiences, and per-card decode is not a quantity the engine reports because a single request is served by all four cards at once. The consequence is accepted rather than worked around — the 50 tok/s loaded target is above V4.1's measured 34.4 tok/s idle ceiling, so V4.1 cannot reach the target on four cards. Section 5 already provides for this: the live comparison is against V4-Flash's loaded per-worker figures, and beating those without reaching 50 is a real outcome. Reading the target per card would let a synthetic aggregate clear a bar no worker ever experiences.

Given §2, is §3 worth the lane downtime and a 511 GB checkpoint?

The lead authorised taking the four-card DSv4 serve down to serve this variant, which settles the lane-downtime half. Two facts found while scoping move the cost and the value in the same direction. The 511 GB download is already paid: the checkpoint is resident at /work/projects/imas_gpu/agents/deepseek-v4-1-flash, 476 GiB, with a committed serve profile beside it. And the competing explanation for this lane's prefix hit rate has just been eliminated on the live serve — the draft-group veto test reads 28.8 percent, inside its own pre-registered no-veto band of 17-40 and nowhere near the 88.9-100 a gating veto predicted. Removing the drafter did not move the hit rate, so pool turnover is the leading remaining candidate, and pool size is precisely what section 3 varies.

§ Followups

Lock both decisions before any serve is touched

The target-unit decision determines whether this plan can pass at all, and the worth-the-cards decision determines whether it runs. Both are answerable from what is already recorded here — neither needs a measurement — and locking them first avoids spending lane downtime and a 511 GB download on a question whose answer is already arithmetic.

Read dsv41-flash-serve-characterisation before dispatching anything. Its §1 and §2 are landed and must not be re-measured; its §4 is the only open experiment and is this plan's §3.
