§1 — Two descriptions disagree; a measurement can settle it

Nineteen flux-loop positions differ between the level-2 nominal geometry table and the reconstruction's own silop positions, by up to 0.437 m at fl_p4l_1. Both are plausible descriptions of the same signal-proven hardware, and no amount of comparing them to each other will say which is right. A measurement will.

The method is ordinary physics used as an arbiter. In a vacuum shot there is no plasma current, so the flux threading a loop is entirely attributable to known coil currents through known coil geometry. Predict the flux at each candidate position and compare against what the loop actually measured: the candidate that predicts the measurement is where the sensor is.

§2 — Why this is not circular, and the line that keeps it so

Using a field solver to adjudicate a geometry the solver consumes invites a real objection, so the boundary matters and must hold explicitly.

The distinction the design turns on
FramingVerdict
Compare our sensor geometry against the solver's geometry Circular — a model confirming its own inputs
Predict a measured signal from each candidate position and score against the measurement Sound — the measurement is independent of both candidates

The disputed quantity is a sensor position. The trusted quantities are coil geometry and measured coil current, which are a different part of the machine description and are not in dispute. That separation is what breaks the circle, and it must be stated in the result: if coil geometry ever enters the disputed set, this method stops being an arbiter and becomes a joint inverse problem.

Nova is already a pinned dependency and its Green's functions have been cross-checked against the in-tree kernel to 1.1×10-9, so it is an independent implementation rather than a second opinion from the same code. Coil couplings use the finite-area cylinder kernel, never point filaments.

§3 — Deliverable

A verdict per disputed loop, with its margin. A loop whose two candidates predict the measurement equally well is a null and must be reported as one rather than resolved by preference — the discriminating power of the test is part of its result.

Done-when: a vacuum-shot cohort selected and its plasma-current-free condition evidenced rather than assumed; for each of the 19 disputed loops the predicted flux at both candidate positions compared against the measured signal across the cohort, with the residual for each candidate and the margin between them reported; a verdict of nominal-table, reconstruction, or null recorded per loop with the counts summing to 19; and the winning positions fed back to the catalog as declarations carrying this evidence, or the null loops explicitly left undecided.

§ Decisions

When both candidate positions predict the measurement equally well, what does the catalog declare?

The plan's §3 done-when requires 'the null loops explicitly left undecided', and §3 states that the discriminating power of the test is part of its result — asserting either candidate on a null would erase that. Declared nulls carry an explicit undecided qualification in the catalog.

§ Followups

Adjudicate the nineteen disputed loop positions on vacuum shots

Select a vacuum cohort and evidence its plasma-current-free condition. For each disputed loop, forward-predict the flux at both candidate positions from measured coil currents and compare against the measured loop signal. Report residual per candidate, margin between them, and a verdict of nominal-table, reconstruction or null. Nulls are a result, not a failure.
