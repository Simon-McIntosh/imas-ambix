§1 — What the lane is doing now

The DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash lane on 98dci4-gpu-0003 (four H200s, 512k context) was observed at 08:08 local on 2026-09-23 running 29 concurrent requests with zero queued, 217 tok/s aggregate output and 19.6 tok/s per stream, with a 133k median prompt, TTFT 1.7 s, KV pool at 78% and prefix-cache hit near 100%. The lane document published four minutes later read 31 running, 0 waiting, 79% KV occupancy and a host-tier cache at 97.5%. The engine was not refusing or queueing anything. It simply let every arrival into the running batch.

That is how the lane is configured. The engine profile (imas_ambix/agent/profiles/deepseek-v4-1-flash.toml) sets max_running_requests = 36, chosen to sit below the 4M-token pool's fill point at roughly 38–40 concurrent. The router's per-consumer admission filter was removed on 2026-09-14 (76a6a235) for good reason. It keyed consumers on host plus user-agent, so every harness on one login node shared a single six-slot bucket, and it refused 37.4% of an afternoon's requests while the engine sat idle. Since then the only bound anywhere in the path is the engine's 36. Reckon crew dispatch reads a published lane ceiling as advice, and nobody outside crew dispatch reads it at all.

The profile justifies 36 with a claim that aggregate decode is flat from width 17 to 32. A later measurement of the same lane contradicts it: aggregate output peaks near 22 concurrent at about 500 tok/s and falls to about 340 past it, while waiting stays at zero and the hit rate stays high. That is the failure the lead is describing. Past the knee, each additional stream buys no tokens and slows every other one. Nothing in the path queues work instead. Nothing tells a consumer outside crew dispatch that the lane is full, so a busy lane looks the same as a slow model.

Superseded by §3 and §6. The ~22 knee above was not reproduced. The engine's decode throughput is flat or rising to its cap of 36 on both serve logs. Median aggregate throughput and the prefix hit rate hold through KV occupancy 0.90. The collapse is a minority tail of intervals, not a width knee. This paragraph records what was believed when the plan opened; the design in §6 rests on the later measurement.

§2 — The levers, by layer

This plan holds to one principle. Admit requests, not agents, up to the width that maximises aggregate tok/s, and let every other request wait in a queue that is cheap, fair and visible. An agent is mostly between requests, executing tools. A running cap of a given width therefore serves many more agents than that, provided queued requests hold no KV and are admitted in a sensible order.

Landed — see the evidence record

The profile's flat claim holds on its stated range; the ~22 knee is not present. On the five-day job (1273253) the reader took 79,436 decode intervals with 0 unreadable, spanning 2026-09-17 22:01:09 to its cancellation at 2026-09-23 06:12:04, and 36 widths cleared the 20-interval threshold. Aggregate generation rises steeply to about width sixteen and is then flat from width 17 to 32: the per-width medians over 17–32 sit between 365.8 and 389.9 tok/s, and the median at the observed operating band — widths 29–31, 317 intervals pooled — is 379.1 tok/s against a plateau median of 378.5, a ratio of 1.0.

The maximum-median width is the cap, not an interior knee. The largest per-width median anywhere in 1–32 is 389.9 tok/s at width 22, a mild local high inside a 380–390 band; widths 23–32 then sit at 366–384, not at 340. The overall maximum is 436.7 tok/s at width 36 (24 intervals, 95% interval 418.5–448.9) — the engine's running cap, so no wider bin exists. The ratio of the operating band to that peak is 0.868 (and 0.866 on the live replacement job 1276246, whose peak sits at width 35, n=77). That peak is reported as measured, not as a knee: every interval at width 29 and above was logged in one mid-run window (2026-09-18 11:xx to 2026-09-21 21:xx), and the width-36 bin carries a higher speculative accept length (3.82) than the operating band (3.65), so its advantage tracks that window's acceptance rather than width alone. No width above 36 was ever logged, so no knee below the cap appears — which is the profile's justification for 36, and the engine log does not contradict it.

The two claims disagree because they measure different objects. The ~22 knee is a window mean: its ordinate is an aggregate output rate averaged over a whole window and its abscissa a mean concurrency (one such window reads "23.05, peak 34"), so a single bin mixes many instantaneous widths and its "knee" is where per-client rate collapses, not where the engine's aggregate rate stops rising. The decode-log instrument pairs a per-step rate with the exact instantaneous width; it separates widths cleanly and finds a flat 17–32 band with no interior peak. Claim B's level (~500 tok/s) is not reproduced by any width on this lane.

§6 — The fix

What binds is KV occupancy, not a width knee. The engine's own decode throughput is flat or rising all the way to its cap of 36 on both serve jobs (§3: 379 tok/s at 29–31 against 437 at 36). The collapse tracks occupancy instead. At 32 running, with KV at 0.83 and a 99% prefix hit rate, the lane ran above 500 tok/s. At 33 running, with KV at 0.88 and a 94% hit rate, it fell to 98 tok/s. Running requests pinned the pool, other agents' prefixes spilled to a full host tier, and they came back as recomputed prefill. So the bound targets the largest width that keeps occupancy below that cliff. It is not a fixed concurrency number, and it is not the lane document's 0.5 occupancy target, which halves the ceiling on a guess.

The design, in three layers, each measured.

  1. Router gate, live now (§7). One global FIFO bound, with no identity and a live-editable width. Waiters hold no KV. Past the wait it answers 529 with Retry-After: 5. It is deployed at width 20, and its width is raised toward the occupancy cliff as the width writer below lands.
  2. Width writer. A small process sets width in router-gate.json from pool tokens × an occupancy target ÷ a slowly moving context estimate. It clamps between a floor of 16 and the engine cap, and treats a missing lane field as a state rather than as no-news. The target is measured, not assumed: bin aggregate output by KV occupancy and prefix hit rate from the decode log, and take the highest occupancy that holds the hit rate. That target is expected near 0.8, which gives about 30 at a 100k context.
  3. Engine backstops at the next relaunch. The engine's waiting queue holds no KV and no prefix-cache lock (§4), so it is a safe second queue. Set schedule_policy=lpm so waiting requests are admitted longest-cached-prefix first. It falls back to fcfs above 128 waiting. Also bound max_queued_requests; its 503 surfaces as an SSE error event on the streaming path, and it binds a caller who bypasses the router. Keep max_running_requests at 36 as the hard ceiling, because the gate carries the live width. Priority cannot reach the engine through /v1/messages (§4), so any interactive-first ordering would have to live at the router.

The durable gain is capacity (§8). The layers above ration a 4M pool that is replicated on every rank. DP attention shards it for a projected 16M tokens, raising the cliff from about 40 to about 162 resident contexts at a 99k context. It is admissible only with session-affine dispatch. That relaunch carries the engine backstops and the loopback bind (§9) in one drain.

Width follows context: an automatic width mode in the gate. The router already publishes the lane document and holds each capacity reading, so the width writer lives in the gate itself, not in a separate process. The gate file gains "width": "auto", with optional occupancy_target (default 0.90, the measured §6 boundary), width_floor (default 16) and width_cap (default 36, the engine's running cap). In auto mode the gate takes floor(pool tokens × target ÷ mean context), where mean context is a slow estimate: an exponential moving average with a time constant of at least ten minutes, fed only by fresh, measured readings. It clamps the result to [floor, cap]. If there is no reading, or the lane shape changes at zero running, the gate holds its last width, or the floor before any reading, and never reads a missing key as zero. An integer width keeps working exactly as it does now. The snapshot published in lane.json gains the effective width and the estimate behind it.

Explain the collapse tail. The median holds through occupancy 0.90, but a minority of intervals collapses: 14 of 397 at occupancy 0.80 or above fell below 200 tok/s on job 1276246. Those episodes, not the median, are what the lead saw. Compare each collapsed interval in both decode logs against intervals at matched occupancy and width that held throughput. For each, report the concurrent prefill load (new tokens and prefill batch lines in the window), the prefix hit rate, HiCache load and backup activity, retraction or eviction lines, and the running width. Name the feature, if any, that separates the two groups. The measurement runs on all_debug and only reads the lane.

Two submitter defects the deploy exposed. generate_router_script and generate_lane_refresher_script in imas_ambix/agent/slurm.py hardcode time_limit="0", which SLURM reads as UNLIMITED. An unlimited job cannot backfill past a higher-priority pending job, and one such job, requesting 1500G, stranded the router for 12 minutes. Those two scripts need a finite limit. The engine and interactive allocations stay unlimited, because everything else waits on them. Separately, a router fallback "pinned" to an old revision imported the main checkout, because the job's working directory precedes PYTHONPATH. A rollback must assert the imported module's __file__ rather than trust the path it set.

Landed — the occupancy cliff, measured (2026-09-23)

The occupancy rule names 0.90, which is the cap — and the tail explains why. Binning both decode logs (five-day job 1273253, live job 1276246) into occupancy bins of width 0.05 and keeping the bins with at least 20 intervals, the highest bin holding both a median aggregate generation within 10% of the best bin and a median prefix-cache hit rate at or above 0.97 is 0.85–0.90 on both jobs — a target that, at a 100,000-token mean context from the 4,000,000-token pool, implies a width of 36, the engine cap. The rule does not find a boundary; it runs out of populated bins. This is not a licence to set the gate at 36.

The collapse is real and it is a minority tail. §7's 98 tok/s reading at KV 88% is present in the live log — 2026-09-23 08:30:10 at occupancy 0.84, 36 running, 151 tok/s; 08:32:22 at occupancy 0.88, 34 running, 103 tok/s — but collapsed intervals are a small fraction of every bin (live job 2.7–4.0%, five-day job 0%), so no median moves. The bin that contains the collapse also keeps a 0.994 median hit rate, so the hit-rate condition does not catch it either. No bin above 0.90 reaches 20 intervals (6 and 7 intervals respectively), so the instrument cannot see past 0.90 even though the cliff sits near 0.88.

Consequence for the width target. Taken literally the rule returns the cap; taken with the tail beside it, the measured collapse argues for a target below 0.90 and nearer this section's expected 0.8 — about 30 at a 100k context. The two readings differ because the medians are the wrong statistic for a transient: the gate is meant to keep the lane inside a regime it can hold, and the median rule is blind to the regime leaving. Selecting between them is an adjudication this node does not make; the number the rule returns is 0.90 and the number the collapse argues for is below 0.90.

§7 — Immediate relief: one global in-flight gate at the router

A second reading at 08:30 local showed 33 running and 1 queued, with aggregate output down to 98 tok/s (from 217), 14.5 tok/s per stream, prefill at 1.95k tok/s, TTFT 3.0 s and the KV pool at 88%. The lane is past its throughput knee and close to prefix eviction, and about thirty agent sessions across several projects share it. None of them read a crew ceiling. Relaunching the engine with a lower max_running_requests needs a drain first, so the fastest queue on the serve side is the router, which is a separate job and restarts in seconds.

The gate is deliberately unlike the filter removed on 2026-09-14. It is one global bound, with no consumer identity and no per-consumer slots. Once the model catalog has resolved a request's owner, requests are admitted in first-in, first-out order, up to a width W. Order is by completion of that lookup, not by arrival at the router. A request that finds the gate full waits, and holds no engine KV while it waits. It is refused only after a wait bound set well beyond an ordinary turn, and the refusal is an Anthropic overloaded_error (HTTP 529) carrying Retry-After, so a client backs off rather than hanging. A request's slot is released in a finally when its stream ends or the client disconnects. Catalog and count_tokens requests bypass the gate, because they cost the engine no decode width. The router serves no health route. W and the wait bound are read at run time from a small operator-editable file, so retuning W after §3 lands needs no restart; if the file is absent the gate uses its launch defaults, and a width of 0 disables it. The router publishes the gate's in-flight and waiting counts in the lane document beside the engine's own.

Done when: a test drives more concurrent streams than W against a stub upstream and observes at most W upstream requests in flight while none is refused. A second test observes the 529 with Retry-After once the wait bound elapses. A third observes a disconnected waiter and a disconnected streamer both releasing their slots. A fourth observes that editing the file changes W on a running app. Deployment is a router restart, which drops the streams in flight. It is authorised separately and is not part of the node.

The wait bound has to beat the client's first-byte window, not only its request timeout. Claude Code 2.1.280, the build clive and crew workers both run, retries 408, 409, 429 and every 5xx ten times by default. It follows Retry-After exactly, uncapped, in place of its own back-off. Its request timeout is 600 s (API_TIMEOUT_MS). While the gate holds a streaming request, the router sends no headers, so the stream's first-byte window (CLAUDE_STREAM_FIRST_BYTE_TIMEOUT_MS) may be the bound that actually applies. Its default is not yet established. If it is shorter than the gate's wait, a queued request dies client-side as a timeout and is not retried. Two designs follow from that answer. If the window is long, the wait bound goes below it. If it is short, the gate opens the stream as soon as it accepts a request. It then emits the Anthropic ping event while the request waits, and ends with an SSE overloaded_error event if the wait expires. That trades the 529 status for a stream that stays alive in the queue.

A caller that takes the obvious field goes straight past the gate into the KV pool. So a gate at the router binds only callers who read the document correctly. §8 — The root fix: more KV capacity per card

The gate protects throughput at today's capacity. Raising capacity is what lets concurrency rise while aggregate throughput holds or grows, and it is the fix to take wherever the numbers support it. Each lever below needs a serve relaunch, so they are ranked before any drain is asked for. For the installed SGLang version and the DSv4.1 profile as launched, establish four things, citing the launch line, the profile or the source for each:

  • Replication. Whether the KV cache is replicated across the four tensor-parallel ranks, what pool size each rank holds, and whether data-parallel attention is supported for this model in this version, with the pool size it would yield.
  • Dtype. The KV cache dtype in use, and whether a narrower one is supported for this model's attention without an accuracy caveat recorded by the vendor.
  • Host tier. The size of the host tier, the setting that fixes it, the host memory the serve's allocation holds against what the node has free, and whether a storage tier below host memory is available in this version.
  • Device workspace. The device memory taken by CUDA graphs, the static fraction and activation workspace at the current settings, and the pool gained by each reduction that does not change the context cap.

DP attention has a condition the projection does not show. Under DP attention each rank keeps its own radix cache. The launch uses load_balance_method=round_robin, which would scatter one agent's successive turns across four separate caches. That turns a 99% prefix hit rate into roughly a quarter of it, and costs far more than the 4× pool gains. The DP-attention experiment is admissible only with a dispatch policy that keeps a conversation on the rank holding its prefix. That is either an engine load-balance method that is prefix- or session-affine, or cache-aware routing at the router. Which of these this SGLang build supports is part of the experiment's design. Affinity is cache locality, not consumer policy. It gives no caller precedence, and a wrong choice costs a cache miss rather than a fair share, so the objection that removed the per-consumer filter does not apply to it. An agent's turns share one long, stable prefix, so hashing a session or conversation identifier to a rank should capture nearly all of the locality there is. If affinity is available, DP attention's 162 contexts beat the 8M pool's 81 without reopening the prefill-margin risk. The price is decoder bounded replay's prefill gain, and prefill is not what binds at today's contexts.

The next relaunch also sets the engine's own backstops. A bounded max_queued_requests refuses where today the engine absorbs without limit, and still binds a caller that bypasses the router. A prefix-aware schedule_policy replaces fcfs, under which one very long prefill blocks a queue of short turns. Both settings are chosen together with the router gate's wait bound, so the two layers do not refuse at different points.

For each lever, project pool tokens and the context-derived concurrency at a 98,750-token mean context, and say what the change costs. The relaunch that applies the chosen levers drains the lane to a sustained zero first, and it is authorised separately.

§10 — Relaunch with the prefix fix, after a drain

The collapse tail is prefix-cache misses (§6), so the next engine relaunch carries the prefix-side levers. The lead authorised it on 2026-09-23, with a drain and not a hard restart.

What the relaunch carries.

  • schedule_policy = "lpm". Waiting requests with the longest cached prefix are admitted first. It falls back to fcfs above 128 waiting.
  • max_queued_requests = 64. It refuses a caller that bypasses the router instead of absorbing it without limit. With the gate at or below the engine cap, routed traffic never reaches this bound.
  • hicache_ratio 2 → 3, only if the serve's measured host memory shows the extra ~39 GB fits the 600G allocation with margin. The host tier ran 99.9% full, and a larger one keeps more evicted prefixes.
  • SGLang's prefill delayer, only if reading its source shows it brakes prefill storms without starving decode, and names settings to use.

The drain. Every dispatching session is signalled to stop submitting local-lane work and to pause its dispatches. Running work is left to finish; nothing is cancelled. The engine is relaunched only once lane.json reads zero running and zero waiting across several consecutive samples at least a minute apart. The relaunch takes on the order of 25 minutes, since weight loading dominates. It is verified by /v1/models, one real completion, the new launch arguments in the engine's server_args line, and the gate's auto width appearing in lane.json. Only then is RESUME signalled.
