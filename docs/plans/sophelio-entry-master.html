§2 — Architecture, and why transfer is sequenced last

The entry is a low-dimensional filtering problem wearing a high-dimensional costume. Grad–Shafranov has exactly two free functions, p′(ψ) and FF′(ψ), and the conductor currents and geometry ship with the data — so the 4,225-number map is a deterministic function of a state of order ten numbers, measured at 99.8% of flux-function variance in three pooled components over 4,141 extracted frames. Transport is the transition kernel, the free-boundary GS solve maps state to observables, and the Thomson chord model is the observation operator: a Kalman-filter structure with exact nonlinear forwards. The prototype campaign established the crux: the free-boundary solve is part of the observation operator — force balance against the known coil currents is the only path through which FF′ (which sets q95 and ℓ_i) is observable, so it runs inside the assimilation loop, never downstream of it (evidence).

Zero-shot transfer is a property the architecture is built to have, not a metric to chase from the outset. Machine identity enters only through the shipped description and the deterministic forwards; every learned component is dimensionless or geometry-conditioned by construction; nothing MAST-specific is fitted anywhere. Transfer is therefore exercised once, at the sealed rehearsal, when the DIII-D architecture stands — locked below as transfer-assessment-timing.

§3 — Execution order

Ranked by dependency and leverage; the numbering is the intended sequence, with 1–4 runnable now and in flight. Each item names its owning plan — the plan owns the detail, this list owns the order.

  1. Quiescent-window vacuum gate — nova:diiid-forward-onboarding §2, re-opened 2026-08-17 under the locked vacuum-gate-measure decision. Census the corpus for labeled frames where every coil |dI/dt| is below a declared threshold; there, exterior ψ against shipped-coil Green functions plus the label-extracted plasma term isolates geometry from transients. The excluded transient windows are banked as the passive-model fitting corpus (item 6). The prior gate attempt failed against an inapplicable fitted bar and exonerated our kernels (diagnosis).
  2. Label re-solve kill gate — nova:diiid-forward-onboarding §5. Extracted flux functions must re-solve to the labeled map at the grid-resolution floor on real train labels: if the labels are not self-consistent under our own GS operator, the entry's training signal is broken. Extraction kernels landed at 2.0e-8 round-trip on synthetic fixtures; the challenge-label re-solve is the untested half.
  3. Free-boundary observability of FF′ — magnetics-free-equilibrium-recovery §5 prototype rung. The banked loop update carried 26.9% of joint gain and −0.12% of FF′; the Shafranov-shift observation (landed, training-free) and the in-loop free-boundary solve are the two candidate FF′ carriers, tested in that order.
  4. Time-varying-geometry simulability rerun — nova:flux-function-forward-transport §3. The FF′-prior negative was measured under frozen geometry, a q95-calibrated boundary F and a proxy radial coordinate — the three approximations FF′ is most sensitive to. The TORAX geometry adapter landed 2026-08-17, so the rerun settles physics versus artifact.
  5. Windowed smoother to the flat-top floor bar — entry plan §5. The minimum submittable entry: held-out flat-top R²_ψ against a bar pre-registered from our own parity spread, then the first leaderboard submission. A missed bar redirects schedule to diagnosis, not to later phases.
  6. Passive and vessel circuit forwards — nova:diiid-forward-onboarding §4. The entry's highest-risk physics: with no solver-side vertical pin, the passive model sets the field decay index during every transient. Item 1's transient-window residual corpus is its fitting data.
  7. Learned closures in the differentiable solver — learned-closure-current-diffusion-v0. Bounded dimensionless corrections trained MSE-free through the adjoint; the simulability split handed this lane an explicit measured deficiency (FF′) rather than a general hope.
  8. Limited-phase ramp traversal — entry plan §6. Limited frames are deleted corpus-wide while actuators run continuously, so carrying state through unlabeled gaps is a scoring advantage only a transition kernel has — and the ITER first-plasma rehearsal's opening move.
  9. Quarantined referee-emission layer — entry plan §8. Deterministic convention projection on both machines; the DIII-D-gated learned residual is switched off for every MAST prediction, with an automated check proving it.
  10. MAST readiness, sealed rehearsal, submissions — entry plan §9. The MAST machine description enters from the in-house validated registry (never FAIR-MAST training data, never MAST EFIT); one sealed zero-shot pass against nova's own MAST reconstructions; three blind-final submissions and the methods report with full disclosure. This is where transfer is exercised — see the locked decision.

§ Decisions

When is zero-shot MAST transfer assessed?

The architecture is being built from the ground up to support zero-shot transfer — machine identity enters only through descriptions and forwards, and every learned component is dimensionless or geometry-conditioned by construction — so transfer does not need assessing from the outset. It is exercised once, at the sealed rehearsal, when the DIII-D architecture stands; no MAST-side estimation work is scheduled before the DIII-D gates close, and transfer readiness is not carried as an open risk on this roadmap.
