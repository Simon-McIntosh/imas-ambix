Nova–Ambix Hybrid State-Estimation Smoke

Purpose. Replace architectural debate with an executable thin slice. The smoke must show that an ML inference workflow can keep Nova in its inner loop, learn from signal innovation, and expose all three state-estimation products without duplicating Nova physics.

Scope

  1. Scenario: a small physically meaningful machine geometry with measured or synthetic coil/current trajectories and a deliberately withheld future segment.
  2. Ensemble: samples over bounded resistivity/transport, source and boundary parameters. Every member advances through the pinned Nova APIs and carries a stable provenance record.
  3. Observation: Nova-predicted magnetics, flux/profile summaries and signal-carrying flux-surface averages are compared with a held-out synthetic truth first, then one independent real-data slice where available.
  4. Conditioning: Ambix updates the closure/state ensemble from innovation, with topology branches kept discrete when necessary.
  5. Products: forecast, causal analysis and fixed-lag/full-sequence smoothing from the same transition and observation components.
  6. Generative by-products: sample equilibrium maps and diagnostic trajectories under edited coil/actuator plans. Camera output uses the existing conditional camera model only when a compatible checkpoint is available; a flux-surface emissivity proxy must be labeled as a proxy.

Starting envelope

Use a 1 kHz observation clock, 100 Hz full Nova correction, forecast horizons of 10/50/100/250 ms, a 100 ms fixed-lag smoother and a complete-shot offline smoother. Begin with 16–32 CPU members, measure 128 on one H200 and target approximately 1,024 sharded members across eight H200s. These values are hypotheses to measure, not acceptance thresholds to tune around.

Banked substrate evidence

The 2026-08-09 CPU capability smoke passed six focused checks in 348.02 s: pinned Nova circuit/transport integration; Ambix forecast/filter/smoother interfaces; camera-token training and checkpoint round-trip; actuator conditioning under an identifiable intervention; and controllable rollout artifact structure.

The same day, job 1242807 used all five H200s not occupied by live services. Five independent fp64 JAX shards ran the Nova-facing connectivity/FSA kernel at batches 128/256/512/1024/2048. Every shard reported a GPU backend, 100% well-posed batches and CPU parity; the worst absolute difference was 1.14e-13. Per-card throughput ranged from 93,366 equilibria/s at batch 128 to 206,987 equilibria/s at batch 2,048. This proves H200 execution, parity and ensemble partitionability, not five-card strong scaling and not full equilibrium-solve throughput.

This plan starts where those results stop: the integrated Nova-propagated posterior, state-estimation skill and measured multi-card scaling of the complete path.
