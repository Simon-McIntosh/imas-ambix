§1 — Motivation: faithful fields for non-rectangular sections

Every conductor in the reconstruction — active PF coils, coil cases, and the passive vessel / stability-plate structures — is currently modelled with one of two axisymmetric representations: a single thick-cylinder filament of rectangular cross section (the in-tree imas_ambix.gs.cylinder.hybrid_greens kernel), or, where the pack is not a faithful filled rectangle, a retained lattice of many small rectangular filaments. Both are approximations for a genuinely non-rectangular section:

  • The single rectangle is exact only for a true filled axis-aligned box. For a slanted (parallelogram) or trapezoidal section it mis-places and mis-shapes the current — which is exactly why the filament-lattice collapse in non-axisymmetric field subtraction was tightened to a multipole-match gate: staggered packs and slanted structures must NOT be smeared into a bounding box.
  • The multi-filament proxy is faithful in the limit of many filaments, but its cost is O(N) per section and it carries a Riemann-sum granularity error that only falls as ~1/√N — so a slanted vessel element or a finely-wound coil needs tens-to-hundreds of filaments to reach the precision our residual fits care about.

The concrete driver: the MAST Data Catalog pf_passive geometry — already ingested element-identical as field sources (verified 0.0 mm centroid match to our efm structural circuits) — describes ~8 elements as true parallelograms: the botcol / topcol end crowns (slant ≈ ±64.7°) and the P2 stability plates — p2larm / p2uarm arms (45° / 40°) and p2ldivpl / p2udivpl divertor plates. Our efm model carries these as axis-aligned bounding boxes. An analytic polygonal-section Green's function is the principled replacement for both proxies: exact fields for an arbitrary polygon section, at O(edges) cost with no Riemann error, usable wherever a rectangle is not the truth.

§2 — The source method (Urankar Part V) and what exists in-tree

Primary reference (read 2026-07-22; PDF in references/). L. K. Urankar, "Vector potential and magnetic field of current-carrying finite arc segment in analytical form. V. Polygon cross section," IEEE Trans. Magn. 26(3), 1171–1180 (1990). Part V gives the vector potential A and field B of a finite circular arc conductor of arbitrary polygon cross section and constant azimuthal current density. The axisymmetric coil is the full-turn (arc = 2π) special case — the regime we need for the vessel / stability-plate passives and the PF sections.

Method (confirmed from the paper). The cross-section surface integral is converted by Stokes's theorem into a contour integral over the polygon edges (eq. 6: f(φ) = Σ_v f_v(φ)); each edge v₁→v₂ is parametrised r'(t)=r'₁+tΔr, z'(t)=z'₁+tΔz with slope b₁ = Δr/Δz (and r₁=r'₁−b₁·…, a₀²=1+b₁²) — exactly nova biot/polygon.py's fingerprint. Crucially, b₁ = 0 collapses the result back to Part III (rectangular) — the regression gate against cylinder.py. Then the arc-angle integration is done in closed form via the substitution φ = π − 2α to Jacobian elliptic functions.

Special functions used: Jacobian elliptic functions sn/cn/dn; incomplete elliptic integrals of the 1st (K(α,k)) and 2nd (E(α,k)) kind; the incomplete elliptic integral of the third kind Π(α, η², k) (the piece absent from scipy — the pacing item); a standard integral I(η²)=η²∫ sn·cn/(1−η² sn²u) du; and recursion relations for the Jacobian-elliptic integrals I_m, E_lm (Byrd & Friedman, Handbook of Elliptic Integrals, is Urankar's ref [9] — needed for the recurrences).

Two nuances the implementation MUST respect (from a close read):

  • A_φ is semi-analytic, B is fully closed-form. The φ-component of the vector potential carries two terms g_p(α)=∫ arsinh β_p(α) dα (p=1,2) that Urankar states "evade an analytical treatment" and are evaluated by 1-D numerical quadrature (eq. 14e). Since our axisymmetric flux/ψ = 2πR·A_φ needs A_φ, ψ is semi-analytic (one bounded 1-D quadrature per edge); the Br/Bz field components are fully closed-form. This tempers the "exact" claim for flux and slightly raises the ψ cost — still far below a converged filament proxy.
  • Apply the published errata. Appendix B collects corrections to Parts II–IV, and the paper itself carries in-line errata to several of its own equations (e.g. "in (16a) read (r'−r)² instead of (r'²+r²) in the z component"; corrected n²_q=2r/(r∓c)). The raw printed formulae contain typos — transcribe against Appendix B AND cross-check the numerics against Babic (2005), or the field will be subtly wrong in ways the rectangular-reduction gate alone won't catch.

Series + follow-up context (read alongside Part V — the polygon result builds on the earlier machinery and the later robustness work):

  • Urankar Part I — filament approximation (IEEE Trans. Magn. 1980); Part II — thin-sheet; Part III — exact rectangular cross section (the case our cylinder.py already covers); Part IV — general 3-D current density.
  • I. Babic et al., "An improvement in the calculation of the magnetic field for an arbitrary geometry coil with rectangular cross section" (Int. J. Numer. Model., 2005) — numerical-robustness / singularity-handling improvements over the raw Urankar forms; the benchmark for our numerics.

In-tree starting points (not ports). The task is an implementation from the paper, verified against independent references — not a code translation. Two artifacts inform it:

  • imas_ambix/gs/cylinder.py — the validated rectangular axisymmetric kernel (hybrid_greens, ψ / Br / Bz); the polygon method must reduce to it exactly for a rectangular section (the first regression gate).
  • nova nova/biot/polygon.py (HEAD ae6385c0, commit d72adf09 "biot polygon initial work") — an abandoned, incomplete attempt at the same method. It already encodes the sloped-edge parametrisation (b1 = Δr/Δz, Gamma(φ), B2, D2, beta1..3) and the arcsinh zeta integrand, so it is a useful cross-check on the algebra — but it is unvalidated and must not be trusted as-is. Treat it as a hint, derive from the paper.

The hard part. The rectangular kernel needs only complete elliptic integrals K, E (in scipy.special). The polygon result adds the incomplete elliptic integral of the third kind Π(n; φ, k), which scipy does not provide — so a robust implementation (Carlson symmetric form RJ, or Bulirsch's el3/cel) must be written and validated first. Singularity handling at field points on a section edge/vertex is the second numerical risk.

§4 — Effort assessment

Size: XL, a dedicated session. This is a from-paper analytic implementation with real numerical-analysis content, not a port:

  • D1 (incomplete Π) is the pacing item — a correct, branch-safe Carlson/Bulirsch implementation with 1e-12 validation is itself a focused piece of work (~1–2 days incl. tests).
  • D2 (the field formulae) requires careful transcription of the Part V closed forms (sign conventions, the edge-sum bookkeeping, the arcsinh + Π/K/E assembly) and the rectangular-reduction regression — the step most exposed to algebra error, hence the cylinder.py cross-check and the nova fingerprint (~2–3 days).
  • D3/D4 (benchmark + robustness) is where correctness is actually earned — dense-proxy convergence references, awkward-geometry tests, singularity treatment (~2 days).

Total order-of-magnitude: ~1 working week of focused effort. The value is foundational (exact fields for every non-rectangular conductor, retiring the Riemann-limited proxy), and it is cleanly isolated from the reconstruction engine, so it can be built and validated entirely against analytic references before it touches any operator column. Recommended to run as its own session so the numerical-analysis work gets undivided attention.

§5 — Sequencing

D1 → D2 → D3 (∥ D4) → D5. D1 and D2 are pure analytic engineering with hard regression targets (Π tables; rectangular reduction to cylinder.py) and no physics risk. D3 is the make-or-break accuracy/cost benchmark against the proxy. § Decisions

Implement the axisymmetric full-turn case only, or the general finite-arc 3-D polygon segment (Urankar Part V in full)?

D2 implemented the axisymmetric full-turn (arc=2π) case: A_φ from Urankar Part V's per-edge closed antiderivative g(u,φ) with the arc integral as one bounded Gauss–Legendre quadrature on [0,π] (integrand even about π), and B as the exact complex-step curl of ψ. This is exactly what the slanted vessel / stability-plate passives and non-rectangular PF sections need, and it collapses the arc integral cleanly. The general finite-arc 3-D polygon segment (which would additionally upgrade the 3-D filaments3d RMP/EFCC kit to finite cross-section) remains a documented extension, not built here.

How to implement the incomplete elliptic integral of the third kind Π(n; φ, k) (absent from scipy)?

D1 spike settled it: Π(n;φ,m) on scipy's Carlson elliprf/elliprj validates to 1e-12 vs mpmath across the full parameter grid (ordinary n<1, complete φ=π/2, φ>π/2 half-period reduction) and — decisively — the n>1 characteristic returns the real Cauchy-principal-value branch for free (scipy elliprj with negative fourth argument == Re(mpmath.ellippi) to 12 digits), the exact regime the polygon integrals cross. Carlson is scipy-native (no new special-function code, matching the cylinder.py precedent) and needs no separate pole handling, so Bulirsch el3 was not implemented — Carlson clears every accuracy/robustness gate outright.

Once validated, does the analytic polygon kernel REPLACE the multi-filament proxy for non-rectangular sections, or run alongside it as an option?

D3 benchmark settled it. Against an independent Richardson-extrapolated exact-affine-tiling ground truth (~1e-9), the analytic kernel is exact to ~1e-13 with no tuning and no tolerance dependence; the operator's real fallback — an axis-aligned staircased filament lattice (PFFilament rectangles cannot shear) — needs ~3k (parallelogram) to ~38k (hollow frame) filaments to RELIABLY reach 1e-4 (suffix-max envelope; the staircase makes err(N) non-monotone — thin rails alias with the grid) and is 3-14x the wall-cost at MATCHED accuracy on every genuinely non-rectangular shape. So the analytic polygon section REPLACES the proxy as the representation for non-rectangular sections (the retained-lattice fallback becomes an analytic-section). The multi-filament lattice survives only as the general fallback for packs that are not a clean polygon (mixed-sign windings, non-decomposable staggered packs, where collapse_rectangular_circuits already keeps the exact lattice); a true axis-aligned rectangle keeps the cheaper cylinder_greens. No robustness edge case forced a proxy fallback — D4 showed the kernel is robust to on-edge/vertex/large-slope/thin-plate stresses.
