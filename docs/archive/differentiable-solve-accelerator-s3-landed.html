Newton–Krylov: the Jacobian corruption is cured; the basin is not NK's to hold

On the fixed-shape map (the arm the prior study measured as erratic), NK under the connectivity read reaches machine-precision residuals (2×10⁻¹² and 5×10⁻¹⁰ on 2/6 slices, and 0.018–0.08 on two more) where hard-read NK thrashes at ~0.13–0.19 on 5/6 — a clean Jacobian-free GMRES at last. But its converged fixed points sit 27–88 cm from the centroid-pinned Picard reference: the fixed-shape map has multiple fixed points and carries no current-centroid pin, so NK converges beautifully to the wrong basin. Basin control lives in the pinned LSQ map; a pinned-map NK is the retained research arm, not a production path.

§4 verdict — CPU per-solve stays batching-bound; the finding informs the GPU rollout

  • ≥3× on CPU per-solve: NO. The connectivity read costs 2.63× the hard solve's CPU wall (the jax read runs on host every sweep); Anderson buys back 1.4–2.5× in sweeps — net ≈ 0.5–0.95× wall. Smoothness fixes convergence, not CPU throughput. The ≥3× multiple is confirmed batching-bound → gpu-accelerated-labeler, where the same read is on-device (7.4 ms/slice batched) and its cost is amortised by the vmap batch.
  • What the GPU rollout inherits: (i) the inner fixed point on the smooth map is well-behaved — a small fixed sweep budget with a warm start is sound (the assumption the batched rollout makes); (ii) Anderson-accelerated Picard is the inner-solve accelerator of choice (interpretable, safeguarded, 1.4–2.5×, byte-comparable); (iii) NK is viable on device only with the centroid pin folded into the map.
