The load-bearing finding: the pin belongs INSIDE the map

  • The disc seed alone is NOT the basin insurance. The §2 hypothesis (batchable disc seeding fixes the corner-lock) is refuted on host ground truth: the unpinned fixed-θ chain corner-locks to the outboard attractor (R ≈ 1.77 m) from the ramp on, disc seed and warm chain notwithstanding. Basin control lives in the centroid-PINNED map — the accelerator study's NK conclusion, now confirmed for Picard too.
  • The device map therefore carries the K=2 pinned scaffold per sweep: a closed-form 2-coefficient LSQ (free-sign p/f pair on the (1−ψ_N) edge family; rows = Ip normalisation + disc-centroid soft tether, σ = 2 cm) — the "tiny per-sweep profile LSQ" the matrix-freeze decision allows. With it the device march tracks the confined branch on all 48 slices.
  • Anderson on the pinned map: a tie (1.00× sweeps vs Picard; both converge 49% of warm slices at the 20-sweep budget). The pinned smooth map does not limit-cycle, so the accelerator's cycling-class win has nothing to buy; safeguarded Anderson (history reset on residual growth + 2× step cap) is retained — on the unpinned probe it reached 3.9×10⁻¹⁶ residual where plain Picard sat at 1.5×10⁻¹⁰.
  • One engine, CPU and GPU: the identical march on the CPU backend agrees with the H200 to 2.9×10⁻¹⁵ m — the backend-portability decision demonstrated on the full chained rollout, not just one solve.
  • Tiered precision through a chained march: tf32 GEMM (fp64 state) costs ~0.9 cm median axis vs the fp64 march — far above the 0.004 cm single-solve figure (§2) because trajectory chaining amplifies the rounding differences. Watch item for §4: gate the tf32 lane at corpus scale before enabling it for labels.

What §4 (offline corpus) inherits
