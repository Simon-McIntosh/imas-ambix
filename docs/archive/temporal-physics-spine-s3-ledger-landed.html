3 — Early-ramp labels: not needed, and not the fix (lead's question answered)

Dedicated audit (scripts/early_ramp_label_audit.py, 8 label-reaching train shots):

  • The magnetics are never noise-limited near the label floor. Plasma-attributable SNR (vacuum-map residual method) crosses 10 at ~12 kA and holds 75–200 across the operating range; the channel noise floor is ~2% of the spine's whitening scales. The 300 kA floor is 25× above the SNR-10 point.
  • The pre-label window is small. From the physical density-onset marker, the unlabelled early window spans ~3–4 ms and carries 4–7% (median 5.6%) of the shot's wall flux swing — a bound that mixes inductive and resistive consumption.
  • Burn-through is safely below the floor. Density onset and the dIp/dt knee sit at t ≈ 3–4 ms and tens of kA; the first 300 kA crossing is ~3 ms later; the first actual label (~450 kA, t ≈ 33 ms) is SANE (li3 ≈ 0.89). The pathological slices are the NEXT two labels — a fast-early-ramp reconstruction pathology at 450–500 kA, not a breakdown-physics or noise problem.
  • Answer to the lead's resistivity question: yes — a model-based resistive backout through breakdown/burn-through would require η physics (few-eV, radiation/impurity-dominated, non-Maxwellian) we cannot model, and no η(ψ_N) family in the operator's bracket covers it. But the ledger never needs it: the chain GAUGES at the first sane labelled slice by construction and only integrates the modelled resistive term where the plasma is well past burn-through. Extending labels below 300 kA would add more fast-ramp reconstruction pathologies plus an unmodellable window — cost, not information. Policy locked into the machinery: keep the 300 kA label floor, gauge at the first li3-sane slice. Only an absolute breakdown-referenced Ejima claim would need the early window, and that claim is out of reach honestly — the windowed, incremental C_E is the honest readout.

4 — What this says about the temporal thesis and the ML question

The lead's framing — "we have a time-dynamics model but seem to lack the diagnostic set to tune it; if the physics spine can't, an ML operator will struggle too" — is now a measured statement, not a hypothesis:

  • The dynamics models themselves are NOT the problem: the eddy circuit model is validated per-circuit at corr 0.96–0.99 on held-back case currents; the diffusion operator matches TORAX to 0.2%; the ledger closes at 60 mWb with η at the Spitzer scale.
  • What the external diagnostic set can see of the interior is: Ip, the current centroid, βp + li/2 (Shafranov), and now the shot-integrated li (ledger, σ_li ~ 0.1–0.2 through the moment/ledger precision). Every temporal channel converts its physics into exactly these observables and no further — and the axis sits in the null of all of them.
  • Consequently any constraint that biases the interior null — however physical — pays the measured −0.16 axis toll on held-out, while genuinely new split information (synthetic 0.044 → 0.029) drowns in that same null on real data. An ML operator consuming the same 73 magnetics + Rogowski + fl loops inherits the same null: R2 measured exactly this (21% sensor-misfit conversion, zero referee skill). The missing tuning information is kinetic/interior (Thomson T_e — corpus-gated; MSE — 99% NaN), not algorithmic.
