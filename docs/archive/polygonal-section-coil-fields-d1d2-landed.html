D1 — incomplete elliptic Π (decision elliptic-pi-impl → Carlson)

Π(n; φ, m) = ∫₀φ dθ / [(1 − n sin²θ)√(1 − m sin²θ)] is absent from scipy.special. Built on scipy's Carlson symmetric forms elliprf (RF) and elliprj (RJ) via DLMF 19.25.14, with a half-period reduction for |φ| > π/2. Validated to 1e-12 relative against mpmath across the ordinary branch (n < 1), the complete φ = π/2 limit, and — decisively — the n > 1 characteristic where the integrand pole is crossed: scipy's elliprj with a negative fourth argument returns the real Cauchy principal value, equal to Re(mpmath.ellippi) to 12 digits. Carlson is scipy-native (no new special-function code, matching the cylinder.py precedent) and needs no separate pole handling, so Bulirsch's el3 was not implemented — Carlson cleared every accuracy/robustness gate outright. This is the reusable foundation a fully-closed elliptic assembly of the polygon fields would rest on (see "what's next").

D2 — the analytic polygon-section kernel (decision arc-scope → axisymmetric-only)

Vector potential — Urankar Part V, from the paper. Stokes converts the cross-section surface integral into a sum over the polygon edges; per edge the edge-parameter (u) integral closes to the antiderivative g(u, φ) (eq 9, with a₀²=1+b₁², Γ, B², D, β₁,₂,₃ per eq 9a), leaving one smooth 1-D integral over the arc angle φ for the full turn — evaluated by composite Gauss–Legendre on [0, π] (the integrand is even about π) and doubled. g was regression-pinned edge-by-edge against a dense 2-D quadrature of the raw ∫∫ r'/D integral before use. A 1990 typesetting trap was resolved: the printed "Γ/2a₀²D" means Γ·D/(2a₀²) — D is a numerator factor (confirmed by dimensional analysis, the b₁=0 reduction, and eq 12). The Appendix B errata were confirmed to correct Parts II–IV only, not Part V.

Field — the exact curl. Rather than transcribe the paper's longer, more error-prone closed B integrands (eq 11b, whose printed BZ row carried a b₁-dependent discrepancy against the filament ground truth), the field is the exact curl of the verified vector potential, BZ = (1/2πR)∂ψ/∂R and BR = −(1/2πR)∂ψ/∂Z, evaluated by complex-step differentiation of ψ (∂f/∂x = Im f(x+ih)/h, h~1e-30). Complex-step is exact to machine precision — no subtractive cancellation — so this is analytic differentiation, and it guarantees ψ↔B consistency by construction.

Validation (12 tests). Rectangular reduction to cylinder_greens at 1e-10 (including interior points); agreement with an exact-affine-tiled filament oracle to ~1e-6 for parallelogram / steep-crown / trapezoid / triangle sections (oracle-convergence-limited, not kernel-limited); Ampère circulation = μ0·I; vertex-order and starting-vertex invariance; smoothness through the conductor.

Decision arc-scope = axisymmetric-only: the full-turn (arc = 2π) case is what the vessel / stability-plate passives and PF sections need, and it collapses the arc integral to the single bounded φ-quadrature above. The general finite-arc 3-D polygon segment remains a documented extension (it would additionally give the 3-D RMP/EFCC kit a finite-cross-section upgrade) but is out of scope here.

What's next — D3 (make-or-break benchmark)

Benchmark the analytic kernel against the multi-filament proxy the operator uses today: per section shape (rectangle, parallelogram, trapezoid, decomposed L/hollow) and at realistic sensor distances, report analytic-vs-proxy field agreement, the proxy filament count N needed to reach 1e-3/1e-4/1e-5, and the wall-cost ratio. The exact-affine-tiled filament oracle built for D2's cross-check is D3's converged reference. Then D4 (robustness at awkward geometries) and D5 (wire the ~8 slanted pf_passive elements into the geometry layer). A fully-closed elliptic assembly of the fields (removing the last φ-quadrature, on the D1 Π foundation) is an optimisation to weigh after D3 shows whether the quadrature cost matters.
