Tokenizer transient-frame fidelity

Status: Active — stratified rFID classifier landed 2026-05-28 (commit e792c71); first measurement will come from the re-bench of production 1209004. v1 phases queued depending on what that measurement shows.

§1 — Why this exists

The tokenizers plan's M1 gate is rFID < 5 on the 100-shot bench. We measured rFID=16.25 on the ImageNet baseline (job 1208918) and 1209004 is running to drive it below 5. But mean rFID across all val frames is the WRONG quantity for the physics that comes next.

A plasma shot is dominated by quiet frames: ohmic ramp-up, steady-state, controlled flat-top, soft termination. Transient frames — ELM crashes, L-H / H-L transitions, internal modes, disruptions — are a small minority (typically 5-30 % of frames). But they are precisely the events the world model is being built to predict. If the decoder reconstructs quiescent frames at rFID=2 and transient frames at rFID=20, the mean rFID can pass the < 5 gate while the world model is trained to predict tokens that fail on every interesting event.

A second compounding effect: from tokenizer-frame-quality-filter, 1.79 % of corpus frames are blank near-black "easy" frames. Decoder-on-blank is essentially zero rFID, dragging the mean down further. Mean rFID is inflated by easy frames at both ends (blank + quiescent) and the physically-relevant transient bin gets averaged into invisibility.

This plan fixes that by: (1) reporting per-class rFID separately, (2) making the rFID<5 gate apply to the transient class (not the mean), and (3) training the decoder so transient frames are over-represented and over-weighted vs the quiescent default.

§2 — Classification approach

Two complementary classifiers, layered:

ClassifierSignalWhat it capturesStatus
Spatial std (v0) per-frame pixel std frames with bright filamentary structure, edge structure, plasma vs no-plasma landed (commit e792c71); frame_activity_class() in imas_ambix/eval/metrics.py
Temporal gradient (v1) |frame_t − frame_{t-1}| / max(|frame_{t-1}|, 1) L-H transitions, ELM crashes, MARFEs — events that are transient in TIME even if individual frames look quiescent queued — needs adjacent-frame access in the eval loop

A frame is "transient" if EITHER spatial-std OR temporal-gradient flags it. Quiescent frames have low values for both. Blank frames are flagged separately (mean<5, std<1) and the frame-quality filter drops them entirely so they don't pollute either bin.

Spatial-std heuristic (v0 — already in use)

# From imas_ambix/eval/metrics.py:frame_activity_class
flat = arr.reshape(T, -1)
fmean = flat.mean(axis=1)
fstd = flat.std(axis=1)

blank_mask = (fstd < 1.0) & (fmean < 5.0)
nonblank = ~blank_mask
median_std = float(np.median(fstd[nonblank]))
quiescent = nonblank & (fstd < median_std)
transient = nonblank & (fstd >= median_std)

Strengths: zero dependency on previous frame, fast, captures spatially busy frames. Weakness: a bright but stationary plasma can look "transient" without being one; a small fast feature on a dim background can look "quiescent" and miss the L-H signature.

Temporal-gradient heuristic (v1 — queued)

# Per-shot, after loading frames in order:
diff = np.abs(frames[1:] - frames[:-1]).astype(np.float32)
norm = np.maximum(np.abs(frames[:-1]).astype(np.float32), 1.0)
temporal_gradient = (diff / norm).reshape(T-1, -1).mean(axis=1)
# frame i is temporal-transient if temporal_gradient[i-1] > threshold
# (frame 0 inherits the classifier from frame 1)

The threshold is calibrated on a small labelled set of known L-H transitions (in §4 Phase 2). Once both classifiers are in place, the union (OR) gives the final transient label.

§3 — Training intervention

Three changes to imas_ambix/tokenizer/finetune_decoder.py:

3a. Importance sampling (replace uniform stride)

Currently: indices = np.linspace(0, T-1, frames_per_shot, dtype=int) — each frame has equal sampling weight. Replace with:

# Compute spatial-std per frame (cheap; one pass over the shot's data)
fstd = compute_per_frame_std(shot)  # cached in the sidecar from frame-quality scan
# Weight: 1.0 for quiescent, alpha (=2.5) for transient
weights = np.where(fstd >= median_std, alpha_transient, 1.0)
weights[blank_mask] = 0.0  # safety belt — should also be filtered by sidecar
# Sample without replacement
indices = rng.choice(T, size=frames_per_shot, p=weights/weights.sum(), replace=False)

Total compute budget unchanged (still 50 frames/shot). Effective transient representation in the training pool rises from ~50 % (uniform, assuming 50/50 quiescent/transient split) to ~71 % (with alpha=2.5).

3b. Per-class loss weighting

The current loss is L = l1_weight × L1 + perceptual_weight × LPIPS, both unweighted across the batch. Replace with:

# Per-sample class label passed alongside the frame batch via the dataset
# (extend _PlasmaFrameDataset.__getitem__ to return (frame, class_id))
loss_per_sample = l1_per_sample + cfg.perceptual_weight * lpips_per_sample
class_weights = torch.tensor({
    "quiescent": 1.0,
    "transient": cfg.transient_loss_weight,  # default 2.5
}[label] for label in batch_labels)
loss = (loss_per_sample * class_weights).mean()

Note: this requires per-sample (not per-batch) loss computation — straightforward since both L1 and LPIPS are reductions of per-pixel distances. Adds < 1 % overhead per step.

3c. Transient-rich mini-batch composition (optional, v1.5)

Beyond per-sample weighting, optionally enforce a minimum transient fraction per batch. E.g., for batch_size=16 ensure at least 8 are transient. Implementation: custom BatchSampler that draws from stratified pools. Trade-off: tighter sampling guarantees vs. lost gradient noise. Defer to v1.5 — measure 3a+3b first to see if it's needed.

§4 — Implementation phases

Phase 1 — Stratified rFID in production bench (LANDED 2026-05-28)

frame_activity_class() + rfid_stratified() in imas_ambix/eval/metrics.py; wired into bench/tokenizer.py when "rfid_stratified" is in config.metrics; both bench configs (v0-rbb-100shot.yaml and -finetune.yaml) opt in. First measurement comes from the re-bench of 1209004.

Phase 2 — Temporal-gradient classifier + calibration

Extend frame_activity_class() to accept an optional temporal_threshold and prev-frame tensor. Update evaluate() in finetune_decoder.py to keep the previous frame's tensor around so the classifier sees consecutive pairs.

Calibration set: 5-10 shots with hand-labelled L-H transition windows (work with diagnostics team — Ben Dudson / NSF-MAST collaborator). Pick the threshold so a labelled L-H window is >70 % flagged transient.

Phase 3 — Importance-sampling in finetune scan

Update _scan_shots() to read per-frame std from the sidecar (or compute on the fly if absent); weight indices by class; return weighted entries. Verify training pool composition: ~71 % transient with alpha=2.5 vs ~50 % with uniform.

Phase 4 — Per-sample loss weighting in compute_loss

Refactor compute_loss() to accept per-sample weights and reduce after weighting. Default transient_loss_weight=2.5. Expose via DecoderFinetuneConfig.

Phase 5 — v1 fine-tune run + re-bench

Re-train decoder with phases 2-4 applied. Goal: rfid_transient < 5 on the 100-shot bench. Compare against the v0 (1209004) result:

  • v0 (no transient handling) — expect rfid_overall ≈ rfid_quiescent, rfid_transient notably higher.
  • v1 (this plan) — expect rfid_transient reduced by 50-70 %, rfid_quiescent slightly worse (no longer the entire training focus) but still below the gate.

Phase 6 — Promote transient-rFID as the M1 gate

Once v1 ships, update the tokenizers plan to make rfid_transient < 5 the M1 success criterion (replacing or alongside the mean-rFID criterion). Document the rationale in a comment on the tokenizers plan.

§5 — Decision: is this v1 required, or polish?

The data point that decides is the re-bench of production 1209004 (after it completes). Two scenarios:

Re-bench resultGap (transient − quiescent)Action

rfid_overall ≈ 5, rfid_quiescent ≈ 3, rfid_transient ≈ 12 ~9 points v1 mandatory — block world-model-v0 until transient-rFID < 5 achieved via this plan
rfid_overall ≈ 8, rfid_quiescent ≈ 4, rfid_transient ≈ 16 ~12 points v1 needed + likely escalate to Cosmos-Tokenizer (the LFQ codebook is too coarse for plasma transients)

§6 — Out of scope

  • Temporal modeling in the tokenizer. Open-MAGVIT2 is a per-frame autoencoder by design. Modeling motion is the world-model's job, not the tokenizer's. This plan makes the tokenizer faithful on transient FRAMES, not on transient TRAJECTORIES.
  • Curriculum learning (start easy, gradually mix in hard). Worth trying if §5 says we're far from the gate; defer to v2.
  • Multi-resolution / hierarchical tokenizer. Bigger architectural change; v2 if Cosmos-Tokenizer doesn't close the gap.
  • Online active learning from world-model failure modes (re-finetune on frames the world-model fails on). v3 + concept.
  • Other cameras. rir IR transients are qualitatively different (longer time-scales) — same framework should work but thresholds need re-calibration.
