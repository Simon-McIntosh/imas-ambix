  • The offset-only static calibration is NOT an instrument model.   • The lead's cross-shot filtering proposal is supported and already embodied: per-shot offsets are channel-characteristic and near-constant across shots (between-shot / within-shot variance median 0.084 pre-breakdown, 0.009 post-dwell, 100% of channels < 1) — nuisance parameters of this class ARE collapsible by cross-shot averaging/filtering, which is exactly what the frozen 10-shot fit performs. No shot-to-shot drift was observed that would require a live filtering pass; the caveat is that the quantity being averaged is model error, not an instrument constant.

The three fit-machinery lessons (each measured, each now pinned by a test)

  1. In-family null-space fill. The free-sign K=2 LSQ posts the best cost floor but reaches it with mixed-sign p′/FF′ splits on 157/160 slices (gross/net current ≈ 1.6): the two drives partially cancel, shearing jφ within surfaces to absorb the residual static model error — and the LCFS pays (−4.0 vs −2.9). R1's lesson recurs one level up the spine: any sign-indefinite freedom, even 2-DOF, gets spent on the unmodelled signal.
  2. The data demand a broad, nearly pure-FF′ profile. The continuous fit rails at its bounds on nearly every slice (β0 → 0.02, α → 0.4 — flatter than linear, edge-weighted, FF′-dominated). Within the sign-physical family this is the closest in-family expression of the near-wall signal the free patch basis absorbed as its 0.75 Ip halo — the same coil-model attribution, measured a third way.
  3. The free-boundary fixed point is multi-branch, and branch selection — not DOF — is the hard part. From a fixed-shape seed the sign-constrained Picard escapes to an outboard corner attractor (axis R ≈ 1.6–1.7 m, cost 5–12); an equally good physical fixed point exists on the confined branch — proven by seeding: free-sign cost 0.428 vs sign-constrained 0.434 on the same slice, same branch, the latter with jφ ≥ 0 everywhere and a single (1−ψN)0.5 FF′ column. The shipped recipe: the stable free-sign K=2 LSQ scouts the branch, the sign-constrained solve certifies a physical profile there — Tier-3 instrument guides, Tier-2 carries. At scale the certification is honest but partial: the scouted sign-constrained arm scores 157/160 at cost 1.85 with axis median 26.7 cm — the certified physical profile gives up part of the axis accuracy the free-sign shear buys (0.60 / 4.0 cm). The gap is a structured, persistent residual: the magnetics reward a within-surface R-shape that the static sign-definite family cannot express — exactly the form of the Maschke–Perrin R⁴ rotation column (W4 Stage A's target) and/or the coil-model remainder; W3's aliasing matrix is the instrument that separates them. Two bounded-solver failure modes were also caught and pinned: a 10⁴-weighted anchor row drowns the misfit below the optimality tolerance; no anchor at all lets the fit prefer ~0.2 Ip and the rescale destroys the optimum.

Information-budget answer (the ladder's question): the magnetics support ≈ 1–2 effective profile DOF beyond the Ip anchor. The sign-constrained ladder is identical to two decimals at K = 2, 4, 6 and 10 (cost 1.85, axis 26.7 cm, LCFS −3.13): every run puts its whole coefficient mass on one broad FF′ column and leaves the higher-K columns at exactly zero, so the CI-gated ladder admits no addition and the smoothness ridge never becomes load-bearing. Growing profile DOF without growing information (interior diagnostics, time) is measured pointless — the spine's §4.3 prediction, now with numbers. The passive rank-8 sidecar IS load-bearing where profile DOF is not: +0.33% Ip of vessel current buys cost 0.60 → 0.56, coverage 129 → 154/160, axis skill −2.45 → −0.31 [−1.09, −0.00] and the first positive X-point-set point estimate of the closure line (+0.11).

W2 — nuisance layer: what was built and measured

  • Vacuum-interval validation (scripts/vacuum_interval_calibration.py): pre-breakdown + post-plasma dwell coil-only slices (no plasma inversion, no EFIT — pure Tier-1 forward prediction), stratified quasi-static vs ramping by measured |dI/dt| (eddy contamination measured, not assumed), 12 fit + 8 held-out shots.
  • Gain adjudication: on the trustworthy quasi-static stratum per-channel gains are unidentifiable (no coil dynamic range — correctly pinned to 1); the only stratum with leverage (solenoid ramp) is eddy-driven (median 1.25, p10–p90 0.88–1.96) and uncorrelated with the rejected plasma-fitted affine gains — no clean vacuum evidence for any real per-channel gain ≠ 1; the affine rejection (832e2a9) is confirmed as laundering.
  • Cross-arm offset consistency: median |Δoffset|/σ — free-arm vs vacuum 0.040, priors-arm vs vacuum 0.128, priors vs free 0.091. The physics-prior arm pushes fitted offsets further from the instrument truth — the priors fold plasma-model structure into the "calibration". A genuine instrument offset would be arm-independent; the frozen offsets are not.
  • Passive eigenmode sidecar (build_passive_sidecar + gate flags --passive-k/--passive-ridge, OFF by default): top-k whitened sensor-space modes of the 146-node g_passive block, singular-value-normalised columns (an ampere-scale column sits ~10⁵ below the Ip-normalised profile columns — unnormalised, any relative ridge silently zeroes the sidecar; caught by the recovery pin), mode flux enters the Picard field via per-circuit grid-ψ columns, amplitudes excluded from the plasma Ip anchor, reported as circuit-space currents. Synthetic-truth pin: cost 0.61 → 0.053 with the truth axis recovered to mm.
