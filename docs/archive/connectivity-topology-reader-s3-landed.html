Quick status

Outcomes

What landed

Classify-after nulls (stencils-only port). Every interior grid vertex is classified by the sign-change count of ψ around its 8-neighbour ring (Kuijper: 0 → O-point, 4 → X-point) — a whole-grid, fixed-shape, differentiable reduction (“find all stationary points” is cheap). The relevant nulls are picked with a fixed-shape proximity mask (the §2 axis-connected flood region + the in-wall raster for the axis; a flux-proximity band at the binding level for the saddle) — an elementwise (nz, nr) boolean AND, never a variable-length gather, so the read stays jit/vmap/grad-safe. A selected vertex's 3×3 neighbourhood is refined sub-grid by a biquadratic surface fit (the stationary point, its flux, and its Hessian type in closed form — nova's select.subnull algorithm, reproduced so the read carries no cross-package dependency). The variable X-count uses the static-count idiom (jnp.where(size=K) + NaN-pad).

Unified boundary binding. The §2 read used a limited/diverted size-ratio switch (two-sided-mean saddle level for diverted, sub-grid wall min for limited), which left the diverted binding at a 0.46 cm / +0.43% residual. §3 removes the switch: the binding is the confined-most (nearest ψ_N to the axis) of {sub-grid wall tangency, sub-grid X-point saddle flux}. A wall tangency binds a limited plasma; an X-point saddle binds a diverted one; whichever is reached first closes the boundary — the same outermost-closed connectivity rule, now sub-grid on both obstructions. On a diverted plasma the reachable wall sits outside the separatrix so it overshoots and loses the min to the saddle; on a limited plasma no on-binding saddle is accepted so the wall wins — no class decision enters the boundary.

Rejecting spurious same-flux nulls

A saddle candidate at the binding flux can appear elsewhere in (or outside) the vessel at the same flux, so the flux band alone is insufficient. Three fixed-shape guards, all ANDed at candidate generation, keep only the true separatrix saddle: (a) inside the wall raster; (b) inside the flood-rejoin band — a few-cell dilation of the axis-connected region, so the candidate sits at the region's edge where the separatrix actually pinches; (c) clear of the axis by a minimum distance (the device analogue of the host min_axis_dist guard against a near-axis null-space saddle). The emergent X-set and the diverted class inherit the same filtered candidates.

What's next — §4

The connectivity read already takes the wall as a raster boolean; §4 validates the boundary read on synthetic single-loop, multi-polygon, and time-varying-wall cases (limited and diverted).
