Physics deep-dive — resistivity, the EnKF comparison, and where the model is thin

Follow-up questions (2026-07-20) on what the engine actually assumes. Short version: the engine’s one free function is the parallel plasma resistivity, held frozen for firewall reasons; its profile regularisation is spatial (a curvature ridge on the low-order p′/FF′ ladder) while its temporal regularisation is the resistive-diffusion transport itself; and it carries no H-mode pedestal and no dynamic sawtooth — both are identified extensions.

1. What η is, and why it is frozen

η is the parallel plasma resistivity profile η∥(ψN) [Ω·m]. In code (current_diffusion.EtaProfile) it is a bounded, monotone 3-parameter family η(ψN) = η0·exp(contrast·ψNshape), edge-hotter-than-axis. It is the only free function in the §3 current-diffusion coupling: it sets the diffusion coefficient (σ∥ = 1/η) and the ohmic parallel current ⟨J·B⟩ = σ∥⟨E·B⟩. Everything else the coupling needs — the plasma current Ip, the surface flux swing — is measured.

Why frozen (three reasons, all firewall):

  • No leakage. η must never be fitted to the held-out MSE — that would tune the model toward the very quantity it is judged on. It is calibrated on the disjoint tune split (shots 11774/11775/11777/11779) against measured drives only — the surface-flux / loop-voltage budget plus the coefficient-prediction consistency — then FROZEN, and the eval consumes the three numbers ([-6.335, -0.097, 4.383]). The calibration reproduced exactly this session (objective 0.252 → 0.0186).
  • Cross-shot, not per-shot. Three parameters shared across every shot. A per-shot or per-slice η could absorb per-shot misfit the way a tuned gain does — exactly the EFIT move the firewall bans.
  • Deliberately not derived from Te. The physically correct resistivity is temperature-dependent (Spitzer η ∝ Te−3/2). The code uses the bounded parametric stand-in “while Thomson Te is gated”: MAST Thomson coverage is patchy across the corpus, so a robust σ∥(Te) closure is not available shot-to-shot, and folding a fitted Te→σ closure in re-opens the “keep folding stuff in → EFIT in all but name” risk.

The temperature-dependence point is exactly right, and it is why the classical EnKF beats us. The EnKF drives TORAX with measured Te(ρ) (Thomson) → a neoclassical σ∥(Te) closure. That measured-Te channel is precisely how it breaks the single-slice magnetic under-determination and reaches 0.225 rad where our frozen-η engine sits at 0.362 (the 0.137 rad gap). Our engine leaves that information on the table by design. These are parameters that should be learnt from data, and that is the whole point of the learned operator (§5): fold the kinetic / high-bandwidth channels that carry the Te (and transport) information — the magic sauce — and learn the closure the firewall engine refuses to assume.

5. Regularisation of the p′/FF′ profiles — spatial vs temporal

There are two distinct regularisers, and the space-vs-time split maps onto them cleanly:

  • Spatial (spectral) smoothness — a curvature ridge. The profile is a low-order non-negative ladder (np=nf=3 monomials (1−ψN)e, e = 0.5,1,1.5 per family). On top of the basis truncation and jφ ≥ 0, a second-difference (curvature) penalty on each family’s coefficient ladder (weight smoothness = 0.01 in the frozen spine, block-diagonal so p′ and FF′ are damped separately) suppresses high-order wiggles in the profile shape. This is spatial and static-per-slice. An optional soft monotonicity penalty exists but is off in the frozen config.
  • Temporal — the transport IS the time regulariser. The only time-link between slices is the diffusion-evolved coefficient prior (§3): the resistive ψ-diffusion propagates the previous slice’s profile forward over the measured interval and soft-centres the next fit on it. So — exactly as the intuition goes — we do not impose an ad-hoc temporal smoothness; the physics transport (resistive current diffusion) carries the temporal regularisation. The smoothness ridge is purely spatial.

6. Transport barriers and the H-mode pedestal

Not modelled. There is a scrape-off-layer decay-foot capability (profile_regularization.profile_basis_foot, with a dimensionless pedestal/foot width w) that lets current continue C¹-smoothly past the separatrix, but it is off in the frozen spine (sol_cap = 1.0 → current pinned to zero at ψN = 1). And even with the foot on, it is a smooth SOL tail, not an edge transport barrier: an H-mode pedestal is a narrow region of steep p′/current gradient, which the low-order (n=3) smooth basis cannot represent. So the engine as scored in §4 carries no ETB/pedestal — part of why the outboard pitch residual (0.62 rad) exceeds the near-axis one (0.43). A faithful pedestal would need a higher-order/knotted edge basis or an explicit pedestal term (firewall-safe if driven by a measured edge quantity).

7. The q ≥ 1 condition and a dynamic sawtooth model

A static q0 ≥ 1 clamp exists; a dynamic sawtooth does not. The code carries a sawtooth prior: q_axis_linear_bound gives the large-aspect circular bound jφ,axis ≤ 2Bφ0/(μ0R0) ⇔ q0 ≥ 1, applied as a soft one-sided hinge on the on-axis current density (SoftPriors.q_axis_max), plus a contour-integrated q(ψN) diagnostic. But it is off in the frozen spine (q_bound = false) — the §4 result used neither this clamp nor the SOL foot. It is a static per-slice clamp, not reconnection dynamics: no periodic q0 relaxation, no Kadomtsev/Porcelli core flattening at the crash.

A dynamic sawtooth model is a strong, complementary extension. In the Kalman-filter equilibrium-reconstruction line (van Mulders et al.), a periodic sawtooth reconnection pins core q toward 1 between crashes and flattens the current, which constrains the internal profile in a way pure external magnetics cannot — a temporal/kinetic constraint of exactly the kind the interior null needs. Two firewall-safe routes here: (a) turn on the soft q0 ≥ 1 clamp and add a dynamic q0→1 relaxation as an additional physics constraint on the §3 diffusion (crash timing from the fast diagnostics that see it — SXR, Mirnov); (b) let the learned operator (§5) learn the sawtooth signature from those fast channels. Both are the same thesis as the resistivity point: the sawtooth cadence and the resistivity are internal-state information that should be learnt from (or constrained by) the fast measured data, not assumed.
