RCA — the boundary-size bias, found and fixed in two steps

A first single escape test (region touches the innermost in-wall shell) fired ~one grid cell inside the true wall crossing / saddle, so ψ_bnd was biased toward the axis and the boundary came out consistently too small (real-data ψ_bnd sat entirely on one side of y = x, median 3.7%). Step 1: reading ψ_bnd at the mean of an inner (~1 cell in) and an outer (~1 cell out) escape test removes the sign for the interior SADDLE binding (diverted → +0.4%, on ψ_N = 1) but leaves a LIMITED wall tangency +2.4% too small — the brackets straddle the wall, whose sub-cell position is not the mean's half-cell. Step 2: read the limited tangency SUB-GRID as the minimum interpolated ψ_N over the wall boundary points adjacent to the axis region, applied only to the LIMITED class (limited vs diverted separated by the region size ratio across the binding — a saddle opening jumps the size ~1.4–1.8, a tangency grows smoothly ~1.2; a min-over-wall on a diverted plasma would read its outboard wall approach, outside the separatrix, and overshoot). Both classes now sit on y = x (limited signed median −0.01%, diverted +0.43%); radii 2.55 → 0.09 cm, ψ_bnd → 0.02%. All fixed-shape jnp → GPU-portable; ψ_bnd differentiable.
