§1 — Assessment: the lead's shielding analogy is the right frame, and the evidence base is already in place

The limited-phase diagnosis (temporal-physics-spine closure record, §3 ledger landed) attributed the 5–10 cm ramp-up boundary gap to "a fast-evolving skin-current profile and a rapidly forming shape that the static spine's representation cannot track" — with passive eddies, the noise floor, the reader, and (mostly) the referee all rejected by measurement, and the classical escape closed (the signed hollow-capable basis converges 0/16; non-negativity is load-bearing). The lead's directive — "implement a dynamic current model for the shape-evolving plasma the same way we did for the passive cases of the coils: the coil cases shield the interior, the outer plasma shields the interior" — is physically exact:

  • The skin effect IS circuit screening. During a fast ramp the external flux swing drives induced current preferentially in the outermost plasma layers (largest mutual coupling to the drive), which transiently shield the core; the current then penetrates inward on the local resistive time. This is the same L·dI/dt + R·I physics that P1 promoted for the vessel — the plasma is just a conductor whose filaments happen to also obey force balance. The static spine's peaked non-negative basis structurally cannot hold this state, which is exactly the measured failure signature (too-round, mis-centred interior; elongation ~5.7 cm + inboard-shift ~4.1 cm harmonics; error peaking in the limited→diverted transition).
  • This attacks the externally VISIBLE half of the problem. The three channels that failed their gates all tried to inform the interior null (the axis), and each paid the same measured −0.16 held-out toll. A screening mode is different in kind: it misplaces O(100 kA) of plasma current RADIALLY, and the boundary tracks the current extent (the staged-disc finding) — the deficit it corrects is the ramp/transition LCFS, which the 73 magnetics and the referee can both see. The win condition of this plan is therefore the ramp LCFS — never the axis, which the gates only guard.
  • "Mutuals known a priori" is true and already in-tree. Plasma-cell ↔ sensor couplings are the existing g_sens Greens; coil/vessel ↔ cell couplings are hybrid_greens thick-cylinder kernels (the locked coupling convention); cell ↔ cell coupling is the grid's own Dirichlet + edge-Green machinery. For the reduced circuit the plasma region is tiled into thick-cylinder patches (the patch-current machinery heritage), giving a ~10²-square mutual matrix — no new kernels, no recalculation.
  • The resistivity closure has a landed template and a new data lever. Filament resistances R_j = η(ψ_N_j)·2πR_j/A_j inherit the bounded 3-parameter EtaProfile family (cross-shot, never per-slice). The closure identification landed at the physical Spitzer scale (0.9–1.9×10⁻⁷ Ω·m) without any temperature input. The NEW lever: the ramp transients themselves — the decay of the screening mode as seen by the magnetics at 1 kHz — constrain the edge-localised η exactly the way the coil-only transients constrained the vessel resistances in P1b (τ_screen ~ μ0·a·δ/η_edge). The edge-contrast DOF of the η family, which the flat-η closure left disengaged, is precisely what a screening layer finally makes identifiable.
  • Honest risks, pre-registered. (i) P1 proved that correct transient physics can convert to little referee skill — but vessel eddies were small far-field currents, while the screening mode is misplaced plasma current of the very quantity the boundary read tracks; the cheap synthetic rung (S1) measures the leverage before any real-data spend. (ii) The H1b refit measured that free signed bases destabilise Picard — so the representation here is the frozen non-negative backbone PLUS one bounded, data-driven screening column, never a free basis swap. (iii) The referee's own ramp slew (~1 cm/step) bounds the measurable gain; gate margins are set accordingly. (iv) A full moving-filament DAE (DINA-class free-boundary evolution) is out of scope — geometry stays frozen-per-interval first (the locked P2 precedent), with sub-interval remap as the S3 upgrade only if S2 earns it.

§2 — Formulation: one system for current dynamics and flux diffusion

The filament circuit and the 1D diffusion operator are the same physics in two representations. Discretise the plasma region into N thick-cylinder filaments (patch tiling of the pass-1 core mask). The circuit system is

M_pp·di_p/dt + M_pc·di_coil/dt + M_pv·di_vessel/dt + R_p(η)·i_p = v_motion

with M exact from the in-tree kernels, R_p diagonal from η(ψ_N) at the filament's ψ_N, the vessel arm carrying the calibrated passive_structure_calibration.json, and v_motion the moving-filament EMF (zero in the frozen-geometry rungs; carried by the pass-1 geometry chain remap at labels, exactly as P2 chained). Flux-surface averaging this system on nested surfaces reduces it to the landed toc·∂ψ/∂t = ∂/∂ρ̂[(g2·g3/ρ̂)·∂ψ/∂ρ̂] operator — which gives S0 a free, decisive identity pin: the filament model in the circular/nested limit must reproduce diffuse_psi (itself TORAX-verified to 0.2%) to discretisation error. The new content beyond P2 is exactly what P2 lacked: the 2D screening state (edge-localised, poloidally structured current the 1D average annihilates and the ladder basis cannot hold) and its coupling to the forming shape.

Screening eigenbasis — the P1 reduction, applied to the plasma. Whiten the filament system as in build_passive_eigenbasis: the screening physics is low-rank (the vessel needed rank 8), so the top-k eigenmodes of the plasma L/R system — with their exact-ZOH integrable time constants — form the dynamic state. The mode shapes are geometry-derived (machine-agnostic); only their resistive time scales carry the η unknown.

Representation in the fit (the H1b-safe form). Pass 2 solves the frozen non-negative ladder PLUS one (or few) screening-mode columns whose amplitude is bounded and prior-centred on the circuit-integrated trajectory — the same soft-prior contract as P1's eddy amplitudes (weight 0 byte-identical, test-pinned). The mode column is FIXED during Picard sweeps (computed from pass-1 geometry), so the convergence anchor that non-negativity provides is untouched; the screening column adds the one direction the basis lacks, with its amplitude told by physics and checked by the sensors.

The Te question (lead): firewalled — and the assessment says it can stay that way. Three reasons, one carve-out:

  • The closure identification already lands η at the physical Spitzer scale with no temperature input, and the screening rung ADDS constraint (the ramp transients) rather than needing more inputs. A Te-parameterised η (Spitzer η ∝ Te^{−3/2} with Te a fitted low-DOF profile) is mathematically just a re-parameterisation of the same closure DOF — it adds interpretability, not information.
  • Thomson Te is corpus-gated on MAST (moment_priors.TE_DATA_GATE) and ~99%-NaN MSE made kinetic inputs unreliable — a Te-dependent fit path would be untestable here and machine-fragile elsewhere. The machine-agnostic North Star wants closures identified from external electromagnetics.
  • n_e (ane, loaded and unused) does not unlock η — Spitzer resistivity depends on Te and Z_eff, with n_e entering only weakly through the Coulomb logarithm. Record, don't chase.
  • Carve-out: Te enters EVAL-ONLY, exactly like the EFIT referee: where Thomson exists, cross-check the closure-identified η(ψ_N) against Spitzer/Sauter η(Te) as a consistency diagnostic (report, never fit). Keep the Sauter interface (already the template in current_diffusion.py) so a machine with routine Te can swap the parameterisation in — a decision for that machine, not this corpus.
