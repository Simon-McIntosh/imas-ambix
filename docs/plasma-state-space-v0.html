Plasma State-Space Engine v0

Stage-1 of physics-world-model-strategy. The home of sprint S7. Goal: prove the probabilistic state-space engine — and that its uncertainty is honest — on a well-posed, general, multi-modal task built on raw MAST signals, before we invest in physical grounding (GS prior) and discovery.

§1 — Objective

Build a learned latent state-space model + Bayesian filter on raw MAST diagnostics (camera + magnetics) and demonstrate that it estimates and forecasts plasma signals with calibrated uncertainty, and flags out-of-distribution regimes.

The acceptance gate is a raw-signal task (see §3): predict a held-out raw diagnostic channel from the others, and forecast a signal through its transients. This is the truest "virtual diagnostic" (our stated ITER utility), its labels are ground-truth measurements (no solver, no inherited bias), and forecasting through transients genuinely exercises the transition kernel — the thing that makes this a world model rather than a regression.

Why not lead with separatrix shape? Shape parameters (κ, δ, volume…) come from efm = EFIT output. A model predicting them is an EFIT emulator: "calibration" would mean calibrated to EFIT, not to truth (where EFIT is biased the model is confidently wrong the same way, invisibly); and shape is quasi-static, so it under-tests the dynamics. That quietly reintroduces the regressor-output dependency the whole project pivoted to avoid (strategy §5). So shape-from-EFIT is retained only as a clearly-labelled emulation sanity check — useful to confirm the diagnostics carry boundary information — not the acceptance gate. (The choice of primary target is an open decision below — the user proposed shape; this plan recommends the raw-signal gate and surfaces the trade rather than swapping unilaterally.)

Generating camera streams is explicitly a byproduct, never a goal.

§2 — Data (raw, available now — no IMAS migration needed)

  • Inputs (raw): rbb fast camera + magnetics groups amb/amc/amm from /work/projects/imas_gpu/mast/level1/shots/<shot>.zarr. Exact signal selection is sprint task S7.1.
  • Primary targets (ground truth): held-out raw diagnostic channels and their short-horizon future — i.e. predict measured signal(s) from other measured signals, and forecast through transients. No solver in the label path.
  • Sanity-check target (EFIT-emulation, caveated): separatrix shape parameters from efm. Always reported as "calibrated to EFIT-reconstructed shape", never as "honest uncertainty about the plasma state." Confirms diagnostics carry boundary info; is not the gate.
  • Splits: held-out shots (calibration) AND held-out regimes (OOD test) — split by Ip/density/shape class/campaign so a whole region of operating space is unseen. Defining the regime split is S7.1.

§3 — Approach (baseline → engine, one variable at a time)

  1. Static baseline. Per-slice regression (other channels → held-out channel) with a deep ensemble for uncertainty. Establishes the observation model, multi-modal fusion, and the calibration harness. No dynamics.
  2. Dynamic engine. A learned latent state + stochastic transition kernel + Bayesian filter (light UQ per strategy §3: ensemble/conformal, not full-variational unless needed). Delivers the three operations: filtering (estimate now), forecasting (predict forward through transients — the what-if), smoothing (best trajectory, for later discovery).

Baseline and engine are evaluated on the same held-out shots/regimes with the same harness, so the engine's value — does modelling dynamics improve calibrated forecasting over per-slice regression? — is isolated. Forecasting through transients is precisely where the static baseline should fail and the dynamic engine should earn its keep.

§4 — Acceptance criteria (what "calibrated and honest" must mean)

  • Calibration: 90% predictive intervals contain truth in 88–92% of held-out-shot slices (reliability diagram near-diagonal across nominal levels).
  • Forecasting: calibrated to a stated horizon H through transients; the dynamic engine's CRPS must beat the static baseline's at H > 0 (this is the test that dynamics matter).
  • Sharpness: mean interval width reported; as tight as possible subject to calibration.
  • Proper scores: CRPS and NLL vs the static baseline.
  • OOD honesty: on held-out regimes, intervals visibly widen / novelty score rises; coverage must not collapse silently (report OOD-detection AUROC or a coverage-vs-distance curve).
  • Sanity: shape-from-EFIT emulation calibrated-to-EFIT (confirms boundary info present) — reported, not gating.

Acceptance is calibration on ground-truth signals, not rFID or pixel fidelity — deliberately, per the strategy.

§5 — Out of scope for v0

GS prior / physical grounding (Stage-2); structure discovery (SINDy/Koopman); cross-machine extrapolation; camera-stream generation; IMAS-format migration. v0 proves the engine + honesty on raw signals; these build on it.

§ Decisions

What is the Stage-1 acceptance target? (THE gating decision for S7 — for the user)

Recommend "both": raw-signal forecasting as the acceptance gate (honest labels + real dynamics), shape-from-EFIT retained as a sanity check that the diagnostics carry boundary info. Surfaced because the user proposed shape, and the trade (escaping vs re-emulating the solver) is theirs to make knowingly — strategy §5.

Which input modalities for the v0 engine?

Recommend magnetics-only baseline first (the classical observable), then add the camera and quantify what it buys.

How do we define held-out REGIMES for the OOD test?

Recommend holding out an I_p×density region as primary (a clear operating-space hole the model must flag); shape-class / campaign as secondary OOD axes. Final choice in S7.1 once the data distribution is scoped.
