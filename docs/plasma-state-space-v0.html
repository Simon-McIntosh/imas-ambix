Plasma State-Space Engine v0

Stage-1 of physics-world-model-strategy. Home of sprint S7. Prove the probabilistic state-space engine — and that its uncertainty is honest — on a meaningful, EFIT-free, cross-family virtual-diagnostic task built on raw MAST signals, before physical grounding (GS prior) and discovery.

✓ LANDED — shipped 2026-05-30 (Stage-1, qualified). Full illustrated detail: stage1-stage2-results. Superseded by the Stage-3 pivot mse-free-current-recovery-v0.

The cross-family RKN engine on raw MAST Dα is calibration-honest (conformal filtering coverage 0.913 ∈ [0.88, 0.92]; transient-NLL win at h≥5; OOD coverage non-collapse 0.94→0.76 with ×3.3 σ-inflation) but a trivial persistence-of-nowcast wins forecast CRPS at every horizon (the Dα target is ~96.8 % quiescent) and the engine-native OOD signal (AUROC 0.748) does not beat the static ensemble (0.810). Dynamics earn their keep on calibrated catastrophic-miss-avoidance + honest widening, not bulk accuracy. The detailed section prose below is the original plan, retained for the record.

§1 — Objective

Build a learned latent state-space model + Bayesian filter on raw MAST diagnostics that predicts (filtering) and forecasts (through transients) a whole held-out diagnostic family from the other families, with calibrated uncertainty, and flags out-of-distribution regimes.

Whole-family hold-out — not within-family. Predicting one magnetic probe from the other ~60 is near-trivial spatial interpolation across a redundant array; it proves nothing about physical understanding. The meaningful test is cross-family: hold an entire diagnostic family out of the inputs and infer it from the others. That tests whether the latent state genuinely carries the shared physical information linking distinct measurement systems — the essence of a virtual diagnostic and of state estimation.

EFIT-free targets. No EFIT/efm labels anywhere — the target is a measured family, so "calibration" means calibrated to ground truth, not to a solver. Leading candidates (final pick in S7.1, by availability + signal richness):

  • Dα (Balmer-α edge/recycling emission) held out, predicted from magnetics + camera. Dα carries strong ELM/transient structure → the best dynamics test.
  • Camera-derived boundary — the visible emission boundary extracted directly from the rbb image (a measurement, not EFIT) — predicted from magnetics. "The boundary directly from measurement." (Note: the emission boundary is a measured quantity related to, but distinct from, the magnetic separatrix — and that's fine.)
  • Magnetics family held out, predicted from the camera — a strong cross-modal inference.

Generating camera streams remains a byproduct, never a goal.

§2 — Data (raw, available now — no IMAS migration)

  • Inputs / targets (all raw, ground truth): level-1 zarr families at /work/projects/imas_gpu/mast/level1/shots/<shot>.zarr — rbb camera, magnetics amb/amc/amm, and the remaining families (esm/xdc/…); plus Dα wherever it lives (S7.1 locates it). One family is designated the held-out target (never an input); the rest are inputs.
  • No EFIT. The efm group (EFIT reconstruction) is excluded from inputs and from labels. The camera-derived boundary, if used, is extracted from the rbb image by our own image processing, not from efm.
  • Splits: held-out shots (calibration) AND held-out regimes (OOD) — split by Iₚ/density/shape class/campaign so a whole region of operating space is unseen. Note the held-out family (cross-family generalisation) and held-out regime (OOD) are orthogonal axes; we test both.

§3 — Approach (baseline → engine, one variable at a time)

  1. Static baseline. Per-slice cross-family regression (other familiest → held-out familyt) with a deep ensemble for uncertainty. Establishes the cross-family observation model, multi-modal fusion, and the calibration harness. No dynamics.
  2. Dynamic engine. A learned latent state + stochastic transition kernel + Bayesian filter (light UQ per strategy §3 — deep-ensemble + conformal, or RKN-style; not full-variational unless needed). Delivers filtering (estimate the held-out family now), forecasting (predict it forward through transients — the what-if), smoothing (best trajectory, for later discovery).

Baseline and engine share the same held-out family / shots / regimes and the same harness, so the engine's value — does modelling dynamics improve calibrated forecasting through transients over per-slice regression? — is isolated. Transients (e.g. ELMs in Dα) are exactly where the static baseline should fail and the dynamic engine should earn its keep.

§4 — Acceptance criteria (what "calibrated and honest" must mean)

  • Cross-family calibration: 90% predictive intervals on the held-out family contain truth in 88–92% of held-out-shot slices (reliability diagram near-diagonal).
  • Forecasting through transients: calibrated to a stated horizon H; the dynamic engine's CRPS must beat the static baseline's at H>0 (the test that dynamics matter), with explicit reporting on transient windows.
  • Sharpness: mean interval width; as tight as possible subject to calibration.
  • Proper scores: CRPS, NLL vs the static baseline.
  • OOD honesty: on held-out regimes, intervals visibly widen / novelty score rises; coverage must not collapse silently (report OOD-detection AUROC or coverage-vs-distance).

Acceptance is calibration on ground-truth measured signals — no EFIT, no rFID, no pixel fidelity.

§5 — Out of scope for v0

GS prior / physical grounding (Stage-2); structure discovery (SINDy/Koopman); cross-machine extrapolation; camera-stream generation; IMAS-format migration; any EFIT-derived label. v0 proves the engine + honesty on a cross-family raw-signal task; these build on it.
