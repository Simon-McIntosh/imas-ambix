Reading note (research). The control-conditioning survey §4 concluded that the camera world model ignores its actuator plan partly for a causal reason: observational flat-top data is confounded (operators set the plan in response to state) and not persistently exciting (flat-top action variance ≈ 0), so the control→camera map is statistically under-identified — "making the plasma driveable is a data/excitation problem as much as a modelling one." This doc characterises the rbb camera corpus to build the missing excitation: a dynamically-weighted, plasma-present, long-horizon, exposure-balanced curated corpus. It runs in PARALLEL with the M4 model re-design and feeds the eventual re-train (not the overfit gate). Acronyms expanded on first use. All numbers from a panel of 331 rbb-bearing shots (of 400 sampled, evenly spread across the 11,012-shot corpus id range; 69 had no readable rbb/Ip record).

§1 — What the corpus actually looks like (the four binding facts)

Four measured properties shape every curation decision. Each is a lever the lead named; the data either confirms the instinct or sharpens the threshold.

  • The camera cadence is wildly heterogeneous (the binding constraint). The rbb frame rate spans 50 Hz → 143 kHz (median ~3 kHz; 18 % ≤ 700 Hz, 29 % at 0.7–2 kHz, 30 % at 2–20 kHz, 22 % > 20 kHz). Only 5 % of shots sit near the nominal 600 Hz. A recording lasts only ~0.1–0.5 s (median 0.36 s). Consequence: a fixed frame count means a different physical horizon on every shot — the long-horizon window must be specified in seconds and converted to frames per-shot (§4).
  • ~1 in 6 recordings is near-vacuum. ``max|Ip|`` over the camera window is bimodal — a vacuum cluster (10th percentile 4.9 kA) and a real-plasma cluster (median 707 kA) with a clean valley between. 16 % of recordings never clear 20 kA (Fig 1b). Consequence: a plasma-presence filter is mandatory, and the bimodality hands us the threshold (§3).
  • Coil excitation is bursty, not flat. The summed coil ramp rate ``|dI/dt|`` is concentrated at breakdown (median breakdown offset 2.5 ms into the recording) and at discrete events, near-zero in between (Fig 2a). Consequence: a selector that FINDS the excited window inside each recording is load-bearing — most of every shot is unexciting (§2).
  • Brightness spans ~2900× across mixed bit-depth sensors. Per-shot median frame brightness ranges from ~0 to 873 raw counts; 305 shots are 8-bit (max ≤ 255), 26 are 10-bit (max ≤ 1023). The v0 global min/max stretch is fragile to exactly this (Fig 4). Consequence: a robust per-shot exposure transform for the curated subset re-encode (§5).

§2 — Coil ramp-rate |dI/dt| is the excitation signal

The survey's persistent-excitation argument says the action→response map is identifiable only where the input varies. The operator's input is the poloidal-field + central-solenoid coil currents (the ``amc`` source — already in the conditioning vector). Their ramp rate ``|dI/dt|`` is the variation that matters; the plasma current ``Ip`` is the response, not a command, so it is kept out of the drive columns and used only for plasma-presence. (PF/CS coil currents and Ip are both already available via the ``amc`` source — no new data source is needed.)

Figure 2a is the decisive picture: the summed coil ``|dI/dt|`` spikes at breakdown and at a few discrete events, and is otherwise flat. A window scored on the level standard deviation of the actuator vector (the existing controllability-gate selector, find_transient_window) would not distinguish a high-but-flat coil current from a ramping one. The curation selector therefore scores the time-mean of the summed per-coil ``|dI/dt|`` over a sliding window — a level-invariant measure, so a flat current scores ~0 and a ramp scores high — and additionally de-prioritises disruption tails (a window dominated by a sharp ``|Ip|`` collapse is down-weighted by ½; disruption tails are abundant but, per the lead, "perhaps not that useful" for learning the driven dynamics). Per-shot available excitation (the peak windowed score) has a median of ~86 MA/s with a long tail to ~2.8 GA/s (Fig 2b).

§3 — Plasma presence: the 20 kA cut is in the bimodal valley

A "play the plasma" model must train on plasma, not vacuum. The criterion (justified directly from Fig 1b): a window is plasma-present when its ``max|Ip|`` clears 20 kA AND at least half its frames individually carry ``|Ip| ≥ 20 kA``. The threshold is not arbitrary — the ``max|Ip|`` distribution is bimodal with a clean valley between the vacuum cluster (≈5–15 kA) and the real-plasma cluster (≈400–1000 kA); 20 kA sits in that valley, rejecting the 16 % near-vacuum recordings without clipping genuine plasma.

Crucially, a window is allowed to start at ``Ip ≈ 0`` — breakdown/initiation is exactly the dynamic phase we want, and the median recording starts 2.5 ms after breakdown (Fig 1c). The half-fraction gate admits a window whose first frames are sub-threshold (the plasma is forming) while rejecting one that only briefly flickers into plasma. The fraction gate is deliberately permissive (0.5, keeping 79 % of shots) because the hard ``max|Ip|`` gate already removes vacuum; a stricter 0.8 would keep only 42 % and would discard genuine breakdown windows.

§4 — Long-horizon windows: a physical horizon, converted per-shot

The current spatiotemporal window is 24 frames at stride 1 — at 600 Hz that is ~40 ms, far shorter than a MAST ramp-up (Ip 10–90 % rise: median 125 ms, 90th percentile 340 ms). To predict over "much longer times than the current window" (lead requirement iii) the window must span at least a full ramp-up. The complication is the ~250× cadence spread (Fig 3a): a fixed frame count cannot span a fixed physical time.

The recommendation is therefore a target physical horizon, converted to a frame span per shot via that shot's measured fps: the native (stride-1) span is round(horizon · fps) + 1; when that exceeds a temporal-budget cap on ``n_frames`` (default 48, twice the current 24), a frame_stride sub-samples so the modelled span still reaches the horizon while ``n_frames`` stays within the attention budget. We recommend a 0.25 s horizon: it contains the full ramp-up for 74 % of shots (Fig 3b) plus a lead-in and a little early flat-top, and fits within the median 0.36 s recording. Across the entire 50 Hz–143 kHz range the recommendation delivers a near-constant ~0.25 s covered horizon (Fig 3c) — exactly what a fixed frame count cannot do.

§5 — Exposure balance: robust per-shot percentile clip, on a SUBSET re-encode

Brightness lives in PIXELS, before tokenisation. The corpus tokeniser (_normalise_frames_to_uint8, mirrored in the stream encoder) currently does a per-shot global min/max stretch — and its own docstring flags it as a v0 ("more careful normalisation can come later"). The characterisation shows why "later" is now: per-shot brightness spans ~2900× across the corpus (Fig 4a) and the sensors are mixed 8/10-bit (Fig 4b). Global min/max is fragile to exactly this — a single saturated reflection or a dark-dropout frame pins ``min``/``max`` and compresses the whole shot's usable range, so the same plasma feature lands at a different token after stretch on different shots. That inconsistency is brightness leaking into the tokens — the opposite of bringing shots to a common relative brightness.

Figure 4c is the demonstration: a synthetic plasma blob with one saturated reflection pixel. Under the v0 global stretch the blob is nearly invisible (crushed into the bottom of the range by the one hot pixel); under a per-shot percentile clip (discard the brightest 0.5 % and darkest 1 %, then stretch) the blob fills the range. Recommended strategy: per-shot percentile-clip normalisation as the corpus default (cheap, deterministic, pure-numpy, no per-frame flicker); per-frame CLAHE (Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalisation) only as a targeted option for the minority of low-contrast shots, profiled automatically. Where it applies: a re-encode of the curated dynamic segments only — NOT the full 4 B-token corpus (§7). CLAHE needs OpenCV, which the ambix env does not ship; the pipeline degrades a CLAHE recommendation to the percentile transform when OpenCV is absent, so the default path is always available.

§6 — The curation pipeline (shipped + unit-tested)

All additions are backward-compatible — the existing find_transient_window and assemble_controllable_window APIs (used by the parallel M4 gate) are untouched; the new behaviour is new functions / new modules / optional kwargs.

Long-horizon window recommendation window_horizon (new) Converts a target physical horizon (s) + per-shot fps into ``n_frames`` / ``frame_stride``, capped at a temporal budget; builds a ready SpacetimeWindowConfig.
Exposure-balancing transform exposure_balance (new) Per-shot percentile-clip stretch (robust drop-in for the v0 global min/max) + optional per-frame CLAHE; profile_exposure recommends which; balance_exposure("auto") degrades to percentile when OpenCV is absent.

Unit tests (tests/worldmodel/test_excitation_curation.py, 23 cases on synthetic in-memory stores) cover: coil columns exclude Ip and are disjoint from gas/NBI; the selector picks the coil ramp, rejects vacuum, rejects flat drive, and admits a breakdown-start window; the presence filter accepts a present window, rejects vacuum, admits a breakdown start, and rejects a brief flicker; the window recommendation holds the physical horizon constant across a 250× fps range and falls back to the reference fps when fps is non-finite; the percentile transform is robust to a saturated outlier, preserves RGB layout, and matches the v0 all-zeros contract on a flat frame; and an assertion that ``find_transient_window``'s signature is unchanged (the parallel gate's contract).

§7 — Proof-of-concept corpus + the gated full re-encode

A small proof-of-concept curated corpus (a handful of dynamic, plasma-present shots; long windows; exposure-balanced) is built end-to-end to validate the pipeline. The full re-encode is GATED behind a lead check-in — it is the only multi-hour GPU step and must not run unannounced.

Compute estimate for the full curated re-encode. The production encoder (stream_encode.py) measured ~308 fps/GPU peak and encoded 4.02 B tokens in ~7 h on a single GPU. The curated corpus is a SUBSET — only the dynamic, plasma-present segments of the dynamic shots, each one long window — so it is far smaller than the full corpus. With a 0.25 s window and the per-shot stride, a curated window is at most ``n_frames`` ≤ 48 frames = 48 × 256 = ~12.3 k tokens; even at, say, 3,000 curated shots that is ~37 M tokens — under 1 % of the full corpus, well under 1 GPU-hour of encode at the measured throughput (the dominant cost is per-frame model forward, and a curated subset has orders of magnitude fewer frames than the full ~600 Hz–143 kHz recordings). The re-encode therefore fits comfortably on a single betelgeuse GPU coexisting with the DeepSeek serve, in-process and SIGTERM-clean per AGENTS.md §2b. Recommendation: run the PoC (handful of shots) now to validate; hold the full curated re-encode for the lead's go, then run it as a single ≤1 GPU-hour in-process job.

§8 — What this changes for the re-train

The curated corpus addresses the survey's deepest cause (causal under-identification) on the data side, complementing the M4 model re-design (history-bottleneck + AdaLN + inverse-dynamics) on the architecture side. Concretely the re-train should: enumerate excited windows (selector score above a floor) from plasma-present dynamic shots, at a per-shot long-horizon window (~0.25 s), on exposure-balanced tokens — with whole held-out shots (18502–18505 and the discovery-index reserve) kept out of the curated training set, the shot-level leakage guard unchanged. The honest expectation from the survey stands: if the logged data is genuinely unexciting even after this curation, the binding next step is a handful of perturbative / interventional shots or simulator-swept inputs — but this corpus extracts the maximum excitation the existing logs contain, which is the right first move.
