§1 — EFIT's four internal-constraint families

Equilibrium reconstruction codes (EFIT and the kinetic-EFIT / IDE lineage) break the magnetics-only under-determination of the internal current profile by importing extra interior constraints. There are four established families, each constraining a different facet of the p′ / FF′ decomposition of j(ψ):

  • Kinetic pressure — a measured pressure profile p(ψ) (from Thomson ne/Te + ion temperature/density) pins the p′ term of the Grad–Shafranov source directly. The most broadly available interior constraint on present machines.
  • MSE — motional-Stark-effect pitch angles measure the local magnetic-field line pitch, constraining the internal q/j directly. The reference interior constraint — and exactly the one absent at ITER startup, which is the founding premise of this whole project (it is our eval truth, never an input).
  • Faraday rotation / polarimetry — line-integrated rotation of the polarisation plane along chords measures ∫ ne B∥ dl, constraining the poloidal field profile interior. The classic ITER-class internal-current diagnostic.
  • Interferometry — line-integrated density constrains ne, which feeds the pressure and (with polarimetry) the Faraday inversion; on its own it is a density, not a direct current, constraint.

Regime ranking for MAST-class, MSE-free recovery. Ordered by how much genuinely-new interior current information they could add given the diagnostics that actually exist on MAST: kinetic pressure ≫ Faraday/polarimetry > interferometry. The next two sections show that for MAST this ranking collapses to a single survivor — pressure — because Faraday/polarimetry and the diamagnetic loop are not usable interior constraints here.

§2 — MAST availability: Faraday and diamagnetic are out

A verified full-corpus inventory retires two of the four families for MAST:

  • Faraday rotation / polarimetry — ABSENT. There is no polarimetry group in the 50-group level-1 vocabulary. This is not a sparsity wall (the MSE presence-vs-content story); the diagnostic simply does not exist in the corpus. Polarimetry is an ITER-class, future-machine internal-current diagnostic — out of scope for v0. Documenting this retires the "chain Faraday rotation" premise cleanly: it is the right diagnostic for the right machine, just not this one.
  • Diamagnetic — only as a banned solver output. The diamagnetic βp signal appears in the corpus only as an efm (EFIT) output, which is a banned solver product under the project's locked efit-eval-use rule (eval-only boundary/global sanity, never an interior reference). There is no raw diamagnetic-loop measurement to assimilate. Out of scope.

That leaves kinetic pressure as the only real new interior lever available on MAST — and, helpfully, the data is present; only the mapping is missing.

§3 — The lever: kinetic pressure from Thomson (atm)

The pressure path is a mapping gap, not a data gap. The ingredients are present:

  • Source — Thomson scattering on the atm core system supplies ne(R,t) and Te(R,t) with usable near-axis coverage. (This is the same Thomson channel whose Te drives the §4 σ(Te) closure in the parent plan; here we use the full p = ne·Te profile, not just Te.)
  • Bridge — map the Thomson profiles into core_profiles pressure_thermal (a thermal pressure profile vs normalised flux), then to p(ψ) on the equilibrium flux grid. This p(ψ) mapping is the one piece that does not yet exist — the data is all there.

The decisive cohort (corrected). Held-out-112 ∩ usable-near-axis Thomson = 45 shots, all via the atm core Thomson system. This corrects an earlier small-sample estimate (13/25) and is large enough to be a decisive pressure-experiment cohort: a 45-shot held-out arm gives real statistical power to the with-vs-without-pressure comparison.

§4 — The H_pressure operator contract (for the baseline plan)

The diagnostics plan delivers the observation operator that the strong sequential baseline, Strong Sequential Full-State Current-Profile Data Assimilation, assimilates in its §7 experiment. The contract:

  • Input — the EKF filtered state x = ψ(ρ).
  • Output — a predicted thermal-pressure profile p(ρ), obtained through Grad–Shafranov force-balance: the p′ term couples into the FF′ / current-density decomposition consistent with the equilibrium the state defines.
  • Noise — a per-channel measurement-noise covariance for the Thomson-derived pressure observation (the Thomson channels have heterogeneous uncertainty; a per-channel R is required, not a scalar).

Crucially this is the physics path: state → predicted p(ρ) via GS force-balance, assimilated as a measurement. It is not the statistical Thomson-features → pitch regression path that the S9 oracle already tested and found negative (multimodal channels, including the Thomson-bearing arms, did not break the ~0.41 near-axis ceiling). The hypothesis is that p enters as a physical constraint on the equilibrium, not as a statistical feature.

§5 — The decisive experiment

Inside the EKF substrate from the baseline plan, run the 45-shot cohort two ways:

  1. Baseline arm — magnetics-only assimilation (the strong v1 frontier, ~0.19 sightline / ~0.41 near-axis).
  2. Pressure arm — magnetics + GS-p′ pressure assimilation via Hpressure.

The primary metric is near-axis interior RMSE vs held-out MSE — the term the ~0.41 ceiling lives on. The question is single and clean: does adding a physical pressure constraint move the near-axis interior below the magnetics-only floor? The sightline metric and cov90 are reported as guardrails (pressure must not degrade them).

§6 — Bonus: CXRS → Ti + rotation (with Zeff assessed negative in level-1)

Charge-exchange recombination spectroscopy lives on the act system (7991 shots in corpus; 106 / 112 held-out shots; all 45 pressure-cohort shots) and in the current level-1 exposure yields three directly-usable quantities:

  • Ti — ion temperature, which (with ne) completes the total pressure beyond the electron-only Thomson term.
  • Toroidal rotation — available alongside Ti, so any later kinetic extension can carry a momentum / rotation prior from the same diagnostic.
  • CX counts — the raw quality / beam-gating signal for the CXRS frame.

Zeff assessment. The committed probe artifact imas_ambix/statespace/artifacts/internal_diagnostics_probe_v0.json shows that the current level-1 act exposure does not surface any Zeff-like field: the available arrays are temperature, velocity, and CX counts, but there are no Zeff candidates to chain today. So CXRS can tighten the ion-temperature / rotation side of the forward model, but it does not presently remove the baseline's free-Zeff degree of freedom.

This remains a secondary, lower-priority constraint: it tightens the forward model rather than adding a direct interior measurement. The concrete assessment is now done — Ti and rotation are available on act, Zeff is not — so any follow-on should target the ion-temperature / rotation path only unless another Zeff source is surfaced.

§7 — Honest framing

Pressure is the one thing that MIGHT break the ~0.41 near-axis interior ceiling — this is the clean test, not a claim that pressure recovers the interior. The under-determination wall has been confirmed three independent times: Stage-2 grounding (near-vacuum current ratio 0.98), the S9 oracle (~0.41 near-axis for magnetics and all multimodal), and the GS-rank analysis (~5–6 informative singular vectors). A physical pressure constraint fights that same wall. We run the experiment because pressure is the one constraint with a credible physical mechanism to add interior information that magnetics cannot — but the honest prior is that it will be hard, and a clean negative (pressure does not move the near-axis floor) is a publishable, decision-relevant result, not a failure of the plan.

Per-channel measurement-noise covariance for the pressure observation (the Thomson channels have heterogeneous uncertainty; a scalar R would mis-weight them).

How is the pressure experiment framed — as a recovery claim or a clean test?

LOCKED (S10, 2026-06-02): framed as "pressure is the one thing that MIGHT break the ~0.41 near-axis interior ceiling — here is the clean test," NOT "pressure recovers the interior." It fights the same under-determination wall confirmed 3× (Stage-2, oracle, GS-rank ~5-6). A clean negative is publishable and decision-relevant.
